0Explain it simply / 白話版

白話版 — the headline in plain English

Last week I told you the whole AI-infrastructure market was being sold no matter how good the news was. Part of that was my own measurement mistake — I compared prices taken before the market opened on two different days, which mixes up two days' worth of movement. Measured properly, the group was almost flat this week and actually did slightly better than the semiconductor index. I've fixed the method and re-run everything.

What really happened is more interesting: the market stopped treating these companies as one blob and started sorting them. Four companies reported in two days. Two of them said "look at all the orders we've won" — and their shares were punished. Two of them said "look at the money we've already collected" — and their shares jumped 7–8% more than the market. The market has stopped paying for promises and started paying only for cash that has already arrived.

That matters to me because my own idea last week was a promise. I picked the chip-bonding equipment layer precisely because its order books were exploding. Four days later the market showed me exactly what it thinks of exploding order books. So I'm keeping the idea but changing the reason: SUSS MicroTec reports on Aug 6, and that report is the first chance to see whether its record orders are turning into actual sales. If they aren't, the whole "buy the order book" family of ideas is wrong for at least six months and I'll say so.

One more thing worth knowing. Amazon told the world it will now spend $220 billion this year instead of $200 billion, and said the extra $20 billion is because memory chips got more expensive — and its shares went up 7–10% for saying it. In the same week, Micron, which sells those memory chips, fell 7pp more than the market because Apple said it might shop around. One of those two markets is wrong. Working out which is the most valuable open question on this map — but there is no small, cheap, under-followed way for a private investor to express it, so it stays research, not a position.

7-term glossary
Conversion (the week's key idea)
The gap between winning an order and recognising the revenue. Equipment makers and integrators book orders quarters before they can ship. "Converted" revenue is cash already recognised; a backlog is not.
Factor-adjusted / excess
A stock's move minus its benchmark's move over the same window. −17% on a day the sector fell 5% is a −12pp rejection; −6% on a day the sector fell 5% is mostly just the tape.
Implied move
What the options market priced as the expected size of an earnings reaction. A move at 1.6× the implied move is a genuine surprise; a move inside it is not.
IV percentile
Where today's option-implied volatility sits in its own past year, 0–1. Near 1.00 = the market is paying up for protection or a bet; near 0.40 = calm.
Priced / EARLY-ineligible
A name with >15 analysts and deep options can't be an early idea — too many people already do the work. It's a yardstick, never the edge.
$-content disaggregation
Asking how many dollars of a given component go into each rack, rather than how many racks ship. Content can inflect much faster than unit volume — that is what MPWR's 85%→130% revision measures.
Event binary
A position whose outcome is decided by one scheduled announcement. Not an information edge — a coin flip with a date on it.
