01

Executive snapshot

為什麼是現在

Hyperscaler + neocloud capex is running at ~$635–690B in 2026 (>2× 2024) and the neocloud slice is compounding ~50–58%/yr. But the binding constraint has moved: GPUs are being allocated faster (CoWoS is loosening) and their rental price deflates 60–80% per generation. The scarce links are now secured electrical power + a powered shell (multi-year grid queues) and a financeable, creditworthy take-or-pay contract that a lender will lend against. The neocloud operator sits in the middle, on a depreciating asset funded with debt — increasingly a commoditizing middleman. On 1–2 Jul 2026 the group fell hard (NBIS −17%, CRWV −14% intraday) on the "Meta Compute" report — a customer turning competitor — which is exactly this risk pricing in real time.

EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

A neocloud is a company that buys thousands of NVIDIA GPUs, puts them in a building with power and cooling, and rents the computing to AI labs. It looks like the purest way to own the AI boom — but the money is made or lost at the two ends, not the middle. Upstream you need electricity and a powered building, and there's a multi-year waiting line to connect big new loads to the grid. Downstream you need a creditworthy customer signing a long "take-or-pay" contract, because the GPUs are bought with debt secured against the machines — and machines that lose ~70% of their rental value every couple of years. The neocloud in the middle can end up as a rent-a-box that keeps the least margin. This report asks: which companies actually own the scarce link (power + a bankable contract), which are just levered middlemen, and — after the July sell-off — where is the honest early entry?

03

The building blocks, illustrated

關鍵元件

A neocloud is assembled from six things. Two of them are genuinely scarce (secured power; a bankable contract), one deflates fast (the GPU fleet), and one is the hidden fragility (GPU-collateralized debt).

Secured power + shell CHOKEPOINT

Energized megawatts + a powered building. The single hardest input to conjure: grid interconnection queues run 3–7 years and transformers/turbines are sold out. You cannot buy your way past it quickly.

Owners: IREN, APLD, TeraWulf, Crusoe; IPPs / utilities

GPU fleet DEFLATING

The visible asset — but a wasting one. Each generation's rental rate falls 60–80% within 18–24 months as the next chip lands. Depreciated over 4–6 years on the books; bears argue real economic life is 1–3 years.

Supplier + financier: NVIDIA (equity in most neoclouds)

Bankable offtake 2ND CHOKEPOINT

A long take-or-pay contract from a creditworthy tenant. This is what makes the GPU debt bankable — the loan is really underwritten on the customer's credit, not the neocloud's.

Anchors: Microsoft, OpenAI, Meta; NVIDIA backstops

GPU-backed debt FRAGILITY

The financing engine: non-recourse loans (DDTL/ABS) secured on the GPUs. CoreWeave's $8.5B DDTL 4.0 is the first investment-grade (A3) GPU-backed facility — but the collateral is a fast-depreciating asset.

Structurers: Blackstone, Magnetar, Blue Owl

Software / ops SOFT MOAT

The managed layer (provisioning, reliability, networking) that separates a "Platinum" cloud from a bare GPU landlord. Real but thin — SemiAnalysis's ClusterMAX rates it; it commands a premium but is replicable.

CoreWeave (Platinum) · Nebius (Gold)

Capital / cost of funds DIVIDER

Whoever raises the cheapest capital wins the buildout race. This is where the field splits: net-cash balance sheets (Nebius) vs. ~$32B net-debt on depreciating collateral (CoreWeave).

Equity backers: NVIDIA, sovereigns, private credit

04

How a neocloud is built up — and where margin leaks

如何組裝一個 neocloud

Capital + power + GPUs + software combine into a rentable AI cluster, sold via a multi-year contract. At each step value is added — but the scarce rent accrues at the two ends (power, contract/financier), while the operator in the middle keeps the thinnest slice.

Assembly view — the operator is a systems-integrator of scarce inputs. Its economics are strongest when it also owns an end (power, like IREN; or a fortress balance sheet, like NBIS) and weakest when it only rents the middle on borrowed money (the CRWV risk).

08

The chokepoint — a two-gate composite

卡點

Secured power + shell (primary) → financeable creditworthy offtake (secondary)

The neocloud model has two links in series that cannot be conjured on demand. Upstream: energized megawatts and a powered building — gated by 3–7-year grid-interconnection queues, 2.5–5-year transformer lead times, and gas turbines sold out to 2028–29. Downstream: a long take-or-pay contract from a creditworthy anchor, because the GPUs are bought with debt secured against a fast-depreciating asset, and that debt is really underwritten on the customer's credit. The GPU itself is not the chokepoint — allocation is loosening and rental prices deflate 60–80% per generation. The operator in the middle is the visible name but captures the thinnest, most contested rent.

VARIANT / DISAGGREGATION · 變異認知

Consensus buys "the neocloud" as the pure AI-demand play. The disaggregation: the durable scarcity rent sits one link upstream (secured power / IPPs / turbines / the grid) and one link downstream (the financier / balance-sheet capacity) — not at the operator, who is a levered middleman on a depreciating asset. Two second-order effects sharpen this: (1) the buildout is partly circular-financed — NVIDIA takes equity in its own customers and backstops their unsold capacity, OpenAI's ~$1.4T of commitments dwarf its ~$20B ARR — so headline "demand" is partly manufactured and the whole edifice rests on GPU residual values holding; (2) on 1 Jul 2026 Meta Compute showed customers can backward-integrate (they own GPUs + power + balance sheet without the GPU-collateralized debt) — the operator's terminal risk. Net: bullish the aggregate AI buildout, bearish the specific listed operator's rent capture. The honest neocloud trade is to own the landlord (power) or the fortress balance sheet, not the tenant.

How to be early / stay honest 如何提前布局

1 · Own the landlord, not the tenant

The scarce link is secured power, not the GPU or the operator. In neoclouds, the picks-and-shovels sit one gate upstream — power hosts, IPPs, the grid.

2 · Watch the balance sheet, not the backlog

A huge backlog against ~7× net debt on depreciating GPUs is fragile. Cost of capital and residual value decide who survives — favor net-cash (NBIS) over levered (CRWV).

3 · Respect the financialization test

>15 analysts = the edge is largely gone. NBIS/CRWV are recognition-priced. Buy weakness in quality for compounding, don't mistake a drawdown for a secret.

4 · Circular demand is real but borrowed

NVIDIA funding its own customers inflates apparent demand. It works while GPU residuals + AI-lab funding hold — treat a credit-market freeze or a customer (Meta) integrating back as the falsifier.
