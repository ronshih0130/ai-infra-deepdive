Honest read for a retail investor: the whole AI-infra stack has stopped being gated by chips and is now gated by POWER — and you can tell because four separate parts of the chain (the clouds, the cooling guys, the server builders, and even the balance sheets) all independently say their bottleneck now sits up in the grid. Backlogs worth hundreds of billions are being signed against megawatts that physically don't exist yet, and starting this summer (Q3-2026) the hyperscalers' combined free cash flow crosses zero, meaning the rest of the buildout gets funded with debt — so 'who owns scarce, energizable power and the gear to deliver it' is the real game, not 'who makes the fastest GPU.' Be honest about what's already over: GPUs, high-end MLCCs, memory and the named optics lasers are still physically sold-out but the STOCKS have fully priced it (MLCC pure-plays up ~450%, accelerator names at priced=9) — owning the shortage there pays you nothing now. The genuinely not-yet-priced ideas are the laggard leaf WITHIN the binding node: medium-voltage switchgear (POWL), which is the named item delaying half of next year's US data centers but sits behind the crowded Eaton/GE Vernova trade; on-rack 800V power silicon where onsemi (ON) has the best structural position yet has de-rated while flashier GaN names ran; and the substrate leaf (Ibiden) where the packaging chokepoint just migrated up while everyone watches CoWoS ease. The power-secured cloud owners (CRWV) ride the same theme but carry real credit fragility, so size them as the higher-risk leg. Net: the easy, recognized part of this trade is priced; the remaining edge is in the boring electrical balance-of-plant nobody is excited about yet. Personal research, not advice.

Highest-alpha contradictions

Per the framework, the best signals come from contradictions & bottleneck rotation (capex rising but cloud prices falling; backlog rising but margins falling).

  • PASSIVE: physical tightness=9 (Goldman 'the next memory', Murata orders >2x capacity, Vera Rubin 15-20x MLCC content) but priced=9 — tradable alpha has fully decoupled from the shortage; Taiyo Yuden +447% YTD means the scarcity is real but the trade is over. Owning the constraint here pays nothing.
  • ACCEL: most extreme decouple — tight=8 yet priced=9. CoWoS sold out into mid-2027 and HBM4 tight, but the constraint OWNERS (NVDA/AMD/AVGO) discount it fully; the only live alpha fled to the adjacency (ALAB connectivity, QCOM ASIC-entrant optionality), NOT the bottleneck itself.
  • MEM / NETOPT: supply still physically sold-out (tight=9/8) but the marginal PRICING risk has flipped to the DEMAND side — SK Hynix -12% on June 23 on Rubin-trim/HBM4-slowdown, COHR/LITE re-rated on NVDA capture. The easing trigger comes from the customer, not new capacity, so 'still tight' no longer means 'still long.'
  • MARGIN: tight=4 (no physical shortage) yet it may be the most important node — it is a RISK node, not a supply node. The credit market is repricing leverage faster than the still-melting-up equity market (Oracle -$23.7B FCF, $156B debt), so the binding constraint is quietly migrating from atoms to balance sheets while equities ignore it.
  • GRID internal: GEV is the famous name (turbines sold out to 2029) but is the PRICED leg; the un-priced asymmetry sits one layer downstream in switchgear (POWL) — fame and alpha have decoupled within the same binding node.

Two data layers. The estimates scaffold validates consensus_estimates.csv, FMP prior earnings events, FMP earnings-calendar timing rows, and FMP analyst-awareness rows, but current-snapshot / timing / recognition-stage rows do not unlock the PIT action gate. Nodes with no free exact feed yet (HBM contract price, CoWoS/ABF capacity, CPU attach/availability) carry research-sourced leading metrics instead, flagged as such. (2) Research (web, sourced, per subsector + SemiAnalysis article/ChipBook-derived metadata): an independent agent verifies tickers, assigns the shortage stage, and pulls recent sourced evidence. SemiAnalysis rows use article titles/URLs and derived classifications; if subscription body text is used, it is transient and not saved. This layer is refreshed weekly and adversarially self-reviewed by Codex (gpt-5.5, highest effort) role-playing a retail investor.

Caveats — do not over-trust. (1) The "priced-in" axis is a price-position proxy, not a valuation model; with ^SOX +101%/6mo the whole sector is extended, so COMPUTED-INFLECTION names are relatively un-run, not cheap. (2) US quarterly fundamentals lag a monthly channel-check; the live edge is the distributor lead-time + the research layer, not the 10-Q. (3) Curated stages carry a confidence flag — low-confidence nodes are hypotheses. (4) Taiwan-listed names (Tai-Tech, GUC, Alchip, Quanta, Yageo, Unimicron) are the true pure-plays in several nodes but are not in your IBKR access — they are shown for completeness and routed to a tradable proxy; the proxy is an imperfect substitute. (5) This is a discovery/triage map, NOT a buy list — every candidate needs an identity + AI-revenue-purity + valuation check before sizing. (6) IBKR is READ-ONLY; nothing here is investment advice.
