AI Optical Interconnect: the chokepoint has moved to the laser

As AI clusters scale toward millions of GPUs, copper runs out of reach at the rack boundary and scale-out connectivity turns to light (intra-node GPU links remain largely copper). On our read of the data, the binding near-term constraint sits at the indium-phosphide 200G EML laser — really a DFB laser paired with an electro-absorption modulator — estimated ~40–60% undersupplied through 2027.[1] This note maps where the scarcity rent could sit, scores how tight the link is within the optical light-source sub-layer, and stages each play on the awareness curve.

Executive summary

The call: the thesis is right and mostly recognised — pay for it carefully

"AI needs light; the laser is scarce" appears well-supported, but it is also largely consensus: the obvious laser and substrate names have already re-rated hard. Of the three ways to play it, only the vendor-neutral packaging name still looks early on the awareness curve. The whole edge is in where you stand on that curve, not in the thesis itself.

Variant perception vs. consensus
The Street treats co-packaged optics as uniformly laser-bullish ("CPO = more lasers = buy LITE"). We think it bifurcates: CPO is bearish the 200G EML specifically (it shifts to simpler external CW lasers) but bullish vendor-neutral packaging/test (FN), which gets harder and more gating. So the durable, still-underpriced rent is one layer over from the re-rated pure-play. If we are wrong, it is most likely because pluggables — and LPO, which keeps the EML — stay dominant longer than expected and laser pricing power proves more durable, in which case LITE outperforms FN.

Why this matters in one screen
  • The pinch: 200G InP EML lasers est. ~40–60% short through 2027; most capacity reported locked under LTAs to CY2027.[1,2]
  • The demand: 800G+ transceivers forecast 24M → ~63M units (2025→2026e), ~2.6×.[2]
  • The tell: Nvidia reported to have committed ~$4B toward laser supply — the buyer is rationing.[2]
  • The edge: value often peaks one layer below the headline. The laser names already ran; the asymmetry is in packaging.

The central counter-argument

CPO is bullish InP light in aggregate — but a real bear to the specific 200G EML

Earlier drafts treated co-packaged optics as unambiguously raising laser criticality. That conflates two different things. CPO removes the pluggable transceiver (where the 200G EML lives) and feeds the silicon-photonics engine from architecturally simpler — though still demanding — external CW/DFB light sources. So:

  • Aggregate InP light demand: still rises — supports the substrate layer.
  • 200G EML specifically: CPO is a medium-term headwind, not a tailwind — fewer pluggables, simpler lasers.
  • Packaging: CPO makes FAU + wafer-level test harder and more gating — strengthens the FN leg.
Why this shifts the ranking, not the thesis. The EML pinch is a 2026–27 scale-out story (1.6T pluggables); CPO scales 2028+ and the two coexist for years — a mix-shift, not a cliff. Two honest offsets: the CW DFB lasers CPO needs are high-power, low-RIN and still InP, and LITE supplies them to Nvidia, so LITE is not a pure CPO loser; and LPO (linear pluggable optics) is the mirror trade — it keeps the EML and attacks the DSP, making EML demand more durable than a CPO-only view implies. Net: FN's packaging exposure compounds through the transition while LITE's EML-mix tailwind matures — the clearest reason the still-early neutral name screens better than the priced pure-play.
How the components interact

200G EML (pluggable) vs. external CW laser (CPO)

The same job — put modulated light on a fiber — done two ways. CPO relocates the modulator onto the silicon-photonics chip and demotes the laser to a simpler external CW source. That single change is why CPO is bearish the specific 200G EML yet bullish packaging and SiPh.

Multi-year development timeline

2025 → 2028: when the pinch tightens, tests, and transitions

The hinge is 2027. LTAs roll off and the market learns whether laser pricing power is structural or merely contracted — at the same moment CPO begins shifting module mix away from the 200G EML. A capacity overshoot into 2028 is the symmetric bear to today's shortage.

Risk register

What breaks the thesis

  • Buyers fund away the chokepoint: the ~$4B Nvidia commitment is partly to create new EML supply — the moat may be eroded by its own biggest customer.
  • CPO / external-CW substitution: CPO shifts scale-up mix off the 200G EML to simpler lasers — a direct, named bear to LITE's core product.
  • 2027 LTA roll-off: pricing power proves contractual, not structural.
  • InP capacity overshoot → 2028 glut: the symmetric bear to today's shortage.
  • AI-capex cyclicality: a hyperscaler digestion phase hits the whole chain at once.
  • Valuations already re-rated: little room for error at ~90× (LITE) / ~36× sales (AXTI).
  • FN customer concentration: a few large customers drive much of revenue — FN's biggest single risk.
  • Transceiver ASP/mix deflation as 1.6T scales can offset unit growth.
  • Mislocated chokepoint: the PAM4 DSP/SerDes duopoly, EAM/modulator packaging, or CoWoS could be the tighter constraint — the laser may not be where the rent ultimately concentrates.
  • China / indium raw-material supply: China dominates indium and InP export approvals — a chain-wide upstream risk, not just an AXTI catalyst.
  • AXTI specifics: thin margin, dilution from the raise, non-dominant share.

Where this note is most likely to be wrong

Unresolved holes (not yet answered)
  • Rent capture is unproven — the LITE-vs-FN swing: we cannot yet show whether scarcity pricing accrues to LITE or to Nvidia via the LTA, which is exactly why we'd own neutral FN over LITE until the 2027 roll-off prints. Margins settle it; today it's an assumption.
  • No hard share data: "primary supplier" and the supply-chain shares are author estimates, not disclosed figures — the 5/5 concentration score rests on them.
  • Sizing is illustrative: only the ~$5B→$10B+ optics TAM is sourced; per-name SAM/revenue bridges are directional logic, not modelled numbers.
  • CPO timing is the swing: if CPO/CW substitution arrives faster, the entire EML-chokepoint premise weakens earlier than 2027.
Retail-investor lens (steelman the bear)
  • FN is not cheap at ~39×; "neutral arms-dealer" can still lag in a laser-led melt-up.
  • Single data vintage: all prices/multiples are a 2026-06-13 snapshot; a fast tape invalidates the curve placement within weeks.
  • Tightness ≠ return: a 19/20 link can be a poor entry if fully discounted (see AXTI).
