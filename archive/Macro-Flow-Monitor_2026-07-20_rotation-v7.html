EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY / 白話版

Think of markets as a building with three floors. Top floor (Layer 1): is new money walking in the door, or leaving? Right now the dial reads neutral — central-bank liquidity is shrinking a touch, but bank lending is growing and nothing is stressed (credit spreads near lows, no funding alarms). Money is not leaving. Middle floor (Layer 2): where inside the stock market is it moving? Out of the AI/semiconductor winners (semis −16% off their June peak) and into financials, energy, staples — a rotation, not a crash. Ground floor (Layer 3): what might lead next? The watchlist holds only names still ignored by the crowd: communications (10 weeks of quiet improvement), Bitcoin (a liquidity-sensitive bounce), gold (waiting for its falsifiable turn signal). The whole point: get in during the quiet accumulation stage — the interactive rotation maps below let you drag through time and watch the tails turn.

RRG construction — exactly what you are looking at

The exact JdK RS-Ratio/RS-Momentum formulas are proprietary to RRG Research; this report uses the standard public approximation (rolling z-score family, point-in-time stable — adding a new week never redraws history):

RS     = 100 · P / Pbench  (adjusted closes)
RS-Ratio = 100 + z(RS, N)    z = rolling mean/std, ddof 0
RS-Mom   = 100 + z(100·(RSR/RSRt−k − 1), N)
weekly N=14, k=4 · daily N=21, k=5 (engineering choice)

Benchmarks: sectors vs SPY; asset classes vs synthetic 60/40 (0.6·SPY+0.4·IEF). Known pitfalls handled: rolling-window z only (full-sample normalization silently redraws tails); trailing k-period ROC (not since-inception); ≥50 bars before plotting. Axis ≈ 96-104 because 1×z ⇒ ~95% of values inside ±2. Evidence posture: zero peer-reviewed literature supports RRG quadrant rules; the "academic" papers often cited for it could not be located and appear fabricated (unverified); the one vendor test (Optuma 2016) is survivorship-biased, 2001-09, and favored contrarian Lagging entries. RRG here is a visualization of tested relative-strength momentum — the calibration panel in §04 is the only quadrant evidence we trust, and it is ours.

What this framework cannot do — printed, not buried

1. It cannot time turns to the week. The best-evidenced inputs (trend, momentum, credit) confirm with 1-3-month lags or lead with 1-13-month ranges; weekly cadence is a monitoring rhythm, not a timing edge — monthly-tested rules run weekly WILL whipsaw more than their backtests imply (hence month-end commits).  2. It cannot tell a liquidity regime from an earnings regime by itself — the costliest failure mode, mitigated (not removed) by tripwires and the NFCI cross-check.  3. It cannot distinguish early accumulation from a value trap in real time: the whole "early" stack fires repeatedly through genuine structural declines (2008 financials, 2014-20 energy). The value-trap brake is a discipline, not a solution.  4. No formal false-discovery control: ~40 signals × 12 tickers × weekly z-scores guarantees "extreme" cells every week; the 3-row cap and ≥2-family rule are behavioral brakes, not statistics — and an LLM-authored narrative will connect surviving dots post-hoc. The graveyard exists to audit exactly this.  5. Several cells are untested extrapolations at the level used, and say so on their face (insider sector rollup, HY-OAS→sector mapping, oil-curve→XLE, gold/real-yield n=1, every RRG quadrant rule).  6. Momentum-family signals decay ~26% out-of-sample and ~58% post-publication (McLean-Pontiff) — the durable edge, if any, is combination discipline plus the leading-panel divergences, not any famous ratio.  7. Bootstrap darkness: revision- and insider-breadth are dark until ~2028; the framework is honestly weaker in 2026-27 than its steady-state design.
