01

Executive snapshot

為什麼是現在

Five cooling deep-dives already mapped the cold plate, the quick-disconnect, the coolant and the CDU. This one goes to the layer they all sit on top of: the thermal interface material (TIM) — the paper-thin film between the silicon and the water-cooled plate. At ~700–1,000W it can be silicone paste. At Rubin's ~2,000W package it cannot — the interface has to go metal. And metal means gallium and indium, two byproduct metals China refines ~99% and ~68% of. That is the chokepoint. The catch — which this report is honest about — is that the rent may have no clean listed owner.

The dated catalyst — a public, un-priced-into-"cooling" event
EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

Imagine pressing a hot pan onto a cold countertop. Even if both look flat, microscopic air gaps trap the heat — so you smear a paste in between to carry it across. In an AI chip that paste is the thermal interface material (TIM), and the "countertop" is a water-cooled metal plate. Today's paste is cheap silicone. But new chips like NVIDIA's Rubin run so hot that silicone can't move the heat fast enough — engineers are switching to a liquid metal (a gallium-indium alloy that stays liquid at room temperature) or a soldered metal joint. The problem: gallium and indium are metals China controls almost entirely, and the liquid metal eats copper unless you plate a nickel shield first — so it's genuinely hard to do. That combination — scarce metal + hard to apply — is the chokepoint. The honest twist this report lands on: the purest maker (Indium Corporation) is private, the listed names are diluted or already-run, the metal is a tiny sliver of a $30,000 GPU, and chipmakers are already working to delete the paste entirely (etching water channels straight into the silicon). So: understand it, watch the November catalyst — but don't overpay for it.

03

The four ways to make the interface metal

介面元件

Three metal options replace polymer as power climbs — plus the disruptor that removes the interface entirely. Two of the three lean on gallium and/or indium; that's why the bottleneck is a materials story, not a one-quarter blip.

Liquid metal CHOKEPOINT

Gallium-indium-tin alloy, liquid at room temp. Highest bulk conductivity; cuts interface resistance ~60–70%. But it attacks copper — a hard-won nickel barrier + containment is the real IP.

Makers: Indium Corp (private), Henkel, Thermal Grizzly (consumer)

Indium solder TIM1

A thin indium foil reflowed into a solid metal joint — the incumbent high-end TIM1 in servers. No pump-out, very reliable, but consumes pure indium and needs a solder process.

Makers: Indium Corp, Henkel, DuPont, 5N Plus (metal)

Sinter silver substitute

Silver nanoparticles sintered into a solid bond — imported from SiC power modules. High conductivity, no gallium/indium. The key substitution risk to the Ga/In thesis.

Makers: Heraeus, MacDermid Alpha (Solstice), Kyocera

In-chip microfluidic 2028+

TSMC's IMC-Si / Microsoft's cold-plate etch water channels straight into the silicon — bypassing the TIM. The long-run disintermediation: it deletes this whole chokepoint.

Owners: TSMC (IMC-Si), Microsoft, Corintis (private)

04

Where the interface sits — the thermal stack, exploded

L3→L1 組裝

Heat leaves a transistor through a stack of interfaces. Every prior cooling run priced the right half (cold plate → CDU → facility). This run prices the two thin films on the left — TIM1 and TIM1.5 — and the metal + nickel barrier that make them work above ~1.5 kW.

The interface is two paper-thin films, but at Rubin power the film — not the plate — sets the junction temperature. The nickel barrier is a quiet co-requirement: without it, liquid metal destroys the copper plate the last four runs were about.

05

Which interface serves which cooling domain

部署對應

The same four options from §3 map onto the §2 domains by heat flux. The chokepoint only binds in the red band — high-power direct-to-chip — but that band is exactly where every flagship AI GPU is heading.

Immersion (§2) is a side pool with its own TIM failure mode (dissolution) and is not drawn on the flux axis. The point: the window where metal TIM is mandatory but not yet deleted is roughly 2026–2028 — a real but time-boxed rent.

08

The chokepoint, scored — and why it's capped at 15

卡點評分

Metal-TIM formulation + gallium/indium feedstock

Above ~1.5 kW/GPU (where die-level heat flux, W/cm², outruns polymer) the interface must go metal, and metal means (a) a qualified formulation + nickel-barrier / pump-out know-how held by a handful of makers (Indium Corp private, Henkel, Solstice/MacDermid, Honeywell), sitting on (b) gallium (China ~99%) and indium (China ~68%) — byproduct metals with inelastic supply and a live export-control history. Real scarcity — but deliberately scored below the rotation's 18–19 monopolies for three reasons: the formulation is an oligopoly not a monopoly, the gallium ban is currently suspended and non-China supply is ramping, and an in-chip disintermediation is already in demos.

VARIANT PERCEPTION · 變異認知 — what the Street misses

Consensus: "AI cooling = liquid cold plates, CDUs, coolant" — and it bids those names. The un-priced deeper cut: the interface itself is silently going from a commodity polymer (cheap, multi-sourced) to a scarce metal, and that metal sits on a China-concentrated feedstock with a dated catalyst nobody tags as a "cooling" trade — the Nov-27-2026 gallium/germanium ban-suspension expiry.

The disaggregation: the same power escalation that mandates metal TIM is also driving direct-die + in-chip microfluidics that delete it (TSMC IMC-Si commercial ~2027, Feynman 2028). So it's bullish metal TIM near-term, bearish the discrete external TIM long-term — and bullish Ga/In only if liquid-metal wins over Ga/In-free sinter-silver.

HOW IT'S WRONG · 反方 — the retail steelman

1 · Polymer holds the line. Henkel's TGF 10000 already hits 10 W/mK; better cold plates + PCM may defer metal TIM past 1.5 kW, slowing adoption.

2 · The ban stays suspended and non-China gallium (Alcoa, Rio Tinto Canada, Eurasian, US DPA Louisiana) ramps — capping the scarcity premium.

3 · Rent is a sliver. A few grams of Ga/In per GPU is immaterial to a $30k+ part — even a 3× metal price move barely moves a diversified material-maker's P&L. That's why no clean listed owner shows the rent in its margin. Margins settle it — and here they say the rent is diffuse.

09

Are we early? The awareness curve

現在是早期嗎

The whole game is owning a chokepoint before the crowd — the way the 2018 MLCC and 2021 CPU/substrate shortages were visible in lead-times and metal prices weeks before the stocks moved. The hard truth here: the purest play is private, and every listed proxy has either run, is diluted, or is merger-capped. The genuine early dollar is a catalyst + watchlist, not a stock to chase.

Horizontal = awareness stage (qualitative, by YTD move + how much is in the price), not a linear return scale. As-of 20 Jul 2026. The clean pure-play (Indium Corp) sits in the entry zone but is unlisted — the defining feature of this run.

Which metal actually wins the socket?

Liquid-metal, indium-solder and sinter-silver are all in play — and sinter-silver uses no Ga/In. If Rubin standardises on sinter (or TLP bonding), the gallium/indium leg of the thesis weakens materially. Is the rent big enough to matter?

A few grams of metal per GPU is immaterial to a $30k+ part. No listed maker yet shows the material moving consolidated margin — so the rent may be permanently too diffuse to own cleanly. This is the same "admire, don't own" failure mode as the GOES run (#21).
