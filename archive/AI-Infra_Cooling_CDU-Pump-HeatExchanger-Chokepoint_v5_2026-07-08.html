01

Executive snapshot

為什麼是現在

"AI needs cooling" is a top-3 infrastructure trade of 2026: a GB300 NVL72 rack now provisions 150+ kW and rejects ~2 MW+ of heat, air can no longer carry it, and NVIDIA's Rubin (2027) goes 100% liquid on warm-water direct-to-chip. Every one of those racks routes its heat through a coolant distribution unit (CDU) — the box that isolates the chip-side loop from facility water and pushes coolant with the flow and reliability the GPUs demand. CDUs run 12–18-month lead-times and the market grows an estimated ~$1B→$4–7.7B this decade. But this report asks the chokepoint question one layer down: the CDU box is assembled by 40+ vendors against a public NVIDIA/OCP reference design (Vertiv leads share, but hyperscalers qualify second sources by policy) — so what inside it is genuinely hard to replicate? The answer is the brazed-plate heat exchanger (BPHE) that transfers the heat between loops and the reliability-critical redundant pump — because if a pump stops, the chips throttle in seconds. The catch, and this report's main idea: the market already found the core (BPHE-centric play Kaori +~450%) and bought the pure-plays (CoolIT→Ecolab, Motivair→Schneider), so the choke is proven but the clean tickets are gone.

EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

A rack of AI chips now throws off as much heat as ~100 homes. Air can't remove it, so cool liquid is piped right up to the chips. But you can't run ordinary building water over $3–4 million of GPUs, so every rack needs a machine — the CDU (coolant distribution unit) — that keeps two water loops separate and pushes coolant around at the right pressure and flow. Inside that machine, two parts are genuinely hard to build well. First, the heat exchanger: a tight stack of thin metal plates that hands heat from the chip loop to the building loop without letting the two waters mix. Second, the pumps: they must run 24/7, because if a pump stops, the chips overheat and slow themselves down within seconds — so data centers always fit a spare that takes over in a blink. Lots of companies can bolt together the CDU box, so the box isn't the scarce thing. The two internals are. Here's the catch, and the point of this report: the market already worked this out. The one pure heat-exchanger stock (Kaori, in Taiwan) is up about five-fold, and the best pump makers are private European firms you simply can't buy shares in. So the bottleneck is real and clear — there's just no cheap, clean way left to own it. The least-crowded entry is a big, high-quality European maker (Alfa Laval) where this heat-exchanger business is a fast-growing but still-small slice.

LAYER KEY · read the loop like a ladder, chip → atmosphere

L1 CHIP cold-plate + TIM on the GPU die (Runs #4 / #16)  ·  MANIFOLD/QD in-rack plumbing + blind-mate quick-disconnects (Run #10)  ·  CDU CORE the pump + brazed-plate heat exchanger inside the coolant-distribution unit the chokepoint  ·  CDU BOX the assembled/qualified unit — multi-sourced, where integration & service rent sits  ·  FACILITY LOOP secondary→primary water, pipes, valves  ·  HEAT REJECT chiller / dry-cooler / cooling tower → atmosphere

6-TERM GLOSSARY

CDU coolant distribution unit — pumps + heat exchanger + valves + controls that isolate and circulate the chip-side loop · BPHE brazed-plate heat exchanger — a stack of pressed metal plates that transfers heat between two liquids without mixing them (the CDU's thermal core) · DLC / direct-to-chip liquid cooling with a cold-plate on the die; today mostly single-phase warm water · L2L / L2A liquid-to-liquid CDU (rejects to facility water) vs liquid-to-air (rejects to room air) · sealless / magnetic-drive pump a pump with no rotating shaft seal — fewer leak points, higher reliability (the premium tier) · N+1 / 2N redundancy a spare pump that auto-takes-over so a single failure can't kill the rack.

03

What's actually inside a CDU — the component gallery

卡點元件圖鑑

Draw the parts, don't just list them. A CDU is an assembly; the two links below (BPHE + pump) are the ones with real supply concentration, long qualification, and reliability stakes. The others (valves, controls, reservoir, filtration) matter for uptime but are more commoditised.

Brazed-plate heat exchanger (BPHE) CORE

The thermal heart: hands heat from the chip loop to the facility loop across pressed, vacuum-brazed plates that keep the two waters separate. Tighter "approach temperature" = more cooling per unit — the metric that binds as Rubin flow doubles.

Who: Alfa Laval, Kaori (TW, BPHE-centric), SWEP (Dover), Danfoss, Kelvion — a concentrated few at the high-performance end.

Sealless / magnetic-drive pump CORE

Moves the coolant 24/7. The premium tier is sealless (magnetic-drive/canned): the impeller and rotor are one piece with no shaft seal, so there is no seal to wear or leak. Reliability is the whole game — a stop throttles the chips in seconds.

Who: Grundfos > Wilo > Xylem (private/diluted); Moog (sealless CoreMotion), Nidec — the listed challengers.

Redundancy + controls + leak-detection RELIABILITY

The reason the pump can't be a monopoly: because it's the single-point-of-failure, hyperscalers demand N+1/2N, 100 ms hot-standby and leak sensors — and multi-source the pump. Reliability engineering caps any one vendor's pricing power (echo of Run #10's open-UQD finding).

Who: CDU integrators + sensor/valve/filter suppliers; leak-detection is an under-covered private "reliability razor."
04

Chip → CDU → facility — how the core builds up into the loop

L3→L2→L1 組裝

The whole-view: the die's heat travels cold-plate → manifold → CDU (pump pushes it, BPHE hands it across) → facility water → heat-reject. Two isolated loops meet at exactly one component — the BPHE — and one component keeps them moving — the pump. That's why the CDU core is the pinch.

The BPHE is the single membrane between the clean chip-side coolant and the messier facility water; the pump is the single thing keeping both moving. Everything else in the CDU (valves, controls, reservoir, filtration, leak sensors) supports those two. As Rubin roughly doubles flow, both the pump duty and the BPHE approach-temp tighten at once — which is why the "core" is the chokepoint, not the "box."

05

The central argument — the box is not the scarce link; the core is

拆解：盒子 vs 核心

The disaggregation that is the edge. Consensus buys "the CDU makers / the thermal integrator" (Vertiv). But two things pull the durable rent one layer down — and one thing (already-priced pure-plays) pulls the edge back out.

VARIANT PERCEPTION vs CONSENSUS

Consensus: the CDU maker / thermal integrator (Vertiv, +80% YTD) is the AI-cooling chokepoint. Variant: the CDU box is assembled by 40+ NVIDIA/OCP partners against a public reference CDU design (Vertiv leads share today, but hyperscalers qualify second sources by policy) so its lead is second-sourceable, not a supply monopoly — its integration rent is real but erodes toward the reference design; the durable scarcity + reliability rent sits one layer below at the BPHE + reliable pump. The honest catch (how the edge is already half-gone): the market has proven this — the BPHE-centric play (Kaori) is up ~450% and the CDU pure-plays got bought (CoolIT→Ecolab $4.75B announced Mar-2026, Motivair→Schneider $850M closed Feb-2025) — so the residual, un-crowded expression is the diluted BPHE proxy (Alfa Laval, +18%) and the private pump layer (Nidec as the listed proxy), not a fresh pure-play. If we're wrong, it's because (a) the pump/BPHE are genuinely multi-sourceable (N+1 by design) so neither earns a monopoly toll; (b) the integrator's 12–18-mo qualification + service annuity is a bigger moat than "the box is dumb" assumes, so Vertiv's premium is deserved; or (c) in-chip microfluidics (Run #16, 2028+) deletes the whole external loop — pump, BPHE and CDU together.

08

Chokepoint callout + 4-factor severity

卡點評分

The CDU core — the reliability-critical pump + the brazed-plate heat exchanger

Every liquid-cooled AI rack rejects its heat through a coolant-distribution unit, and inside it two links don't commoditise: the brazed-plate heat exchanger that transfers the heat between an isolated chip loop and facility water, and the reliable, redundant pump that keeps both moving — because a pump stop throttles the GPUs in seconds. Both tighten as Rubin roughly doubles flow. But this is a severity-15, not a monopoly-18 chokepoint: there are several qualified BPHE and pump makers, hyperscalers deliberately multi-source (N+1/2N) precisely because it's the single-point-of-failure, and NVIDIA's own reference CDU + OCP designs keep the box multi-vendor. Tightness is real; a monopoly toll is not.

Scope & co-bottlenecks — don't over-narrow

The 15/20 scores the CDU core. Co-binding links tighten alongside and could be the "real" pinch in specific builds: the quick-disconnect coupling (Run #10, severity-15, at ~450 junctions/rack), the 2P-DTC co-qualified system + secondary loop (Run #16, severity-16, as single-phase tops out), and the cold-plate itself (Run #4). If flow at Rubin forces two-phase sooner than expected, the pump duty (refrigerant-rated) tightens further; if in-chip microfluidics arrives early, the whole core loosens. Remaining asymmetry ~6/20: the choke is real but the pure vehicles (Kaori +450%) and pure-plays (CoolIT, Motivair) are gone — tightness ≠ a good buy.
