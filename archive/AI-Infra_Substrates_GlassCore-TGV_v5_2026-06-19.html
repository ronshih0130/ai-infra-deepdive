01

Executive snapshot

總覽
EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

Every powerful chip sits on a substrate — a small circuit board that fans the chip's thousands of tiny contacts out to the motherboard. For 30 years that board has been made of organic plastic resin (the "ABF" build-up film from Run #6). But AI accelerator packages are now bigger than a credit card, and plastic warps and bends at that size. The fix is to switch the core of the board from plastic to glass — glass stays flat, runs cooler, and carries faster signals. The catch: to wire a glass board you must drill tens of thousands of holes straight through a sheet of glass (a "Through-Glass-Via", TGV) without cracking it, then fill each with copper. Glass shatters from a single micro-crack — so this drilling step is the chokepoint: whoever masters TGV yield owns the transition. The honest part: this is a 2027–2029 story, not a 2026 one, and plastic just got a 5–7-year life-extension (CoWoS-L). So the pure-play stocks have already run on the promise — and the real edge is owning cheap, buried exposure and waiting for proof, not chasing the laser stock that has roughly quadrupled.

Run #6 named today's binding substrate chokepoint: ABF build-up film (Ajinomoto ~95%) + low-DK glass cloth (Nittobo ~90%). This run goes one paradigm deeper — to the material that replaces organic when packages outgrow it. The scarce link is not the glass (an oligopoly of Corning / AGC / SCHOTT / NEG, but available) and not the finished substrate (several makers racing) — it is the Through-Glass-Via (TGV) formation step and its yield: drilling fine, deep, smooth-walled holes through brittle glass and filling them with copper, where a single micro-crack scraps the panel. The investable honesty: the purest exposures (the LIDE tool, the glass) have already re-rated on anticipation; the genuine early-alpha is cheap/buried exposure (SKC's Absolics; the still-latent glass and consumables names) entered on a yield-crossover catalyst — sized small because the timeline can slip to 2028+.

The components, illustrated

Three pictures decode the layer. The chokepoint is the middle one — TGV formation — where brittle glass meets micron-scale drilling.

Glass vs organic core why switch

Glass holds flatness, matches silicon's thermal expansion (less solder fatigue), and has ~10× lower dielectric loss — letting packages grow past the size where organic warps.
Material: Corning · AGC · SCHOTT · NEG

Through-Glass-Via CHOKEPOINT

Tens of thousands of fine, deep, smooth-walled holes drilled through brittle glass, then void-free copper-filled. Micro-cracks, warpage and poor fill are the yield killers — a single crack scraps the panel.
Tools: LPKF (LIDE) · Schmoll · Philoptics · F&S Electron

LIDE deep-etch enabling tool

Laser-Induced Deep Etching: a laser locally modifies the glass, then a wet etch opens clean sub-10µm holes with no melt cracks — the leading method for high-density TGV. >80% of major customers reportedly selected LPKF kit for evaluation / early lines (est., company/trade press).
Owner: LPKF (LIDE) · alt. methods exist

08

The chokepoint — and how tight it really is

卡點與嚴重度

Through-Glass-Via (TGV) formation & yield

Glass shatters from a single micro-crack. To wire a glass-core substrate you must form tens of thousands of fine (<10µm), deep, smooth-walled holes through a brittle panel and fill them void-free with copper — at 75–85% yield versus organic's 90–95%, at 2–3× the cost. Whoever closes that yield gap first owns the transition. But unlike Run #6's ABF film, this rent is diffuse and forward-dated: the glass is an available oligopoly, the TGV capability spans several tool-makers and methods (LIDE, mechanical, alt-laser), and a handful of substrate fabs are racing — so no one yet holds a single ~95%-share toll. And organic just bought itself an estimated 5–7 more years (CoWoS-L; est., bear case). The tightness is anticipatory, not binding.

VARIANT / DISAGGREGATION — WHAT THE CROWD MISREADS

Consensus is buying "glass substrate = 2026 AI story" — and has bid the purest exposures (the LIDE tool roughly +330–370%, the glass leader +101%) as if the transition is imminent. The disaggregation: (1) the scarce link is not the glass (available) nor the finished substrate (several racers) — it's TGV yield, a capability, not a part; (2) it binds in 2027–29, and organic just extended an estimated 5–7 years via CoWoS-L (est., bear case) — Ibiden (~90% of NVIDIA's substrates) shows no glass commercialization roadmap and its 2026 capex is organic-only (though it is in TSMC's CoPoS glass-validation program). So the pure-plays price a 2026 inflection that is really 2028. The edge is to own cheap/buried exposure (a maker hidden in a conglomerate; the glass oligopoly at a normal multiple; the consumables beneath it) and let the yield-crossover catalyst re-rate it — not chase the tool that already tripled.

RENT-CAPTURE TEST — WHO KEEPS THE SCARCITY RENT?

Margins settle it. Today (pre-volume): the equipment maker books the first orders (LPKF book-to-bill 1.4) — but on tiny, shrinking, loss-making revenue, so it's early-cyclical rent, not durable. At volume (2028+): the substrate maker that cracks yield first captures the durable rent (Absolics is furthest along); the glass oligopoly (Corning/AGC) earns a stable toll throughout; and if yield commoditizes, the buyer (TSMC/Intel/AMD) keeps most of it. Net: rent migrates downstream from tool → fab over the cycle — which argues for the substrate maker as the durable holder and the tool as the high-beta early trade.
