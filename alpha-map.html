白話版 — the headline in plain English

Imagine four companies all reported spectacular results in the same week — the world's most important chip factory, Google, the biggest maker of power equipment for data centres, and the maker of the machines that stack chips together. Every one of them beat, and several raised their forecasts. Every one of their share prices went down or nowhere.

That is what "already priced in" actually looks like. The facts about the AI build-out are getting better, not worse — Google is now planning to spend up to $205 billion this year, Korea's chip exports nearly tripled in the first twenty days of July. But if good news no longer makes prices go up, then knowing the good news is worth nothing. Being right and making money are different things, and this map exists to keep those two apart.

So this week the honest answer is mostly "don't". Last week's favourite idea — a small German machine-maker called AIXTRON that grows the crystal layers for the lasers inside AI networks — reports in three days, its options are priced for maximum drama, and analysts are forecasting more revenue than the company itself has guided to. That is not an edge, that is a coin flip. So we take it off the top spot and wait for the number.

What replaces it is a genuinely new observation. Two groups of analysts argue about two separate things: one group asks "are chip-stacking machines busy?", the other asks "will a new optical design kill the old one?". This week BESI let slip that its chip-bonding machines are now being bought for that new optical design too. Which means the machine-makers win either way — and nobody is joining those two arguments together. The cheapest way to look at it is SUSS MicroTec, whose order book just hit an all-time record while its published forecast says this will be a down year. It reports Aug 6. That gap between "orders at a record" and "profits look terrible" is exactly where under-priced information tends to live.

7-term glossary
Consensus gap
The difference between what analysts publicly model and what I think the number will be. No gap = no trade, however tight the supply is.
Priced / EARLY-ineligible
A name with >15 analysts and deep options can't be an early idea — too many people already do the work. It's a yardstick, never the edge.
IV percentile
Where today's option-implied volatility sits in its own past year, 0–1. Near 1.00 = the market is paying up for protection or for a bet; near 0.40 = calm.
Blowout-sold
A record result that the share price falls on. The cleanest single proof that expectations were already above the news.
Order-vs-revenue gap
Equipment makers book orders long before they can recognise revenue. A record backlog next to a collapsing income statement is a timing artefact — and one of the few places retail information advantage genuinely survives.
Architecture-agnostic
A supplier that wins no matter which competing design standard is adopted downstream. The safest kind of chokepoint to own, because you skip the technology bet entirely.
Event binary
A position whose outcome is decided by one scheduled announcement. Not an information edge — a coin flip with a date on it.
