01

Executive snapshot

為什麼是現在

A GPU runs at under 1 volt but draws thousands of amps; the grid delivers hundreds of thousands of volts. Bridging that gap used to be easy because racks were small. They are not small anymore. NVIDIA's roadmap takes a rack to 1 MW and rips out the old 54V distribution for an 800-volt DC (HVDC) architecture (Rubin Ultra / Kyber, 2026–27). That forces a brutal engineering problem into a few rack-units — and the parts that solve it (wide-bandgap silicon + high-frequency magnetics) become the new bottleneck. The twist this report documents: the chip everyone is buying is in glut, while the unglamorous magnetic core nobody talks about is reportedly sold out.

EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

Think of getting power to a GPU like getting water to a city tap. The reservoir (the grid) is at enormous pressure; the tap (the chip) needs a gentle trickle. You step the pressure down in stages. The more water the city needs, the bigger and hotter those pumping stations get — until they no longer fit. NVIDIA's answer is to switch to a much higher-pressure pipe inside the building (800-volt DC) so fewer, smaller pumps are needed. Two parts do the actual pressure-stepping: special switching chips (silicon-carbide and gallium-nitride) and the transformer/inductor "magnetics" wrapped around a special metal core. Here is the catch most people miss: the chips are still relatively abundant (the electric-car slump left an overhang that is only now being absorbed), but you cannot shrink a magnetic core with Moore's law — and the best core material is reportedly sold out. So the genuine bottleneck is the boring metal ring, not the shiny chip. The investing question: every obvious power stock has already doubled — is there anything still early, and where?

03

The conversion layer, illustrated

轉換層元件

The four building blocks that turn 800 volts into a clean <1V supply at the GPU. The first two (the switches) are abundant today; the third (the magnetics) is where the physical scarcity concentrates; the fourth (energy buffer) is the next-latent frontier.

SiC device ABUNDANT

Silicon-carbide switches block high voltage with low loss — the front-end AC/DC and the solid-state transformer. Still working off the EV-era oversupply, so capacity is not today's constraint; qualified datacenter design-ins are.

Makers: Infineon, onsemi, STM, ROHM, Wolfspeed

GaN device CONTESTED

Gallium-nitride switches faster than silicon, shrinking the DC-DC stage and the magnetics with it. The smallest-dollar link but the highest-growth — and every silicon vendor is piling in, so pricing power is thin.

Makers: Navitas, Infineon, Innoscience, TI, Power Integrations

HF magnetics CHOKEPOINT

The transformer + inductor that store and step energy. At 500kHz–1MHz, ordinary steel cores burn up; you need nanocrystalline ribbon — reportedly sold out through mid-2026. The one link Moore's law cannot shrink.

Cores: VAC (VITROPERM), Proterial (FINEMET), TDK

BBU / super-cap NEXT-LATENT

AI loads swing 1–50 ms; the DC bus sags. A hybrid super-capacitor + battery "sidecar" smooths it. Barely a market today — production ramps land in FY2027, so it is the next link to wake.

Players: Panasonic (CBU), Skeleton, Infineon, Eaton

電網到晶片的建構

Every watt passes through every stage left-to-right, shedding voltage at each step. The value and the scarcity concentrate in the middle — at the high-frequency conversion + magnetics — because that is the step that is hardest to shrink and impossible to skip. 800V HVDC's whole point is to delete conversion stages, which makes the survivors more critical.

The same conversion layer serves three jobs at different voltages. Note that magnetics appear in every isolated conversion box — only the bare point-of-load step (already low voltage) escapes the transformer.

08

The chokepoint — and how tight

卡點與嚴重度

High-frequency conversion + nanocrystalline magnetics

You cannot deliver a megawatt into a rack without converting it many times, and high-density conversion needs switching at 500 kHz–1 MHz — speeds at which ordinary silicon-steel cores overheat. The default is nanocrystalline ribbon (VAC's VITROPERM, Proterial's FINEMET), reportedly sold out through mid-2026 as AI capex tops $200B/yr. This is the link Moore's law cannot help: a magnetic core stores energy by physical volume, so it cannot be shrunk like a transistor. The catch for investors: the constraint is real, but the obvious listed plays around it (the silicon, the PSU integrator) have already re-rated +82–231% YTD, and the purest core makers are private — so the edge is narrow and specific.

Variant perception · the disaggregation that is the edge 變異認知

CONSENSUS BELIEVES

The headline "800V / NVIDIA power trade" is the GaN pure-play (Navitas, +231% YTD); the broader "AI power" money crowds into the integrators and SiC names (Vertiv, Delta, onsemi). The shared belief: the power semiconductor / system box is the bottleneck, and the design-win headline is the thesis.

THIS REPORT'S VARIANT

The device is not the scarce link: SiC is still oversupplied (the EV overhang is only now absorbing), GaN capacity is ramping, and the chipmakers have all re-rated +110–231% YTD. The genuinely scarce, un-shrinkable link is the high-frequency magnetics + nanocrystalline core (reportedly sold out). And the durable rent pools at the integration box (Delta, >60% share) and the broad silicon gate (Infineon) — not the contested pure-play priced at ~125× sales on revenue that fell 39% YoY.

How it could be wrong: GaN/SiC stay genuinely supply-tight and the pure-plays keep winning sole-source sockets at premium prices; magnetics suppliers expand fast and the shortage clears within a year; the NVIDIA 800V roadmap slips to 2027+ and pulls in the whole layer's demand; or Delta's >60% share erodes to LiteOn/Megmeet as hyperscalers force dual-sourcing.

RENT-CAPTURE TEST · who keeps the scarcity rent? (margins settle it)

Rank by where the dollar sticks: integration box ≈ broad device gate > core material > GaN pure-play. The PSU/power-shelf integrator (Delta) captures rising ASP ($43.8k→$171k/server) at a structural >60% share — real, growing rent. The broad silicon gate (Infineon) sells qualified parts into every stage at healthy margin. The core maker (VAC/Proterial) is the scarcest physical link yet keeps only modest rent and is largely private/unbuyable. The GaN pure-play (Navitas) sits at the smallest, most-contested sub-link — tiny revenue, negligible margin — yet trades at the highest multiple. So the scarcest link is unbuyable, the most-priced link is the least-scarce, and the best-priced rent today sits at the integrator and the broad gate.

How to be early next time 如何提前布局

1 · Find the link Moore's law can't shrink

Transistors get cheaper every node; a magnetic core, a copper busbar and a capacitor do not. When demand surges, the un-shrinkable physical part is where scarcity sticks — here, the nanocrystalline core.

2 · Distrust the headline design-win

The press-release stock (Navitas) sits at the smallest, most-contested link. The biggest dollar content (the PSU box, Delta) and the steadiest rent (the broad gate, Infineon) rarely make the headline.

3 · A glut is not a bottleneck

SiC is in oversupply from the EV slump — so "buy the SiC shortage" is a 2022 thesis, not a 2026 one. Check the actual lead-times before assuming a part is scarce.

4 · Respect that "tight" ≠ "cheap"

The magnetics are tight and the whole layer is up 82–231% YTD. Tightness tells you the business is good; only price tells you whether you are early — and here, you mostly are not.
