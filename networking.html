Executive snapshot

為什麼是現在

EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

To train a giant AI model, thousands of GPUs must talk to each other at once, super fast, as if they were one chip. The signals run so fast (224 billion bits/second per wire) that the electrical pulse smears out after barely a few feet of copper. So you need a little chip called a SerDes / retimer to "redraw" the signal cleanly, and smart cables (AECs) with chips inside. These are the picks-and-shovels between the GPUs. The investor question: own the incumbent that makes the switch chips and owns the best SerDes (Broadcom), or the pure-play specialists (Astera retimers, Credo cables) — and notice that the specialists have already run while the incumbent hasn't.

03

The chokepoint & severity

卡點與嚴重度

224G connectivity silicon (SerDes · retimers · AECs)

At 224 Gbps/lane the electrical signal collapses across even a half-rack of copper — so the link physically cannot exist without chips that clean and re-drive it. That makes the 224G SerDes and retimers the binding link of scale-up networking, and the smart cables (AECs) that carry them. The honest caveat: leading-edge SerDes is held by a handful of players (Broadcom, Marvell, Astera, Credo, plus IP from Alphawave/Synopsys), so it's an oligopoly, not a monopoly — and these are chips, which ramp fast. The forcing function is real: UALink, ESUN and Ethernet all converge on the 224G standard, so demand is non-optional.

VARIANT PERCEPTION vs CONSENSUS · 變異認知

Consensus: "AI networking is a winner — buy the high-flying pure-plays (Astera, Credo)." The disaggregation: the pure-plays are the chokepoint, but they're already euphoric (+121% / +84%), while the company that owns the dominant switch ASIC, the leading 224G SerDes IP, AND the custom-XPU franchise — Broadcom — is up just +11% and trades below its 52-week high, because that rent is diluted inside a mega-cap with software and legacy semis. So the binding link's scarcest IP sits in the cheapest stock. The contrarian sub-claim: own the gate (AVGO) for risk-adjusted asymmetry; the pure-plays need the scale-up TAM to compound flawlessly to justify the price. If we're wrong on AVGO, it's that custom-XPU competition (Marvell, in-house designs) erodes its franchise faster than networking grows.

Who captures the rent?

Margins settle it. AVGO holds the scarcest IP (224G SerDes) plus the switch ASIC and custom-XPU franchise — fat margins, but the AI-networking rent is diluted inside a $T+ company with software and legacy chips. ALAB and CRDO are pure connectivity plays — every dollar is the chokepoint, which is why they re-rated so hard, but they need the scale-up TAM to compound flawlessly. Conclusion: the scarcest IP sits in the cheapest stock (AVGO); the purest exposure sits in the priciest (ALAB/CRDO). Pick your trade-off.
