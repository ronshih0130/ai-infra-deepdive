01

Executive snapshot

為什麼是現在

An AI package substrate is a stack of build-up layers: thin sheets of Ajinomoto film, each drilled with tens of thousands of tiny holes (microvias), then plated with copper to wire the layers together and etched into hair-fine circuits. As GPUs grow (Blackwell >2× Hopper substrate area; Rubin package-substrate area ~+75% again, est., single-source) and lines shrink (mSAP 15µm→<10µm on FC-BGA; fan-out RDL to 2µm on a different, finer package class — see §2), the number of plating steps and their difficulty rise faster than the substrate count. The scarce, sticky link is the plating chemistry that makes those steps yield.

EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

A chip's "substrate" is the fancy circuit board it sits on — and it's built by gluing on thin plastic film, drilling tiny holes, and then growing copper into those holes and into hair-thin wires. Growing that copper needs special chemical baths (the "plating chemistry"). Think of it as the secret sauce that lets the copper fill perfectly without voids or shorts. The finer the wires get (and AI chips need them very fine), the harder the sauce is to make — and only a handful of firms know the recipe, which they must re-certify with each customer over a year or two, so nobody switches. That certified recipe is the chokepoint. The market is busy buying the film maker (Ajinomoto) and the board maker (Ibiden, up ~200% this year). This report asks the quieter question: who owns the copper-growing chemistry — and is that link still cheap (early) or already expensive (late)? Short answer: the cleanest pure-play (JCU) is up ~44% YTD while the board maker is up ~200% — the scarce recipe sits in the cheaper stock. Honest caveat: JCU is not undiscovered (it's ~+120% over 12 months and a handful of specialist bulls have begun writing it up), so the edge here is relative value versus the priced fab, not virgin discovery.

03

Layer 3 — the fab process, illustrated

製程元件

How a bare sheet of ABF film becomes a wired build-up layer, in four steps. Two of the four are chemistry steps — and that is exactly where the bottleneck lives.

1 · Laser via-drill CO-BIND

A CO₂/UV laser burns tens of thousands of microvias through each ABF layer. Throughput-limited: finer, denser vias need slower, more precise drilling.

Tools: Hitachi High-Tech · Mitsubishi · Via Mechanics · MKS/ESI

2 · Electroless-Cu seed CHOKEPOINT

A catalytic chemical bath grows a thin, uniform copper seed on the (non-conductive) film and hole walls — the foundation everything else plates onto. Deeply co-qualified; failure = shorts/opens.

Chem: Atotech/MKS · MacDermid · JCU · Uyemura · Dow

3 · Dry-film + direct imaging

A photoresist film is laminated, then a maskless laser writes the circuit pattern, opening channels where copper will grow. Finer L/S needs finer resist + higher-resolution imagers.

SCREEN · Adtec · Orbotech/KLA · resist: Asahi Kasei · Resonac

4 · Via-fill + SAP plate CHOKEPOINT

An electrolytic bath fills the vias void-free and builds copper up in the resist channels; a final flash-etch removes the seed, leaving fine SAP lines. Void-free fill at high aspect ratio is the hardest chemistry.

Chem: JCU (CU-BRITE) · Atotech/MKS · MacDermid · Dow

Steps 2 & 4 are the chemistry. They don't shrink with Moore's law — they get harder as lines shrink and vias multiply, and each recipe is co-qualified per customer/node. That stickiness is the moat.

08

The chokepoint & severity scorecard

卡點評分

Plating / metallization chemistry — the co-qualified razor

Every microvia filled and every fine line grown inside an AI substrate passes through a handful of co-qualified chemical baths. The recipe is certified per customer and per node over 12–24 months, then supplies a decade-long consumable stream at above-corporate margins — a genuine toll. But it is an oligopoly, not a monopoly (top-5 ~50–60%; Atotech/MKS, MacDermid, JCU, Uyemura, Dow), with rising Chinese domestic suppliers 10–20% cheaper — so severity lands at 15/20, below the 18–19 Ajinomoto/GOES monopolies of earlier runs. Tightness is real; it is not absolute.

09

Variant perception — the disaggregation

認知差

The substrate trade is really two stacked shortages the Street already bids — the ABF material (Ajinomoto) and the ABF fab (Ibiden, +202%). Between them sits a third link the crowd doesn't tag as an "AI stock": the plating chemistry that turns bare film into fine-line copper. Its cleanest vehicle (JCU) is up only +44%.

CONSENSUS

"Buy the AI substrate shortage." → Ibiden +202% at ~72×, or "buy AI advanced packaging" → MKS +131%. The material (Ajinomoto) and the fab (Ibiden) are both priced. The plating chemistry is treated as a boring industrial input, not part of the AI trade.

OUR VARIANT

The durable rent sits one layer below the fab, in the co-qualified plating chemistry — a razor that gets harder (2µm, panel) as AI scales, sold by a top-5 oligopoly. And panel fan-out is bullish the chemistry even though it threatens wafer OSATs — more RDL area to plate. The purest vehicle (JCU) is the laggard.

THE DISAGGREGATION

"AI substrates" is bullish the aggregate but the crowd buys the fab (priced). Disaggregate the fab line and the un-bid, most-durable link is the metallization chemistry — and within chemistry, the pure-play (JCU) is bullish while the diluted proxies (MKS at 3 divisions; Uyemura at ENIG-heavy mix) share it. The strategic tell: Solstice is paying $14.5B for Element Solutions/MacDermid (6-Jul-2026) explicitly for electronics/packaging chemistry — a buyer validating the chokepoint while removing a listed vehicle (the Alphawave→Qualcomm, CoolIT→Ecolab pattern again).

Open questions & honest holes

自我批判
Unproven rent capture

The thesis assumes the chemistry keeps a durable, high-margin toll. But the big absolute rent visibly settles at the fab (Ibiden's +89% EPS) — the chemistry is a small-dollar link. If pricing power stays downstream, JCU is just a nice compounder, not a chokepoint winner.

China substitution is the swing factor

The single biggest hole: Chinese domestic chemistries at −10–20% are qualifying up-tier, and JCU earns 25% in China. I can't yet prove leading-node fabs will not qualify them — that's the falsifier to watch.

Estimated shares

"Top-5 ~50–60%", the ~18-mo drill backlog, FC-BGA fab shares, and the Atotech ~$1.4–1.5B split are all triangulated estimates from market-report aggregators, not audited disclosures. Treat as directional.

Denominators & dilution

MKS's AI-substrate chemistry is a fraction of a fraction of a ~$4B revenue base; JCU's semiconductor slice is buried inside "electronics" (~45%). Neither is a clean read — both need segment disclosure I don't fully have.

Wrong-link risk

I feature the chemistry, but the via-drill equipment (backlog) or the dry-film/DI could bind tighter at 2µm. If test/imaging is the real gate, the trade migrates to SCREEN/Adtec/resist.

Retail steelman of the bear

"JCU is up 44% in a year of AI mania on a business that's 45% non-electronics; Ibiden already is the substrate trade; and if I want chemistry I can buy MKS in USD. Why reach for an illiquid ¥172B Tokyo small-cap?" — a fair challenge; the answer is purity + price, but it requires conviction the AI mix re-rates it.
