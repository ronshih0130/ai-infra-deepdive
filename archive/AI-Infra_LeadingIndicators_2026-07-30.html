The system's binding constraint has finished rotating out of silicon entirely and now sits in POWER/GRID — specifically in energized, near-term-deliverable megawatts. The tell is not the GRID node itself but the fact that three other nodes defer to it: CLOUD's bottleneck is no longer GPU allocation or even shells but interconnect queues and transformers, confirmed by Alphabet conceding it will rent third-party neocloud capacity in Q3; COOL states outright that cooling added in 2027 will be gated by interconnection rather than fabrication; SERVER names grid power as its next rotation. DOE authorizing PJM to curtail data-center load during the July heat wave is the regime marker — the scarce input is now being rationed administratively. Within GRID the sub-link is moving off the iron (2028+ relief from Hitachi Energy, Siemens Energy, GEV) toward energization, electrical field labor and behind-the-meter bridging power, and then one step further into funding capacity, where hyperscaler spreads have widened to ~78bp, Oracle CDS sits above 125bp and aggregate capex has crossed above operating cash flow. The uncomfortable part is that GRID scores both the tightest (9) and the most priced (9), so the honest conclusion is that the constraint is real while its obvious US equity expression is spent; residual asymmetry has migrated (a) upstream into raw materials the market does not classify as AI — ABF build-up film, InP substrate, MLCC dielectric and nickel powder — (b) offshore into JP/EU grid and power-electronics names, and (c) into the two places where the order book and the tape are pointing in opposite directions: powered-capacity landlords with prepaid contracts, and cooling integrators that de-rated 20-30% in the same week they printed record backlogs. What is rotating out is everything that was the 2025 story: CoWoS slots, cold plates, 12V power stages, 1.6T module assembly, rack integration capacity, and the AI-server margin-compression bear case that SMCI's 7/21 print largely disproved. The framing risk to hold onto is that this dashboard treats backlogs as leading and price as lagging; the COOL divergence is the live test of whether that premise survives the 7/29-7/30 hyperscaler prints. Highest-alpha contradictions

Per the framework, the best signals come from contradictions & bottleneck rotation (capex rising but cloud prices falling; backlog rising but margins falling).

  • GRID is named the binding constraint by CLOUD, COOL and SERVER, yet its own read is signal=neutral with priced=9 — the dashboard is simultaneously asserting that the tightest link in the system carries the least residual asymmetry. Both cannot be fully true; either the node-level priced score is measuring only the crowded US mega-cap expression (GEV/ETN/VRT) while the constraint rent is accruing to offshore and labor sub-links, or the 'binding constraint' designation is a physical statement with no tradable consequence. This is the single most important unresolved tension in the run.
  • MEM says commodity DRAM is the leg that is cracking first (Huaqiangbei DDR4 -30% from peak, contract momentum decelerating to +13-18% QoQ) and flips the node to peak_tightness, while CPU and SERVER both name server DRAM as the newly BINDING constraint at 40+ week lead times, record RDIMM pricing and 35-40% channel fulfilment. Directionally these are opposite reads of 'DRAM'; they reconcile only if the consumer/spot and server/HBM legs have fully decoupled, which is an assumption the dashboard asserts but does not evidence. Anyone treating DRAM as one variable will mis-time both.
  • COOL is the clearest order-versus-price divergence in the run and its interpretation is genuinely ambiguous: record backlogs (Comfort Systems $14.06B +73%, Munters 3.6x book-to-bill) against a 20-30% equity de-rate into 7/24. Either the market is discounting an AI-capex durability break that the order books have not yet seen — in which case the backlog is the lagging indicator and the tape is the leading one, inverting this dashboard's core premise — or the de-rate is the opportunity. The COOL node cut priced 7→6 on the assumption it is the latter, which is a judgement call, not a measurement. Note the node also says its own share of the marginal constraint is peaking while carrying signal=bull.
  • POWER contains an internal trap: low-end and automotive SiC is in outright overcapacity with Yole projecting a downturn through 2027-28, while AI-grade high-voltage and high-current parts are short — so a credible, widely-reported 'SiC glut' headline could de-rate precisely the names that own the tight sub-link. Separately, the framework is revising its own prior-week calls at a high rate (PASSIVE retracts 'VSH is the least-repriced proxy' as stale; MEM inverts 'buy SKHY' to 'own the KR line' on a ~30% wrapper premium), which is intellectually honest but implies the weekly signal is less stable than the tightness/priced scores imply.

Method & honest caveats

Two data layers. (1) Computed (free, deterministic, reruns weekly): SEC EDGAR XBRL revenue-acceleration + gross-margin trend + inventory/RPO (point-in-time via filing date), Yahoo 52-week position + relative-strength vs ^SOX, live DigiKey + Mouser part-number lead-times for the catalog-distributable nodes (power silicon now up to 61.5wk, MLCC/passives ~24wk, retimers ~26wk, memory modules ~30wk), public cloud GPU rental pricing from Lambda/RunPod/Azure, LBNL interconnection queue requested/IA-executed MW for system-level GRID stress, Texas TDLR/TABS data-center project registrations plus a derived utility follow-up watchlist for site-level construction evidence and next MW/service-date proof targets, a source-linked transformer supply rowset, and a source-linked ISO/utility large-load rowset for direct data-center/load evidence. The estimates scaffold validates consensus_estimates.csv, FMP prior earnings events, FMP earnings-calendar timing rows, and FMP analyst-awareness rows, but current-snapshot / timing / recognition-stage rows do not unlock the PIT action gate. Nodes with no free exact feed yet (HBM contract price, CoWoS/ABF capacity, CPU attach/availability) carry research-sourced leading metrics instead, flagged as such. (2) Research (web, sourced, per subsector + SemiAnalysis article/ChipBook-derived metadata): an independent agent verifies tickers, assigns the shortage stage, and pulls recent sourced evidence. SemiAnalysis rows use article titles/URLs and derived classifications; if subscription body text is used, it is transient and not saved. This layer is refreshed weekly and adversarially self-reviewed by Codex (gpt-5.5, highest effort) role-playing a retail investor.

Caveats — do not over-trust. (1) The "priced-in" axis is a price-position proxy, not a valuation model; with ^SOX +101%/6mo the whole sector is extended, so COMPUTED-INFLECTION names are relatively un-run, not cheap. (2) US quarterly fundamentals lag a monthly channel-check; the live edge is the distributor lead-time + the research layer, not the 10-Q. (3) Curated stages carry a confidence flag — low-confidence nodes are hypotheses. (4) Taiwan-listed names (Tai-Tech, GUC, Alchip, Quanta, Yageo, Unimicron) are the true pure-plays in several nodes but are not in your IBKR access — they are shown for completeness and routed to a tradable proxy; the proxy is an imperfect substitute. (5) This is a discovery/triage map, NOT a buy list — every candidate needs an identity + AI-revenue-purity + valuation check before sizing. (6) IBKR is READ-ONLY; nothing here is investment advice.
