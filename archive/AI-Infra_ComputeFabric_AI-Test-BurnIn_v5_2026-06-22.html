01

Executive snapshot

為什麼是現在

A finished AI accelerator is worth tens of thousands of dollars and a packaged HBM stack pulls together a dozen-plus dies bonded together (Run #8). You cannot ship either until it has been tested — proven good — and increasingly burned-in (run hot under voltage to weed out early failures). Test is the last gate, it is concentrated in very few hands, and — the key 2026 fact — it gets heavier per chip every generation: more stacked dies, tighter pitches and chiplet partitioning multiply the number of test "insertions." The catch this report is honest about: the market already noticed. Every test stock is up triple digits in 2026 — except, oddly, the one with the deepest moat.

EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

Imagine a factory that makes very expensive watches. Before any watch ships, it goes through a special, very expensive testing machine that checks it works, then sits in a hot room for hours to catch the ones that will break early (that's burn-in). For AI chips, almost the whole world's testing runs through two companies' machines — and one of them, Advantest, makes more than half. As chips get more complicated (stacking memory in towers, splitting a chip into "chiplets"), each one needs more testing time — so the testers get busier even if the number of chips doesn't grow much. That's the hidden engine. The honest catch: investors already figured this out — the small testing stocks have tripled or quadrupled in a year. The strange part, and this report's main idea: the company with the strongest grip (Advantest) went up the least. So the question isn't "is testing a good business" (it is) — it's "what's left to earn, and is anything still early?"

03

The test layer, component by component

測試元件 · 圖解

Five pieces of the back-end test stack. The chokepoint is the ATE platform (the tester itself) plus the consumable interface (probe cards, sockets) it can't run without. Note how every device — logic, memory, 3D — must pass through several of these.

Probe card CONSUMABLE

Custom needle-array (MEMS) that contacts each die at wafer sort. Re-designed per chip, wears out, recurring razor revenue. Scales with insertions + die count.

Leaders: FormFactor, Technoprobe, MJC

ATE platform CHOKEPOINT

The tester. SoC + memory ATE drives signals into the chip and reads them back, many parts in parallel. Long lead-times; software + parallelism = the moat. The gate everything passes.

Duopoly: Advantest, Teradyne

Burn-in FASTEST-GROWING

Stress the part hot, under voltage, for hours to catch "infant-mortality" defects before they fail in a $40k accelerator. Moving "left" (to wafer level) for AI/HBM. Lumpy, small base.

WLBI/PLBI: Aehr; sockets; in-house

Test socket LATENT

The reusable contact between a packaged part and the tester. HBM4 sockets must handle high-GHz-class signals (>24GHz target, est.) and >95°C. Consumable, scales with insertions, still priced like a commodity.

ISC, Leeno, Yamaichi, Smiths

SLT RISING

Run the finished chip in a realistic system to catch what structured tests miss. Increasingly required for complex AI parts — but hyperscalers/memory makers can build it in-house (the disintermediation watch).

Cohu, Advantest; SK Hynix in-house

05

Where it bites — by device domain

在哪裡卡最緊

Three device domains, three test-intensity profiles. The gate binds hardest in memory/HBM (KGD on every die in a tall stack) and 3D/chiplet (insertions multiply); logic/GPU drives the long high-parallelism SoC test.

Domain view — test intensity rises left → right. Memory/HBM is the tightest (KGD), 3D/chiplet the fastest-growing (insertions multiply). All three route through the same concentrated ATE gate.

08

The chokepoint & the variant view

卡點與變異認知

The high-parallelism ATE test gate (+ its consumable interface)

Nothing ships until it's proven good. Every AI GPU and every HBM stack passes through an ATE tester, and the high-end SoC + memory ATE that AI parts require is a duopoly — Advantest (~58% of ATE, >60% of HBM test) plus Teradyne ≈ 80% combined. The moat is decades of test-program software, parallelism and customer co-development; lead-times are tight enough that Advantest is ramping capacity from 3,000 → 5,000 systems in 2026 and toward 10,000 by 2028. The decisive twist for an investor: the gate is real but the market already bid the whole test complex up — and, strangely, the gate itself (Advantest, +55–66% YTD) ran the LEAST while the smaller, more cyclical links (burn-in +474%, handlers +200%, probe +174–210%) re-rated far more. Tightness is acute; the buyable edge is the inversion, not the theme.

The variant perception — a two-part disaggregation 變異認知

Consensus: "AI = buy GPUs and HBM" (and lately, "buy the test names too"). The variant has two layers, and the schematic shows the first — why test grows even in a flat-unit year:

① Second-derivative (the structural part)

The Street models test as a cyclical equipment line tied to wafer-fab spending and chip units. The variant: test scales with complexity — every HBM die, every 3D bond, every chiplet partition adds an insertion, so test $/chip rises each generation. That makes the test gate a structurally-compounding tollgate, not just a cyclical capex play. Bullish the aggregate test layer. (Honest caveat: this is now broadly accepted directionally — the genuinely off-consensus piece is the inversion in ②.)

② The inversion (the real edge)

Within the run-up complex, the scarcest, highest-margin, rent-capturing link — the ATE gate (Advantest, +55–66%) — ran the LEAST, while smaller/cyclical links (burn-in AEHR +474%, handlers COHU +200%, probe TPRO +210% / FORM +174%) ran 2–9×. So "buy the test theme" via the hot small-caps is buying the most-priced, least-moated links. How it's wrong: if DFT / on-die BIST / in-house SLT (SK Hynix; TSMC base-die collaboration) compress external test intensity, or a capex pause hits the cycle, the whole layer de-rates — and Advantest's "cheapness" may be a slower memory-mix + Japan/FX discount, not hidden value. The honest counter-tell: Teradyne — the other half of the same ATE duopoly — ran +127%, more than 2× Advantest, so the lag is specific to Advantest (Japan/FX + heavier memory-test cyclicality + a US-listing premium on TER), not proof the gate as a category is cheap.

Rent-capture test (margins settle it): ATE gate (Advantest, record ~47% op margin = rent captured) → probe-card razor (FormFactor, GM ~49%, recurring/custom) → burn-in (Aehr, only just back to non-GAAP profit, lumpy — rent unproven) → handlers/contactors (Cohu, commodity-ish, cyclical). Confirming tell: the link with the deepest moat and the best margins is also the one that ran least — the market is paying up for the cyclical edges and under-paying the gate.

How to be early next time 如何提前布局

1 · Watch the architecture shift, not the headline

Test intensity per chip was visible in HBM4/3D roadmaps 18–24 months before the test stocks moved. The next leg (sockets, SLT) is visible the same way today.

2 · Go one layer below the equipment name

When AEHR and FORM ran, the sockets, contactors and MEMS pins they consume stayed cheap. That's the un-bid razor-blade layer.

3 · Distrust the small-cap after a 3–5× move

AEHR at ~70× sales on ~$48M revenue is a momentum/binary trade, not an early entry. Own the gate (Advantest) for compounding, not the re-rating.

4 · The inversion is the signal

When the best business (the gate) lags the cyclical edges, that lag is the relative-value tell — buy moat that the crowd skipped, not momentum it already paid for.
