01

Executive call

the binding link · 卡點在哪一層

Explain it simply · 白話版

An AI accelerator board is etched out of a raw sheet called copper-clad laminate (CCL). That sheet is three stacked materials pressed together: a resin glue, a woven glass cloth skeleton, and a thin sheet of copper foil. At today's signalling speed (224 Gb/s per lane, soon 448G), the signal bleeds away as heat unless all three materials are near-perfect — ultra-low "loss" resin, ultra-flat low-dielectric glass, and ultra-smooth copper.

The question this report answers: of those three materials plus the company that blends them, which one is the real bottleneck? Answer: the two upstream materials — the special low-Dk glass cloth (made essentially by one Japanese firm, Nittobo) and the HVLP copper foil (led by one Japanese firm, Mitsui, where each grade upgrade roughly halves the available supply). The companies that blend the CCL (Elite, TUC, Shengyi, Doosan) are an oligopoly being actively multi-sourced by NVIDIA — so the durable scarcity rent lives one layer below them.

The catch (the honest part): the insight is right but the obvious expressions already exploded. Nittobo is ~+465% over a year and near an all-time high; Mitsui +147% YTD; Elite, TUC and Shengyi are all +100% to +800% off their lows. The genuinely-early dollar is no longer the monopolist — it's the second-source qualification race (Co-Tech in HVLP4 foil; Asahi Kasei / AGC / Taiwan Glass / Nan Ya in low-Dk glass; Doosan in M9 CCL).

Resin (L-upstream)
SABIC PPE / SA9000. ~70% from one Jubail plant (est.). Acute but event-driven; rent migrates downstream.
Glass cloth (BINDING)
Nittobo ~90% T-glass. Sold out to mid-2027. Hardest to replicate (melt+yarn+weave). The primary choke.
HVLP foil (CO-BINDING)
Mitsui ~60% HVLP2+, ~80% HVLP5. Each grade halves capacity. 28-39% structural shortfall.
CCL formulation
Elite #1, TUC #3, Doosan rising. Owns the qualified recipe + 12-18mo NVIDIA co-qual — but multi-sourced.
Dk (dielectric constant) — how much a material slows/bends a signal. Lower = faster, cleaner. E-glass ~6.7; low-Dk glass ~4.4; Q-glass ~3.9.
Df (dissipation factor) — how much signal bleeds away as heat. Lower = better. Megtron-6 ~0.003; M8 ~0.0015; M9 ≤0.0007. FR-4 is ~10× worse than M8.
HVLP foil — Hyper-Very-Low-Profile copper foil; ultra-smooth (Rz <1µm) so the high-frequency current doesn't scatter on a rough surface (skin effect).
M6/M7/M8/M9 — NVIDIA's private "loss-class" ladder for CCL, keyed to Df. M8 = 224G, M9 = quartz-class for 448G/Rubin.
T-glass / Q-glass — low-CTE / low-Dk specialty glasses. T-glass for chip substrates; Q-glass (quartz) for the lowest-loss boards.
Co-qualification — the 12-18 month process by which NVIDIA/ODMs certify one specific material recipe on one specific board. Once won, locked for a platform generation.

Variant perception — where the consensus trade is mis-located (a refinement, not a contradiction)

Consensus (correct, now priced): "AI CCL is a tight oligopoly — buy Elite / TUC / Shengyi." That trade has run +100-800%.

Our variant (the disaggregation): the scarcity rent does not peak at the CCL maker — it peaks one layer below, in the low-Dk glass cloth and HVLP foil, because the CCL maker is a price-taking converter (its own bill of materials is 42% copper foil / 26% resin / 19% glass / 13% other — so ~87% of its cost is passed-through scarce input) whom NVIDIA is actively multi-sourcing. The monopoly is upstream; the laminator is the squeezed middle.

Falsification — if we're wrong, it's most likely because: the second-source wave (Asahi Kasei/AGC glass, Co-Tech foil, multiple M10 CCL qualifiers) arrives faster than the multi-quarter qualification cycle implies, collapsing the upstream rent into 2027 before the obvious incumbents de-rate — OR the whole AI-board build-rate air-pockets, in which case the most-loved (Elite/TUC) fall hardest.

04

The material-grade ladder for AI speed tiers

grade → Df/Dk → glass → foil → board

Each SerDes step demands a lower-loss CCL grade, which simultaneously pulls a lower-Dk glass, a smoother foil, and a tighter resin. This is why the bottleneck compounds: one speed step tightens all four links at once, and the upstream two have the fewest suppliers.

Sources: queenems M9 guide; nextpcb / PCBSync Megtron; Megtron 8 datasheet (Df 0.0012 @10GHz; press ~0.0015); SemiAnalysis Vera Rubin (boards/layers); datahorizzon (qual cycle, cost ladder). Df values are frequency-dependent — treat single-decimal precision as approximate.

05

The four links beneath the chip — drawn, not listed

resin · glass · foil · CCL

① Low-loss resin — SABIC PPE / SA9000

The PPE/PPO oligomer that gives the laminate its low Df. ~70% of high-purity PPE comes from a single SABIC Jubail facility (est.); an April-2026 outage cascaded a ~40% PCB price spike. But changing resin forces a full CCL re-qualification, and the durable rent migrates to the CCL formulator, not the molecule. No clean listed pure-play (MGC 4182.T diluted, Resonac 4004.T also a CCL maker).

② Low-Dk glass cloth — Nittobo (BINDING)

Woven low-Dk / T-glass / Q-glass, spread flat to remove "weave skew." Nittobo ~90% T-glass, >80% low-Dk, ~60-70% NER. Sold out to mid-2027; reportedly courted directly by NVIDIA/MS/Google/Amazon/Apple (reported). The hard, IP-guarded step is the melt-chemistry + ultra-fine yarn drawing — Nittobo outsources only the weaving (to Nan Ya, 20% by 2027). The deepest moat in the stack.

③ HVLP copper foil — Mitsui (CO-BINDING)

Ultra-smooth electrodeposited foil (Rz <1µm) so high-frequency current doesn't scatter (skin effect). Mitsui ~60% HVLP2+, ~80% HVLP5. The killer mechanism: each grade upgrade roughly halves available capacity, so tightness escalates with every speed step. Goldman models a 28-39% structural shortfall 2026-28; NVIDIA is intervening directly to secure HVLP4. Co-Tech (8358.TWO) is the one certified second source.

④ CCL formulation — Elite / TUC / Doosan

Blends resin + glass + foil into a qualified sheet and wins the 12-18mo NVIDIA co-qual. Elite #1 (~22%), TUC #3 (~16%) in high-speed CCL; Panasonic displaced; Doosan won the M9/GB300 slot EMC reportedly failed [SS]. The co-qualification IS a genuine, durable moat — like a foundry that passes through wafers but still earns rent on the qualified step, the laminator's expanding margins (Elite GM 27.9%→29.8%) prove it captures real qualification rent. The honest read is the rent is shared: upstream owns the scarcity rent, the laminator owns the qualification rent. We tilt to upstream because (a) the single-source monopoly is there and (b) NVIDIA broadened M10 to multiple vendors to dilute the laminators specifically — but this is a refinement of consensus, not a claim the CCL maker earns nothing.

10

The central counter-argument — three disaggregations

how the obvious trade misreads it

① The rent is below the laminator, not at it

The whole Street is long Elite/TUC/Shengyi. But their cost is 42% foil / 26% resin / 19% glass — a converter's economics. The defensible monopoly is Nittobo glass and Mitsui foil. The laminators have real (expanding) margins, but they are the squeezed middle, not the toll-keeper.

② The customer is breaking the CCL monopoly

The single most important bear fact: EMC reportedly failed NVIDIA's GB300 M9 cert → Doosan took an exclusive slot; for M10 NVIDIA broadened qualification to multiple (1 TW + 2 CN) suppliers to "break single-source dependency" (Kuo, [SS]). So the CCL-maker pricing premium is being actively diluted — another reason the durable rent is upstream of them.

③ The copper-clock cuts cables, not boards

When 448G scale-up eventually goes optical (Feynman, 2028+), the loser is the active-copper-cable vendor (Amphenol-type), not CCL: optical engines still need more, denser, higher-layer boards. Don't conflate the CCL thesis with the copper-cable thesis. CPO is a 2028-2029 switch-CCL risk, not a near-term compute-tray risk (SemiAnalysis Jun-2026).

How it could be wrong

Second-sources arrive faster than the 12-18mo qual cycle implies (Asahi Kasei/AGC glass, Co-Tech foil, M10 multi-vendor CCL), collapsing the upstream rent into 2027 before the priced incumbents de-rate. The sharpest version is China domestic substitution: Shengyi/SYTECH in CCL, Chinese OPE resin (Shengquan/Dongcai), CN copper-foil and glass entrants, and NVIDIA's own M10 design (reportedly 1 TW + 2 CN qualifiers [SS]) imply a China supply wave that has historically broken Japanese material monopolies fast once qualified — the top risk to a JP-monopoly thesis. Or the AI-board build-rate air-pockets — the most-loved (Elite/TUC, +100-800%) fall hardest. And the "shortage" is partly cyclical pricing the monopolists are themselves expanding into (Nittobo 3×, ¥120B capex).

Why these are the "be-early" set

The monopolists (Nittobo, Mitsui) and laminators (Elite, TUC, Shengyi) have already run +100-800%; their remaining asymmetry is execution-on-expansion, not discovery. The earlier dollar sits in the qualification race — the names that win a second-source slot capture the next content step at a fraction of the incumbents' valuation. The risk is symmetric: a failed qualification or a faster-than-expected supply digestion. None is a clean undiscovered secret — they are earlier on the same wave, which is the honest state of a theme already in recognition.
