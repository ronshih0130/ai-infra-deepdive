01

Executive snapshot

為什麼是現在

An NVIDIA GB200 NVL72 rack wires 72 GPUs into one giant GPU with a copper "spine" — 5,000+ copper cables, 2 miles of copper, all blind-mating into a backplane. Copper (not optics) because it saves ~20 kW/rack and is more reliable for short hops. As lane rates hit 224G, passive copper reach shrinks to ~1 m, so the burden shifts onto the connector + cable assembly — and, when reach runs out, the active electrical cable (AEC). That physical link is the un-covered chokepoint of networking, and one company quietly custom-built NVIDIA's spine: Amphenol. The question this report answers: who owns the connector toll, and is it still cheap (early) or already priced (late)?

EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

Picture an AI rack as 72 people in a room who must all shout to everyone at once. NVIDIA wires them together with copper — over 5,000 copper cables and 2 miles of copper in a single rack — because copper is cheaper, cooler and more reliable than light for short hops. The hard part isn't the wire; it's the connector and cable assembly that can carry a 224-billion-bits-per-second signal without it falling apart. As speeds rise, plain copper can only reach about one metre, so you need active cables (with a tiny chip inside) and ever-more-precise connectors. One company quietly makes the custom "spine" that holds NVIDIA's rack together — Amphenol. The chokepoint is the high-speed connector + cable. The investing question: who owns it, and is the shortage still a secret (early) or already in the share price (late)?

03

The interconnect components, illustrated

互連元件

The four physical building blocks of scale-up copper. The connector (★) is the scarce, architecture-agnostic link — it is needed whether the topology is a cable harness or a midplane, and whether the medium is copper or (later) optical.

Passive DAC TODAY

A plain copper cable with a connector on each end — no electronics. The default scale-up link inside the rack: zero added power, lowest cost, most reliable. The catch: at 224G it only reaches ~1 m.

Makers: Amphenol, TE, Luxshare, Molex, Bizlink

Active AEC REACH-EXTENDER

A copper cable with a tiny retimer/DSP chip in each connector head that rebuilds the signal — extending reach to ~7 m. The link that keeps copper alive as 224G shrinks passive reach. Where the cable becomes part-semiconductor.

Pure-play: Credo (AEC); also Marvell, Astera (Taurus)

Connector ★ CHOKEPOINT

The precision metal+polymer interface where two boards or a cable and a board meet. At 224G the contact geometry, plating and crosstalk control are brutally hard — this is the scarce, hard-to-replicate link, and it survives every architecture shift.

Leader: Amphenol (Paladin B2B); TE, Luxshare, Lotes

NVLink spine RACK ASSEMBLY

The blind-mate cartridge bundling thousands of copper cables into the backplane that hard-wires all 72 GPUs together. Amphenol custom-engineered it for NVL72. At Rubin it becomes a PCB midplane (Kyber) with board-to-board connectors.

Custom-built by Amphenol; rack OEMs assemble

04

From connector to rack — and the architecture shift

元件如何組成機櫃

The same connector ends up in two very different rack architectures. GB200/300 NVL72 uses a cable harness (5,000+ discrete copper cables). Rubin "Kyber" moves to a PCB midplane with board-to-board connectors. The cable-harness content peaks and partly fades — but the connector persists across the shift. That is the crux of the whole thesis.

Assembly + disaggregation view — the connector (left) is common to both architectures; what changes is whether it terminates a cable (A) or bridges two boards (B). The connector type survives the shift — but at Rubin, NVIDIA may move the Kyber midplane connector to Foxconn Interconnect (FIT), so "Amphenol keeps it" is a live debate, not a given (see §8, §11).

05

Where it runs — scale-up / out / across

部署拓樸

The three scale domains as physical topologies. Reach grows left-to-right by ~3 orders of magnitude — and the medium flips from copper (cheap, short) to optics (expensive, long). The chokepoint lives in the leftmost box.

Topology view — same fabric, three radii. Copper dominates the dense, short scale-up box (the chokepoint); optics owns the long links. As 224G shrinks copper reach and NVL576 arrives, the copper/optics boundary creeps leftward — the slow-burn disintermediation.

08

The chokepoint — and the variant

卡點與變異認知

The signal-integrity-grade high-speed connector (+ its cable assembly)

The physical link every scale-up bit passes through after it leaves the SerDes. At 224G the mated-contact geometry, plating and crosstalk control are brutally hard, and the connector type is needed whether the rack is cabled (NVL72) or a midplane (Rubin Kyber). Amphenol custom-engineered NVIDIA's NVLink spine cartridge and is qualified on the Paladin board-to-board connector — but the franchise is contested at Rubin: GF Securities (Mar-2026) reported NVIDIA may make Foxconn Interconnect (FIT) the major Kyber midplane connector supplier, a live risk to the "architecture-agnostic toll" framing (partly reflected in APH's Jan-2026 "cable-free Rubin" slide). It is an oligopoly (Amphenol leads; TE #2; Luxshare/FIT/Lotes/Bizlink), not a monopoly — so the link earns volume + content-growth rent, not a single-supplier toll. Severity is high but capped at 14/20: multi-sourced, copper has an optics-substitution sunset on the longest runs, and connectors scale faster than fabs.

VARIANT PERCEPTION vs CONSENSUS · 變異認知

Consensus trades "AI scale-up networking" as a silicon story — buy NVIDIA / Broadcom (the switch) or the hot AEC pure-play (Credo, Marvell). Variant: the broadest, most architecture-durable scarcity rent sits in the passive physical interconnect — the high-speed connector + cable assembly — and it hides inside a diversified industrial connector conglomerate (Amphenol), priced as an "industrial," not a "semiconductor." (The same "hides-in-a-food-company" pattern as Run #6's Ajinomoto — here it hides in an industrial conglomerate.)

The disaggregation (two parts): (1) copper bifurcates and content migrates — passive DAC → active AEC (Credo) as 224G shrinks reach → and at the Rubin Kyber midplane the content moves from cable assembly to board-to-board connector. The "copper is a one-generation NVL72 trade" bear half-misreads this: the connector type persists across the cable→midplane shift, so connector demand is durable even as discrete cable-harness content peaks — but who captures it at Rubin is contested (the Foxconn/FIT threat below). The nuance cuts both ways. (2) Optics is the long-run disintermediation but partial and later — CPO scale-up is first used between racks at NVL576 (2027+), while within-rack scale-up stays copper through Rubin Ultra.

How it could be wrong: (a) Amphenol's AI slice is diluted by mobile/auto/defense/industrial → not a clean AI bet, and the dilution that under-prices it also caps the upside; (b) copper→optics could arrive faster, compressing copper content; (c) the connector is multi-sourced → volume rent, not a monopoly toll (severity capped at 14); (d) Credo's customer concentration (top-2 ~70%) is binary; (e) NVL72's 5,000-cable content is partly one-time as Rubin folds cables into a midplane; (f) the biggest single-name risk: NVIDIA may second-source the Rubin Kyber midplane connector to Foxconn Interconnect (FIT) (GF Securities, Mar-2026) — directly threatening the "Amphenol keeps the connector" pillar at the Rubin transition.

Who captures the rent? · margins settle it

The connector owner (Amphenol) earns architecture-agnostic volume + content-growth rent — adj operating margin ~27.3% with a 1.24 book-to-bill = pricing power, rent being captured, yet still only ~30× forward. The AEC pure-play (Credo) earns the highest growth (+205.7% FY26 revenue) but it is customer-funded and concentrated, at ~36× sales = the rent is fully priced. The #2 generalist (TE) earns real AI growth (DDN +70% YoY) but diluted into an ~$18.7B auto/industrial base → cheap (~19× fwd) but not a clean AI bet. Net: the connector owner captures the most durable rent; the cleanest growth is the most priced; the cheapest exposure is the most diluted.

Are we early? The awareness curve

現在是早期嗎

The whole game is owning a chokepoint before the crowd prices it — the way the 2018 MLCC and 2021 CPU/substrate shortages showed up in lead-times and order books weeks before earnings moved the stocks. The good news this run: unlike recent layers where everything had already run, networking interconnect has a genuine spread — a euphoric pure-play, a relative-value conglomerate, and a real laggard down on the year.

Horizontal position = awareness stage (qualitative), not a linear return scale. Dot height = how much of each story is already in the price as of 26 Jun 2026 — TEL least (and diluted), CRDO most. APH is the relative-value middle: it has run, but its AI depth is under-priced inside a conglomerate.

How to be early next time 如何提前布局

1 · Watch the order book, not the headline

Book-to-bill, capex guides and lead-times move weeks-to-quarters before earnings. Amphenol's 1.24 book-to-bill is the kind of physical tell that led the CPU/MLCC moves.

2 · Look where AI hides inside a generalist

The biggest under-pricing is a real AI chokepoint buried in a diversified industrial (APH, TE) — the market discounts it as "cyclical industrial," not "AI." That gap is the relative-value edge.

3 · Sell the recognition, not the news

Once a pure-play has tripled and upgrades flood in, the asymmetry is gone. CRDO (+86%) and MRVL (+231%) are past that — own them for compounding, not a re-rating.

4 · Ride the link that survives the architecture shift

The connector wins whether the rack is cabled or a midplane, copper or (later) optical. A name that wins regardless of the topology war carries less single-bet risk — that's where the durable rent sits.
