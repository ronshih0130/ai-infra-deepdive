01

Executive snapshot

為什麼是現在

Every AI accelerator is now a stack of dies — logic on logic, memory on logic — and the joints between them used to be tiny solder balls (microbumps). At the leading edge those balls are too big and too hot, so the dies are now bonded directly, copper-to-copper, with no bumps at all. That is hybrid bonding, and the hardest version — die-to-wafer (D2W), used by TSMC's SoIC for NVIDIA Rubin/Feynman — needs sub-100-nanometre alignment that only a handful of tools on Earth can hit. Run #2 priced the package (CoWoS); this report asks whether the bonding step underneath it still hides asymmetry.

EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

Imagine building a skyscraper out of chips by stacking floors on top of each other instead of laying them side by side. The old way glued the floors together with thousands of tiny solder balls; the new way welds the copper wiring of one floor straight onto the next — no glue, no balls — so signals travel a shorter distance and run cooler. That weld is hybrid bonding. To weld a small chip ("die") onto a big wafer perfectly, the machine has to line up the two within a fraction of a thousandth of a hair's width — and only a couple of companies build a machine that good (BESI, with Applied Materials; ASMPT second). Run #2 said "CoWoS packaging is the bottleneck." This report goes one floor down: the bottleneck under the package is the welding machine — and the camera that checks every weld. The investing question: now that the package is priced, is there money left in the bonder-and-inspection layer below it — and which name is still early?

03

The bonding layer, illustrated

鍵合技術

The four pieces of the joining layer that turn separate dies into a 3D stack. The first — die-to-wafer hybrid bonding — is where the scarcity rent concentrates, because it owns the hardest, highest-precision step the leading edge can't skip.

D2W hybrid bond CHOKEPOINT

Places a small die onto a full wafer and fuses copper-to-copper directly — no solder. Sub-100nm overlay, oxide-to-oxide seal. The volume mode for logic 3D SoIC today.

Tool makers: BESI+Applied (Kinex), ASMPT

W2W hybrid bond EASIER MODE

Bonds two whole wafers at once — higher throughput, but both wafers must be the same size and high-yield. Used in CMOS image sensors and some memory; less flexible for mixed die.

Leaders: EVG, SUSS (XBC300), BESI

Bond inspection UNDER-PRICED

A bumpless bond is buried and un-reworkable, so every bond is inspected for voids, overlay error and interface quality. Volume scales 1:1 with bonds — the picks-and-shovels of the bonders.

Stack: Camtek (Hawk), Onto (Dragonfly G5), KLA

TC bonding INCUMBENT

Thermal-compression bonding presses microbumped dies together with heat and force. Still the HBM4 workhorse (12→16 layer); hybrid bonding only displaces it at HBM4E (~2027+).

Leaders: ASMPT, Hanmi Semiconductor

08

The chokepoint — and how tight

卡點與嚴重度

Die-to-wafer copper-to-copper hybrid bonding

You cannot build a leading-edge 3D accelerator without fusing the dies, and the volume way to do it is die-to-wafer hybrid bonding — a bumpless copper-to-copper bond at sub-100nm overlay that only BESI (with Applied Materials' Kinex tool) and ASMPT can do at the ultra-high end, with BESI the precision leader. The recipe is years of accumulated yield know-how, and the bond is un-reworkable — one bad joint scraps the whole stack. On top of base demand, NVIDIA's Rubin/Feynman move logic into SoIC, and SoIC carries far higher equipment intensity (~$6.8–7B per 10k wafers, est.) than CoWoS — a fresh, capital-heavy tool cycle. The catch for investors: the constraint is real, but BESI has already re-rated (+137% YTD to a 52-week high), so the edge now is narrow and specific.

Variant perception · the disaggregation that is the edge 變異認知

CONSENSUS BELIEVES

"Hybrid bonding" is the next HBM memory story — buy the bonder makers because HBM4 is moving to bumpless bonding now, and that's where the volume is.

THIS REPORT'S VARIANT

The timing is mis-priced. HBM4 stays on TCB (microbumps) — hybrid bonding in memory only arrives at HBM4E in ~2027. Today's hybrid-bond volume is logic SoIC at TSMC (BESI+Applied Kinex). So: the near-term HBM rent is TCB (ASMPT/Hanmi), the near-term hybrid rent is logic (BESI), and the one link that grows either way is the inspection gate (Camtek) — which has run the least.

How it could be wrong: HBM4E hybrid-bonding pulls forward into 2026 (bullish BESI sooner, bearish the "TCB bridge" thesis); BESI's +104% order surge proves lumpy/pull-forward; ASMPT closes the precision gap and the duopoly compresses pricing; or the SoIC ramp stalls if TSMC's 130k-CoWoS goalpost slips, taking the 3D-stacking volume with it. A 2027–28 packaging-capacity overshoot would hit the toolmakers' order book first.

RENT-CAPTURE TEST · who keeps the scarcity rent? (margins settle it)

Rank by gross margin: device > bonder ≈ inspection > OSAT. The GPU maker (Nvidia/TSMC) captures the package-pricing rent — but it's already priced (Run #2). The bonder (BESI, GM ~64–66% guided into Q2-26) earns a genuine "sell-the-picks" rent — but trades at a full multiple after +137% YTD. The inspection vendor (Camtek, GM ~50%) earns a healthy, method-agnostic rent at a lower multiple and a smaller move (+82% YTD). So the scarcest link (the bonder) keeps strong rent but is fully-valued; the best-priced rent today sits one box over at inspection.

How to be early next time 如何提前布局

1 · Watch the order book, not the revenue line

In capital equipment, orders lead revenue by 2–4 quarters. BESI's hybrid-bond orders doubled while reported revenue still lags — that gap is the alpha window.

2 · Go one layer below the obvious chokepoint

Run #2 said "CoWoS." Below the package, the dies are bonded by a tool, checked by an inspection system. The deeper you go, the less the crowd has modelled it (inspection is barely tied to the hybrid-bond story).

3 · Separate the timing legs

"Hybrid bonding" is two stories on different clocks: logic now, memory at HBM4E. Price the legs separately and you avoid paying a memory multiple for a logic cycle.

4 · Respect that "tight" ≠ "cheap"

This whole layer is tight — and the bonder is fully-valued. Tightness tells you the business is good; only price and the order-inflection tell you whether you're early.
