01

Executive snapshot

為什麼是現在

A finished optical link is not just a laser. The laser chip, the silicon-photonics chip and the hair-thin glass fibre have to be glued together to sub-micron precision — and that assembly step is the slow, low-yield, hard-to-automate part. For a plug-in transceiver it is one small module you can throw away if it fails. For co-packaged optics (CPO) — optics built onto the switch chip — it explodes to ~1,000+ fibre attaches onto a single, non-repairable multi-chip package. One bad joint scraps a package worth dozens of plug-ins. On 9-Jun-2026 an analyst note arguing CPO would slip on exactly this yield problem knocked the whole optics complex down double digits — and it did so again in the 2-Jul session this report is priced from. That selloff is the setup.

EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

Making the laser is hard (earlier reports covered that). But an even trickier job is attaching the tiny glass fibre to the chip so almost no light leaks. It has to line up to a fraction of a hair's width, and a machine can only do a few per minute. As datacentres move optics right next to the switch chip (CPO), the number of these delicate joints per part jumps from a handful to over a thousand — and if any one is bad, the whole expensive part is scrap. So the new bottleneck isn't the laser, it's the assembly line that puts everything together. This report asks: who owns that assembly line, and is the shortage already in the share price? The honest answer — the one name that wins whether CPO speeds up or slows down is Fabrinet, and it's the cheapest of the group.

This report's chokepoint is not a component (L3) or a module (L2) — it is the packaging/assembly step that turns L3 into L2, the cell marked in red below. 03

What's actually inside — the assembly gallery

元件圖解

Six steps turn loose parts into a working optical engine. The scarce know-how sits in steps 3–4 (align + attach) and step 5 (test-good). Each little picture is one step; who owns it is underneath.

1 · Component die L3

EML/CW laser, photonic IC (PIC), monitor photodiode — the light-makers from Runs #1/#7/#13.
Who: Coherent, Lumentum, TSMC (SiPh), InP epi foundries

2 · Active alignment choke

Move the fibre live in 6 axes until light peaks — minutes per package, sub-micron. The throughput wall.
Tools: ficonTEC, PI, Aerotech (all private/CN); MKS Newport proxy

3 · Fibre-attach choke

Epoxy the fibre-array to the chip edge/grating without shifting alignment. 1,000+ of these per CPO system.
Who: Fabrinet, TSMC/OSAT, SENKO/US Conec/Corning (connectors)

4 · Test / known-good co-choke

Optical wafer/engine test. Several sources call this the real CPO bottleneck, co-equal with fibre-attach.
Who: Advantest, FormFactor, MPI; TSMC in-line

5 · Optical engine / module L2

The finished light part. Pluggable (discardable) or a CPO engine soldered to the switch.
Who: Fabrinet, Innolight, Coherent, Lumentum

6 · CPO switch package L1/L2

Optics fused onto the switch. Nvidia Quantum-X / Broadcom Bailly. The extreme end of the assembly problem.
Who: TSMC COUPE, Broadcom, Nvidia + partners

The one-sentence read of the gallery

Steps 1, 5 and 6 are where the stock market looks (lasers, modules, switches). Steps 2–4 are where the cost and the yield actually sit — align, attach, test — and they are owned by outsourced assemblers (Fabrinet), private tool-makers (ficonTEC/PI/Aerotech) and the foundry (TSMC). The rent hides in the step nobody quotes.

08

The chokepoint, scored

卡點評分 · severity /20

Photonic packaging & fibre-attach / active alignment

The step that marries laser + PIC + fibre into a working engine is the slow, low-yield, hard-to-automate part of optics — ~70–80% of silicon-photonics module manufacturing cost (vs ~20% for electronic ICs). It scales brutally into CPO: a plug-in has ~16 discardable fibres; a CPO switch package has 250–1,000+ fibres on one non-repairable assembly. At OFC 2026 TSMC itself named wafer-level test, FAU integration and high-speed optical packaging assembly as the three constraints gating CPO — not the photonic chip. Severity is high but not monopoly-tight: it is a capability + capacity gate (several qualified assemblers, OEMs can in-source, test is a co-bottleneck), so it scores 16/20, not 18–19.

VARIANT / DISAGGREGATION · the actual edge

Consensus, post-9-Jun, is fighting over CPO timing — SemiAnalysis says packaging yield slips it to 2028–29; Nvidia/Broadcom guidance and Global Semi Research say the ramp is real in 2026–27. Both camps sold the whole optics complex when in doubt. The disaggregation they miss: packaging difficulty is bullish the assembly-capacity owner on BOTH sides of that bet. If CPO ramps, Fabrinet does the CPO sub-assembly (named Nvidia Quantum-X partner). If CPO slips, 1.6T pluggables stay dominant longer — and Fabrinet is the #1 pluggable assembler. The market is treating the CPO-delay note as uniformly bearish optics and has left FN the laggard (+20% YTD vs KLIC +166–195%, COHR +100%) — that mispricing is the edge.

HOW THIS COULD BE WRONG · honesty

Denominator trap: the "70–80%" is module-manufacturing cost at low volume, not finished-transceiver BOM (~40% assembly per LightCounting); EPIC Photonics argues it collapses toward 20% at scale. Test not attach: Optica/IEEE call optical test the co-equal or bigger CPO bottleneck; Broadcom claims already-automated fibre-attach. In-sourcing: TSMC/Foxconn owning CPO assembly could route around the merchant assembler. FN concentration: Nvidia 27.6% + Cisco 18.2% = ~46% of revenue in two customers. This is a capability gate, not a monopoly — don't over-rent it. The branch that matters: the 16/20 is a close call (packaging 92 vs test 78) — if optical test proves the binding wall, the trade migrates to the ATE duopoly (Advantest / FormFactor), not the assembler; weight FN accordingly.

Open questions & risks

未解與風險
UNRESOLVED HOLES (honest self-critique)
  • Is packaging really "the" chokepoint, or co-equal with test? Multiple credible sources name optical test as the bigger CPO wall. This report treats them as co-binding (bars: 92 vs 78) — but if test dominates, the tool/ATE names (Advantest, FormFactor) matter more than the assembler.
  • The 70–80% figure is denominator-sensitive and volume-dependent (module mfg cost, not transceiver BOM; EPIC argues it collapses to ~20% at scale). The direction is solid; the magnitude is a low-volume artefact.
  • CPO in-sourcing. If TSMC (COUPE) + Foxconn own end-to-end CPO assembly, the merchant assembler's CPO rent is capped — FN's CPO upside could be smaller than the pluggable-durability case.
  • No clean listed pure-play for the actual tooling. ficonTEC is a restricted Chinese A-share; PI/Aerotech private; MKSI is diluted. The "buy the picks-and-shovels" instinct has no clean vehicle.
