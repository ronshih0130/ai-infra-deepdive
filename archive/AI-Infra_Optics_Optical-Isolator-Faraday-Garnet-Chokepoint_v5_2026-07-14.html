01

Executive snapshot

為什麼是現在

Every AI optical link starts with a laser, and every laser hates its own echo: a sliver of light reflected back from a fiber joint or a photonic chip can make it stutter, spike its noise, or hop modes. The fix is a one-way valve for light — the optical isolator. It's a passive so small and cheap nobody puts it on a slide, yet you need one on every reflection-sensitive laser (CW-DFB / EML / coherent / ELS) — a rising but sub-100% share of all lasers — and those laser counts are exploding with 1.6T and co-packaged optics. The scarce, hard-to-replicate part inside it is a magneto-optic garnet crystal grown by a handful of Japanese (and a few Chinese) crystal houses — and that crystal is what this report follows.

EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

Picture a laser as a singer who cannot stand hearing an echo of their own voice — even a faint one throws them off pitch. In an AI datacenter there are millions of these tiny laser "singers," and the fibers and chips around them constantly bounce faint echoes back. So next to every reflection-sensitive laser (the single-mode ones — the cheap short-reach VCSELs mostly skip it) sits a microscopic one-way door that lets the singer's voice out but blocks the echo coming back: the optical isolator. The trick that makes the door only open one way is a special magnetic crystal (a "Faraday rotator garnet") that twists light — and only a few companies in the world can grow it well. That crystal is the chokepoint. The catch, and the reason this isn't a slam-dunk: the door is cheap and several firms can assemble it, China just tightened the rare-earth supply for the crystal, and engineers are inventing lasers that don't need the door at all. So the honest question here isn't "which stock to buy" — it's "is there any clean way to own this scarce crystal, and is the crowd early or already elsewhere?"

VARIANT PERCEPTION · WHAT WE THINK THE STREET IS MISSING

Consensus is fighting over two things: CPO timing and laser (EML/InP) supply. Almost nobody is pricing the isolator specifically — it's un-bid because it's invisible and the market is tiny. But there are two off-consensus reads, and they point opposite ways. (1) Bullish, near-term: the same CPO push that everyone loves multiplies lasers, and high-power external-laser CW DFBs are more reflection-sensitive — so isolator (and garnet) demand quietly compounds, and the crystal is a Japanese near-oligopoly. (2) Bearish, longer-term (the disaggregation): that same silicon-photonics integration is racing to make lasers isolator-free (quantum-dot lasers, self-injection-locked DFBs, on-chip garnet) — so CPO is bullish light sources yet potentially bearish the discrete isolator by 2028–30. The edge is holding both at once: a real, un-bid tightness today with a real, un-hedged obsolescence tomorrow. If we're wrong, it's most likely because isolator-free designs mature faster than expected (killing the thesis early) — or because the crystal was never that scarce (several assemblers, a small market that capacity can chase).

COHR — own-the-rent, integrated isolator+garnet+laser, but priced (Recognition) TDK / Granopt — the hidden merchant garnet monopoly, but diluted to invisibility LYSDY — the rare-earth upstream, topical but a red herring for optics

Leans are the author's personal opinion for a private notebook — not a recommendation or solicitation. Tightness ≠ a good buy.

One crystal, three demand pools. The isolator is architecture-agnostic across scale-up/out/across — but it is not a monopoly toll, because several firms assemble the finished part and the crystal market is small enough that capacity can chase it. That is the honest ceiling on this thesis.

03

What the isolator actually is — illustrated

元件圖解

Four pictures. The first three build up the chokepoint (a valve → the crystal that makes it one-way → how it guards a laser). The fourth is the threat: a laser engineered to need no valve at all.

Optical isolator CHOKE

A one-way valve: light passes forward through polarizer → rotator → analyzer, but a reflected echo gets its polarization twisted a second time and is stopped at the input. <1 dB loss, >30 dB isolation.
who: Coherent, Molex/Oplink, Thorlabs, O-Net, Accelink, AC Photonics

Faraday-rotator garnet the link

Bismuth-substituted rare-earth iron garnet (Bi:RIG), a magnetic film grown by liquid-phase epitaxy. Its magnetism twists light one way only — the physics no other cheap material delivers at 1550 nm.
who: Granopt (TDK), Coherent (LPE), a few JP/CN crystal houses

Laser + isolator (ELS) where it sits

The isolator lives on the path from laser to fiber/photonic chip. Co-packaged optics pulls the CW laser off-chip (ELS) and runs it hot — the most reflection-sensitive, highest-isolation spot in the system.
who: Lumentum, Coherent, Broadcom, captive Nvidia

Isolator-free laser DISRUPTOR

Quantum-dot lasers and self-injection-locked DFBs are being designed to shrug off back-reflections with no isolator — the 2028–30 disintermediation that caps this chokepoint's asymmetry.
who: Quintessent, Nexus Photonics, Intel, Nvidia/Broadcom CPO (mostly private/captive)

Cards 1–3 are the chokepoint; card 4 is why the severity is 14/20, not 18. Hold both in your head — that tension is the whole report.

From rare-earth to running link — the whole build-up

L3→L2→L1 組裝

How a pinch of rare-earth oxide becomes a valve guarding a laser inside a switch. Value and difficulty peak at one step: growing the garnet crystal. Everything to its right is assembly the market can multi-source; everything to its left is commodity chemistry — except when China controls it.

The classic house pattern — value migrating to the narrowest point — but softer here: the needle's eye is a needle, not a monopoly. Note that the biggest dollar rent is captured one box to the right (the integrated laser house), not at the crystal itself (see §07).

05

Where the same crystal shows up across the network

部署對照

One garnet, three jobs. As you move from short-reach CPO to long-reach coherent, the isolation grade rises and the volume falls — so a crystal squeeze bites the high-volume pluggable pool first, and the high-grade coherent pool worst.

Deployment mapping — the crystal is architecture-agnostic, so isolator-free integration has to beat it in every pool before the chokepoint truly breaks (it will fall in scale-up/CPO first, coherent last).

RENT-CAPTURE TEST · 誰賺到租金

The rent settles downstream and captive, not at the crystal:

  • ▸ Coherent grows its own garnet and builds its own isolators inside its lasers — vertical integration keeps the rent in-house (GM ~40% and rising).
  • ▸ Granopt/TDK is the leading merchant supplier of latching LPE garnet (near-monopoly in that niche, not the whole isolator), but it's a rounding error inside a ¥2T-revenue components giant — a durable toll, not a needle-mover.
  • ▸ Lynas sells the rare-earths, but optics is ~0% of Tb/Dy demand — it banks magnet/defense rent, not optics rent.

Verdict: a real toll, thinly spread. Nobody gets rich purely on isolator garnet — you buy the businesses around it.

08

Severity score & the disaggregation

卡點評分與變異觀點

Chokepoint: the magneto-optic Faraday-rotator garnet crystal

Every reflection-sensitive laser transmitter needs reflection protection, and the only cheap material that provides efficient non-reciprocal Faraday rotation at telecom wavelengths is a bismuth-substituted iron garnet grown by liquid-phase epitaxy (Bi:RIG for datacom; TGG for high-power). The know-how sits with a handful of Japanese houses — Granopt (TDK) and Coherent lead — with a few Chinese entrants. It is genuinely tight and genuinely un-bid. But it is not an Ajinomoto-grade monopoly: the finished isolator is multi-sourced, the market is small enough that capacity can chase it, and isolator-free lasers threaten to delete it. Hence 14/20, not 18.

THE DISAGGREGATION · 拆解

The popular trend (silicon-photonics / CPO) is bullish optics in aggregate but points both ways on this one component. It multiplies lasers now (bullish the isolator) while racing to make those lasers isolator-free by 2028–30 (bearish the discrete isolator). So the trade is not "buy the crystal for the AI boom" — it's "the crystal is quietly tight today, quietly obsolescing tomorrow." Own it only where you're paid for the tightness without betting the farm on its permanence.

Component-interaction schematic: the same optical job done two ways. The left needs the chokepoint; the right routes around it. Whoever commercialises the right at scale first sets the clock on the left.

Awareness curve — early or already elsewhere?

認知曲線

The honest picture: all three "plays" are already in Recognition — but each on the wrong thesis (transceivers, batteries, magnets), not on the isolator. So the genuinely-early dollar isn't in the top-3 at all; it sits to the left, in the watchlist. That's rare for this rotation and worth saying plainly.

Read it as: nobody is bidding these names for the isolator. The chokepoint itself is still Latent/Early — but the only pure ways to own it (SiPh disruptors, Chinese crystal pure-plays) are private or hard-to-reach. Being "early" here means watching, not buying.

Risk register

風險
Isolator-free disintermediation (the big one)

If QD/self-injection lasers reach volume before ~2028, the discrete isolator — and its garnet — start shrinking just as you'd hope to be paid. This single risk caps the asymmetry at 6/20.

Mislocated chokepoint

The neighbouring fiber-array/photonic-packaging step (Run #19) may be the tighter, more investable bottleneck. The isolator could be a co-bottleneck, not the one.

Not a monopoly — multi-sourced

Several firms assemble finished isolators; the crystal has a few makers. Limited pricing power caps the rent even while supply is "tight."

No clean vehicle → dilution

Every listed name touches the chokepoint only through a big, unrelated P&L. You can't isolate the exposure; you buy transceivers, batteries, or magnets to get a sliver.

Rare-earth red herring

The terbium-controls story is real for the material class but weak for datacom Bi:RIG specifically (TGG/high-power is the terbium-heavy grade). Don't over-connect Lynas to optics.

China substitution

Chinese crystal + isolator makers (Accelink, CASTECH) are climbing the grade curve; they could erode the Japanese premium — bear for the JP-monopoly framing, bull for CN pure-plays.

Small-TAM trap

A $0.9–1.8B finished-isolator market simply can't move a $40–50B-cap needle. Even a perfect thesis is financially immaterial to the "plays."

Open questions — honest self-critique

未解問題
Q1 Is the terbium/rare-earth squeeze actually relevant to datacom isolators?

Only partly, and I've flagged it throughout. Datacom fiber isolators use bismuth-substituted iron garnet (Bi:RIG, LPE); terbium is central to TGG (high-power free-space), a different grade. So the Apr-2025 Tb controls squeeze the material class and high-power isolators more than datacom Bi:RIG. Over-connecting the two would be the report's easiest factual error — hence Lynas is explicitly the red-herring leg.

Q2 Is the isolator even the binding link, versus fiber-array/packaging?

Genuinely unresolved. Run #19 argued photonic packaging/fiber-attach is the tighter, more investable bottleneck. The isolator may be a co-bottleneck. I've scored it 14/20 (below the rotation's monopolies) partly to acknowledge this.

Q3 The "plays" are all sliver-exposure proxies — so why own any?

Retail steelman: you probably shouldn't own any of them for the isolator. COHR is defensible only if you like it as a transceiver/materials business anyway (the garnet is a free kicker). TDK and Lynas have no investable isolator exposure. The honest deliverable is education + a watchlist, not a buy.

Q4 How firm are the shares and counts?

Soft. Granopt/Coherent garnet share isn't disclosed ("leader" is directional); "~1 isolator/laser, 4–8/module" is an estimate that varies by design (some ELS share isolation); market size is an aggregator range ($0.9–1.8B). All marked (est.).

Q5 What single thing would most change the conclusion?

The isolator-free timeline. If it's 2027 (not 2029–30), the chokepoint is already fading and even COHR's garnet kicker is worthless; if it's 2032+, the tightness compounds and a Chinese pure-play could become a genuine early buy. The entire asymmetry hinges on a date nobody knows — which is why this is a watch, not a position.
