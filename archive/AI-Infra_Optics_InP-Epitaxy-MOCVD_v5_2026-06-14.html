01

Executive snapshot

為什麼是現在

Every AI optical link needs a laser, and lasers are not printed in silicon — they are grown on indium phosphide (InP) one crystal layer at a time. That growing step is epitaxy, and the volume tool for it is MOCVD, where one company (AIXTRON) holds an estimated 70–90% of the advanced-photonic market. The headline optics names (Lumentum, Coherent) and even the substrate (AXT) have already run hard; the question this report asks is whether the deposition layer underneath them still hides any asymmetry.

EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

Picture a tiny laser chip as a cake with 100+ ultra-thin layers, each a few atoms thick. You can't carve that cake out of ordinary silicon — silicon can't make light. You have to grow it on a special crystal wafer (indium phosphide), and the oven that grows it is called an MOCVD reactor. If one layer is off by a nanometre, the laser is junk — so the ovens are extraordinarily hard to build, and basically one company (AIXTRON) builds the good ones for photonics, with Veeco second. Run #1 said "the laser is the bottleneck." This report goes one floor down: the bottleneck under the laser is the oven and the wafer it grows on. The investing question: now that the lasers and the wafer makers have already soared, is there any money left in the oven-and-processing layer below them — and which names are still early?

03

The deposition layer, illustrated

磊晶設備

The four pieces of equipment that turn a bare wafer into a working laser chip. The first one — the MOCVD reactor — is where the scarcity rent concentrates, because it owns the hardest, highest-volume step.

MOCVD reactor CHOKEPOINT

Metal-organic chemical vapour deposition: flows precursor gases over hot, spinning wafers to grow crystal films. High throughput → the default for volume InP laser manufacturing.

Tool makers: AIXTRON (G10-AsP), Veeco (Lumina)

MBE chamber PRECISION

Molecular-beam epitaxy: fires atomic beams in a vacuum, one layer at a time. Lower throughput, higher precision — the workhorse for quantum-dot lasers, qubit stacks and next-gen oxide modulators.

Leaders: Riber (ROSIE), Veeco (GEN)

InP substrate UPSTREAM

The crystal wafer the laser is grown on. Hard to grow, few suppliers, painful to scale in size — now mid-transition from 4-inch to 6-inch, which itself triggers a new tool buy.

Crystal: Sumitomo, AXT, JX, Freiberger

Back-end stack UNDER-COVERED

An epi wafer isn't a chip yet: etch the waveguides, passivate, mirror-coat the laser facets. A near-monopoly supplier owns this multi-step stack — and almost nobody models it.

Stack: Oxford Instruments (OIPT) near-monopoly

08

The chokepoint — and how tight

卡點與嚴重度

Volume InP laser epitaxy on MOCVD

You cannot make an AI optical laser without growing it, and the volume way to grow it is MOCVD — where AIXTRON holds an estimated 70–90% of the advanced-photonic market, with Veeco the only credible second source. The recipe is years of accumulated yield know-how, not a tool you can buy and switch on. On top of base demand, the industry is mid-transition from 4-inch to 6-inch InP wafers, which forces an entirely new, automated toolset — a fresh multi-year order cycle layered on the shortage. The catch for investors: the constraint is real, but the deposition-tool stocks have already re-rated (AIXTRON +218% YTD), so the edge now is narrow and specific.

Variant perception · the disaggregation that is the edge 變異認知

CONSENSUS BELIEVES

"AI optics" means buy the modules and lasers (Coherent, Lumentum) — and the MOCVD-tool makers are EV/SiC equipment plays stuck in a power-cycle downturn, with AIXTRON revenue down 47% YoY.

THIS REPORT'S VARIANT

AIXTRON is one stock running two opposite cycles: SiC/power is down and priced, but photonics/InP is up and unpriced — opto is already ~70% of orders while reported revenue still reflects the old trough. The order book has turned before the income statement — the classic CPU/MLCC lead-indicator. The market is selling the arms-dealer on the wrong cycle.

How it could be wrong: the opto order surge proves lumpy / pull-forward; the SiC trough deepens and swamps the opto recovery; the whole industry's InP capacity race (Coherent 3×, AXT 2×2, Sumitomo +40%) overshoots into a 2028 glut, turning tool orders into an air-pocket; or China's AMEC/NAURA climb the advanced-photonic MOCVD curve faster than expected.

RENT-CAPTURE TEST · who keeps the scarcity rent? (margins settle it)

Rank by gross margin: device > tool > substrate. The laser maker (Lumentum/Coherent) captures the chip-pricing rent — but it's already priced (~90× fwd P/E). The tool maker (AIXTRON, GM ~40–42%, EBIT 17–20%) earns a "sell-the-picks" rent that is healthy but cyclical — and trades at ~2.6× revenue / ~20× EPS, a 40–70% discount to WFE peers. The substrate maker (AXT) runs single-digit margins despite the shortage — buyers dual-source and fund supply. So the scarcest physical link (substrate) keeps the least rent; the best-priced rent today sits at the tool.

How to be early next time 如何提前布局

1 · Watch the order book, not the revenue line

In capital equipment, orders lead revenue by 2–4 quarters. AIXTRON's opto orders turned up while reported sales still fell — that gap is the alpha window.

2 · Go one layer below the obvious chokepoint

Run #1 said "the laser." The laser is grown on a tool, on a wafer, finished by a back-end stack. The deeper you go, the less the crowd has modelled it (the back-end is barely covered).

3 · Disaggregate a two-cycle stock

When one business is hated (SiC) and hides a second that's loved (photonics), the market often prices the hated one. Separate the two and you find the unpriced leg.

4 · Respect that "tight" ≠ "cheap"

This whole layer is tight — and mostly re-rated. Tightness tells you the business is good; only price and the order-inflection tell you whether you're early.
