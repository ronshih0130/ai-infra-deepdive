01

Executive snapshot

為什麼是現在

"AI needs power" is the most-crowded trade in the market — everyone owns the grid, the transformer, the rack power-shelf. The hidden engine sits one layer past all of that: the last inch, where 48 volts on a busbar gets stepped down to under 1 volt and shoved into a GPU die at 1,000–2,000+ amps. At those currents the wires between the converter and the chip waste too much power, so the converter has to move directly under the die (vertical power delivery) and the chip needs a dense bank of capacitors right next to it to survive the current spikes. Two links there are genuinely scarce — the vertical-power IP (Vicor, now suing Delta over the core patent) and the high-end capacitor (an MLCC shortage is back). The catch: the market already noticed — every name in this report is up double or triple digits in 2026.

EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

Think of pushing water to a thirsty plant. The grid is the reservoir; the transformer and rack gear are the big pipes (everyone already owns those). But the last few millimetres — the nozzle that drips water onto the roots — is now the hard part. An AI GPU is like a plant that suddenly drinks a fire-hose of low-voltage power; if the nozzle is even a little too far away, you lose a chunk of the water as heat. So the industry is moving the "nozzle" (the power converter) to sit right under the chip (that's "vertical power delivery"), and packing tiny capacitors (energy buffers) right beside it to smooth the gulps. Two companies own scarce pieces of that nozzle: Vicor (the converter design — it's even suing a rival over the patent) and Murata (the best capacitors, which are sold out). The honest part: investors already figured this out — Vicor is up ~210% and Murata ~244% this year. The strange part, and this report's main idea: the biggest power-chip supplier to NVIDIA (Monolithic Power) went up the least (~59%) — because its link is the one rivals are fighting hardest to take.

LAYER KEY · read the voltages like floors of a building

GRID/800V medium-voltage grid → 800V DC (facility, the Run #3/#9 layer)  ·  48V PDB rack busbar 48V → 12V on a power-distribution board (Renesas)  ·  ~1V VRM/VPD 12V → under 1V at the die — the multiphase VRM (MPS) and the new vertical-power converter (Vicor) the chokepoint  ·  CAPS the decoupling capacitors next to the die that absorb current spikes (Murata MLCC + silicon caps) — the co-binding link

6-TERM GLOSSARY

VRM (voltage regulator module) the multiphase converter that turns 12V into the ~1V the chip uses · VPD (vertical power delivery) putting that converter directly beneath the die so current travels millimetres, not centimetres · current multiplication Vicor's trick: deliver high current at the die from a lower current upstream (less copper loss) · IR drop the power wasted as heat when big current flows through any resistance — the enemy of the last inch · MLCC multilayer ceramic capacitor — tiny energy buffers; AI servers use far more of them · IVR (integrated voltage regulator) the next paradigm — the converter built into the chip package (Empower, just bought by ADI).

The last-inch components — a picture of each

關鍵零件圖解

Five sub-technologies make up the last inch. The VRM and the vertical-power converter are the chokepoint; the capacitor is the co-binding link; the PDB feeds it; the IVR is the next paradigm. Each card draws the part, its role, and who makes it.

PDB 48→12V

The power-distribution board: one 8kW module per compute tray (two 4kW bricks, ~$250) that steps the rack's 48V down to 12V.
Renesas (GB200 exclusive) · the under-credited link

Multiphase VRM CHOKEPOINT

16–32 power "phases" in parallel turn 12V into ~1V at >1,000A with <10mV ripple — sitting beside the die today (lateral).
MPS (lead) · Infineon · Renesas · ADI — contested

Vertical power (VPD) CHOKEPOINT

The next step: the converter mounts under the die so current travels ~1.5mm. Vicor's "current multiplier" delivers up to 40× current at the die.
Vicor (FPA IP, suing Delta) · MPS/Infineon racing

Power-integrity caps CO-BIND

Dozens of decoupling capacitors hug the die to absorb sudden current swings. AI servers use far more high-cap MLCCs; silicon caps go even closer.
Murata (~40% MLCC) · Samsung-EM · Empower(→ADI)

IVR / power-on-package NEXT

The endgame: integrate the regulator + silicon caps inside the package, next to the cores. Removes the last centimetres entirely.
Empower (Crescendo) → ADI ($1.5B) · the frontier

The chokepoint is the red pair (VRM + VPD); the capacitor is amber/purple (co-binding); the PDB feeds it (blue); the IVR (green) is where the link goes next — and it's being bought up before it ships (ADI–Empower).

卡點與變異認知

The last inch — sub-1V / 2,000A delivery at the die (+ the capacitor that buffers it)

Every GPU package must be fed ~1 volt at one-to-two-thousand amps, and at that current the wires between the converter and the die waste too much power as heat — so the converter is forced under the die (vertical power delivery) and the die needs a dense capacitor bank beside it. Two links there are genuinely scarce: Vicor's non-isolated current-multiplier IP (Delta is suing to invalidate the '087 patent precisely because it is "the only viable path" to Rubin's efficiency — a tell) and the high-end capacitor (Murata's high-end MLCC lines are >90% utilised and sold out; silicon caps are concentrated). The decisive investor twist: the market already bid the whole last inch up — and, unusually, the biggest-dollar conversion link (MPS, +59% YTD) ran the LEAST, because it is the one rivals (Renesas, Infineon, ADI) are fighting hardest to take. Tightness is high; the buyable edge is the disaggregation, not the theme.

The variant perception — a two-part disaggregation 變異認知

Consensus: "AI = a power crisis → buy the power names" — and the crowd has now bid the whole last inch (Vicor +210%, Murata +244%, MPS +59%, Renesas +122%). The variant has two layers; the schematic shows the first — why "lateral" must become "vertical":

① The chain is SPLIT, not one winner

The Street treats "NVIDIA power" as one link and buys MPS as the winner. But the path is split across distinct owners: Renesas holds the 48→12V PDB (exclusive, ~$250/tray), MPS holds the 12→1V VRM (contested), and Vicor's VPD is the next-gen die-side link. "Buy MPS = NVIDIA power" misreads where each link's rent actually sits — and under-credits Renesas (mistraded as an auto/MCU name).

② The durable scarce link is IP + capacitor, not the conversion chip

Multiphase VRMs are multi-sourced and NVIDIA funds new supply (Infineon joined MGX; ADI bought Empower for $1.5B) — so that rent is competed away. The genuinely scarce, durable links are (a) Vicor's non-isolated current-multiplier IP (Delta suing to kill the '087 = the tell) and (b) the power-integrity capacitor (Murata >90% util). How it's wrong: Vicor has a long design-win→revenue slippage history, the ITC may fail (2027), and NVIDIA may dual-source VPD → a 170× P/E de-rates hard; and the MLCC "shortage" is cyclical and multi-sourced (the 2018→19 bust is the template) with Murata already +244%. Confirming tell either way: ADI paid 11× the funding for Empower — buyers are paying up to OWN the next-gen link, which is where the rent is migrating.

Rent-capture test (margins settle it): Vicor VPD-IP (GM 55.2%, backlog +70% QoQ, tiny ~$570M base) ≈ MPS VRM (biggest $, GM ~55–56%, contested) > Murata capacitor (cyclical pricing rent, +10–20% price hikes) > Renesas PDB (exclusive but a ~$250 low-ASP box, diluted). The tell: the buyer is funding new supply at every conversion link — so the durable rent sits in IP, the capacitor, and the package (IVR), not the multiphase chip.
