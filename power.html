Power Delivery — the bottleneck you can't fab your way out of

Chips you can build in months; a grid connection and a high-voltage transformer take 2–5 years. As AI racks hit 1 MW, power has become the slowest physical constraint on AI — and the rent splits between the grid gate, the facility integrator, and the contested last-inch chip power. This maps where it sits and who's still early.

EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

An AI datacenter is a giant electricity customer. Power flows downhill from the grid → through a big transformer that steps the voltage down → into the building's power gear → into the rack → and finally a tiny chip-side voltage regulator (VRM) drops it to ~0.65V for the GPU. The traffic jam is at the top: utilities and transformers take years to deliver, so you can't just plug in a new datacenter. For an investor the choice is: own the grid gate (GE Vernova), the facility builder (Vertiv), or the chip-power chip (Monolithic) — and figure out which still has upside after all three have run.

THE PATH · 分層 (read first)
Generation where power is made (utility / gas turbine) · Grid/transformer high-voltage delivery + step-down · Facility power UPS, switchgear, busway in the building · Rack power power shelf + PSU in the rack · Board/chip power VRM dropping volts for the GPU.
6-TERM GLOSSARY · 名詞
VRM voltage-regulator module (chip-side power) · PMIC power-management IC · HVDC / 800VDC high-voltage DC distribution · SST solid-state transformer · BBU battery backup unit · BYOP "bring your own power" (onsite generation).

03

The chokepoint & severity

卡點與嚴重度

High-voltage transformers & grid interconnect

The one link you cannot speed up with capex or cleverness: large power transformers and grid connections run on 2–5 year lead times, set by copper, grain-oriented electrical steel, skilled labour and utility queues. That's why hyperscalers now build onsite generation ("bring your own power"). Unlike a chip, you cannot fab your way out — which is exactly what makes it the hardest gate. The catch for investors: the gatekeepers (GE Vernova, Hitachi Energy, Siemens Energy) are huge diversified companies, so the scarcity rent is diluted.

VARIANT PERCEPTION vs CONSENSUS · 變異認知

Consensus: "AI is power-constrained — buy the power names." The disaggregation: the binding physical gate is the multi-year transformer/grid lead time (GE Vernova's turf) — but GEV's rent is diluted across turbines and services, so it's the safest, not the highest-return, exposure. The highest-margin link is the on-chip VRM (Monolithic) — but that is now the most competitively contested ("GB200 power-module war": Infineon, Renesas, Vicor, ADI, and Nvidia's incentive to dual-source). The cleanest neutral arms-dealer is the facility integrator (Vertiv, power+cooling). If we're wrong on GEV, it's that grid relief (faster permitting, modular transformers) arrives sooner than the 2–5-yr base case; if wrong on MPWR, it's that competition compresses its share/margin faster than AI volume grows.

Who captures the rent?

Margins settle it. GEV holds the scarcest link (the grid) but spreads it across turbines + services — high visibility ($163B backlog), diluted rent, lowest beta. VRT is the broad power+cooling integrator with a $15B backlog — wins on volume, mid margin. MPWR holds the fattest gross margin (last-inch power IP) but is the single most contested seat — Nvidia wants a second source. Conclusion: the scarcity and the margin sit in different names — you can't own both in one ticker.
