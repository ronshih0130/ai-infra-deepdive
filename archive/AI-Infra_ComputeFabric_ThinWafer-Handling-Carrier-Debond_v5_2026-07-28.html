4 · Bottom line (page-one honesty)

NO POSITION-GRADE CANDIDATE. Three WATCH, three HYPOTHESIS/NO VEHICLE, zero PAPER, zero ACTION — with the caveat from §1 that the data gate alone forced this.

The chain is a genuine physical chokepoint: you cannot stack 16 DRAM dies without thinning them to roughly 30µm, and you cannot move a 30µm wafer without bonding it to a carrier.C01C02C14 What makes it unlike every other chokepoint in this rotation is that the degree of difficulty is set by a package-height specification written by the customers themselves — so the scarcity rent is negotiable in a way that, say, an optical preform's physics is not.

Which way the spec is pushing is the whole question, and this run cannot answer it. A taller package can buy thicker dies (relief for the chain) or more layers at the same thickness (more thinned wafers, more work for the chain). The source that reports the relaxation says the firstC06; the same source family says the 825–900µm figure is being weighed for 20-high stacksC07, which is the second. The one precedent available — the earlier move from 720µm to 775µm — bought four extra layers at a thinner per-die targetC02C03, i.e. it went the second way. See §12 for the arithmetic and why it does not close.

The likeliest reading is not a fork at all — it is a sequence. C-05/C-06 describe ~900µm for HBM4E; C-07 describes 825–900µm for 20-high. Those are plausibly two different generations in order, not two mutually exclusive outcomes: relief first at 16 layers, then the envelope refilled with 20 layers and hybrid bonding behind it. If that is right, the chain gets a soft patch and then a step-up, and the correct posture is not "avoid" but "be ready for 2027–28". This run cannot confirm which generation each report refers to, which is precisely why §14 signal 1 asks for height and layer count together.

What is not in doubt: A16 Super Power Rail slipped to 2027C21, HBM4 is shipping on MR-MUF microbumps rather than hybrid bondingC04, and DISCO's Q1 beat came from consumables while equipment missed planC08. Those are three real facts about near-term tool demand — and they are why "wait for the fork to resolve" beats "buy the drawdown".

5 · Explain it simply / 白話版

The problem in one sentence. To fit 16 memory chips into a finished stack no taller than 775µm, each chip must be ground down to roughly 30µm — about a third the width of a human hair.C01 At that thickness a 300mm silicon wafer behaves like cling film: it curls, cracks, and cannot be picked up.

Careful with two different 775s. A raw incoming wafer is also about 775µm thick. That is a coincidence of numbers, not a relationship. One is how thick a single starting wafer is; the other is how tall the finished 16-die package is allowed to be. The whole argument in §12 is about the second one.

So the industry glues the wafer to a carrier. A thick glass or silicon disc is temporarily bonded on, the wafer is ground thin, processed on its back, then debonded — peeled or laser-released — and the glue residue washed off. Every one of those steps can destroy a wafer that already carries months of accumulated value.

Why an investor should care. The chain is unavoidable, it is supplied by a handful of Japanese and European toolmakers, and it scales with every extra layer of HBM. That is the standard bull case, and it is why these stocks ran hard into 2025.

Why this report says wait rather than buy or sell. The premium in this chain comes from difficulty, and difficulty here is set by a committee that writes the height rule. If a taller package means thicker chips, the difficulty falls and so does the premium. If it means more chips at the same thinness, the work goes up. Reports say both things. Until that is settled, you are guessing — and the last time the rule changed, it bought more layers, not thicker chips.

Layer key — where "L1/L2/L3" sit

  • Upstream: consumables and materials — grinding wheels, back-grinding and dicing tape, temporary-bond adhesives, laser-release layers, glass or silicon carrier discs.
  • L3 (sub-technology): the individual process steps — backgrind, TAIKO ring-grind, carrier bond, laser debond, residue clean, warpage metrology.
  • L2 (module): the tool that performs one step — a grinder, a temporary bonder, a debonder.
  • L1 (finished thing): the thinned die that goes into an HBM stack, a 3D-stacked logic die, or a backside-powered CPU/GPU wafer.

Glossary — the ten terms that actually appear in the triggers

  • HBM — high-bandwidth memory. DRAM chips stacked vertically and wired to a processor through the package rather than across a board. "16-Hi" means sixteen memory dies in one stack.
  • JEDEC — the industry standards body whose members (the memory makers and their customers) agree the specifications, including the maximum package height that this whole report turns on.
  • Non-die overhead — everything in an HBM package that is not core DRAM: the foundry-made base logic die, the microbumps, the underfill and the mold cap. The height left over after subtracting it is what the memory dies must fit inside.
  • OSAT — Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test. The independent packaging houses (ASE, Amkor) that do assembly work fabs do not. DISCO's "OSAT ratio" is the share of its shipments going to them, and it is one of only two named DISCO triggers below.
  • TTV — total thickness variation. How un-flat a thinned wafer is. At a 30µm target, a few µm of TTV is a scrap wafer.
  • TAIKO — a grind that leaves a thick outer ring, so a thin wafer keeps a stiff rim and stops curling.
  • TBDB — temporary bond / debond. Gluing the wafer to a carrier and getting it off again.
  • TSV reveal — grinding the wafer back until the copper through-silicon vias buried in it emerge on the far side, so the next die can connect to them.
  • CMP — chemical-mechanical planarization. Chemical-plus-abrasive polishing that makes a surface flat to atomic tolerances.
  • MR-MUF — mass-reflow molded underfill. SK hynix's way of stacking HBM with microbumps instead of hybrid bonding.C04
  • Hybrid bonding — direct copper-to-copper joining with no bumps. Thinner stacks, far harder. Reported as needed only at 20 layers or more.C05
  • BSPDN — backside power delivery. Power wires moved to the underside of the wafer; requires flipping, bonding and extreme thinning of a logic wafer. TSMC's version is "Super Power Rail".C23
  • Book-to-bill — orders received divided by revenue billed in the same period. Above 1.0 means the backlog is growing. SUSS's Q1 was far above it.C11

12 · Chokepoint callout, the specification fork, and the severity scorecard

The binding link

Stage 3 — ultra-thin grind/thin/polish of a carrier-mounted wafer, co-binding with stage 2 — carrier bond and debond. A 300mm wafer at 30µm cannot be handled without a carrierC14; the carrier cannot come off without a debond whose practical bottleneck is reportedly the residue clean rather than the releaseC14; and the grind that gets there is supplied by three names, one of which holds most of it.

The specification question — four readings, of which the likeliest is a sequence, not a fork

A taller allowed package height can be spent more than one way. Below is the arithmetic, using only figures already in this report's source register. Read the assumption warning first, because it governs every number in the table.

Assumption warning. Both branch rows are computed by holding non-die overhead constant at the HBM4 value of ~295µm. Non-die overhead means everything in the package that is not core DRAM die: the foundry-made base logic die, the microbumps, the underfill and the mold cap. That assumption is almost certainly wrong — the same arithmetic implies only ~120µm of overhead at HBM3E, a 175µm jump in one generation. The branch rows are therefore illustrative of direction, not predictive of thickness, and the two right-hand columns should be read as "what this assumption would imply", not as forecasts.

Branch A is relief: dies get roughly 25% thicker, handling gets easier, the difficulty premium falls. This is what the source explicitly says — "the need to process ultra-thin DRAM wafers may decline"C06.

Branch B is the opposite: same ~30µm target, but 25% more thinned wafers per package, more TSV reveals, more bond/debond cycles. This is what the same source family describes when it says the 825–900µm range is being weighed for 20-high stacksC07, and it is the branch under which hybrid bonding — reportedly essential at 20 layersC05 — also arrives. But "25% more wafers" needs a denominator, and this run does not have one. Going 16-Hi to 20-Hi puts 25% more dies in a package and 25% more capacity in it. If demand is set by accelerator unit count with a fixed number of HBM sites per package, thinned-wafer volume rises 25%. If demand is set by total DRAM bit demand, the same bits are simply stacked taller and volume is roughly flat. Under the bit-demand denominator branch B is neutral, not an accelerant, and most of the fork's asymmetry disappears. And branch B is worse at the bottom of its range than the top: at 825µm rather than 900µm, the same overhead leaves 530µm for 20 dies, i.e. roughly 26µm each — thinner dies AND more of them.

The precedent points to branch B. The last height change, 720µm → 775µm, did not make dies thicker. It bought four more layers at a thinner per-die target (50µm → 30µm)C02C03. If height increases are historically spent on layer count, a relaxation is a demand accelerant for this chain, not a defusal.

And the arithmetic does not close, which is itself the finding. Note the implied non-die overhead: ~120µm at HBM3E but ~295µm at HBM4. A constant-overhead budget cannot reconcile the published height and thickness figures, so the reported 50µm and 30µm are approximations that cannot settle the fork. Both branch rows above therefore inherit that error bar and are marked (est.). Anyone telling you this chain's rent is clearly rising or clearly falling in 2026 is working from numbers that do not reconcile.

13 · Awareness curve — honestly staged

The awkward honesty, in two parts. First: in most runs of this rotation the answer to "where is it still early" is a name further left on this curve. Here the genuinely-latent nodes are a private company and a neglected micro-cap. Second, and less comfortable: this run named that micro-cap and then did not underwrite it. 19 · Open questions, risks, and what this run could not resolve

  1. The specification fork is unresolved and the arithmetic in §12 does not close — implied non-die overhead differs by 175µm between HBM3E and HBM4 on published figures. This is the run's central open question.
  2. The height-relaxation reporting is single-origin. C-05, C-06 and C-07 trace to one chain of Korean trade reporting relayed by TrendForce. Republication is not corroboration. No JEDEC primary document was located.
  3. The 30µm figure is trade-press-grade. C-01 and C-02 are both T3 aggregators drawing on the same origin chain, so an earlier "HIGH confidence" rating on them was inconsistent with the single-origin rule applied to TrendForce. Downgraded to MEDIUM and marked (est.).
  4. DISCO's equipment-vs-consumables split comes from one earnings-call write-upC08, not the issuer tanshin. The headline figures are corroboratedC09; the split is not. Consolidated and non-consolidated shipment figures are also unreconciled.
  5. No price history. IBKR returned "No market data permissions" for TSEJ and IBIS, so drawdowns come from a 52-week-high field and exact peak dates are unknown. They are bounded, though — the 13-week and 52-week high fields are equal for DISCO and SUSS, placing both peaks inside the last 13 weeks (see §4). The snapshot also flags that DISCO's last price prints below its own reported 13-week low, i.e. the low fields lag — which puts some freshness doubt on the high field too.
  6. No valuation data of any kind — no multiple, market cap or earnings anchor for any name. This alone blocks any ACTION state and, as §1 says, predetermined the verdict.
  7. No lead-time evidence. Not one delivery-time quote for a grinder, bonder or debonder. The lead-time severity factor is the weakest of the four.
  8. Chinese domestic tool entrants were not examined — the live structural threat to DISCO's share and the largest single hole in the moat assessment.
  9. ASMPT and Ebara were not evaluated despite being named chain participants, and molding/encapsulation (TOWA, Resonac, Namics) — flagged by the dissentC29 as the actual narrowest knot — was not covered at all.
  10. Vendor market-sizing for this chain is unusable — two reports differ by roughly 4x on the same equipment poolC26C27. Cited as evidence of the problem, not of the market.
  11. The unit-toll / difficulty-rent split is unquantified.
