01

Executive snapshot

為什麼是現在

Every AI fabric — GPUs talking inside a rack, racks talking across a hall, datacenters talking across a region — moves bits through one tiny, brutally hard analog circuit: the SerDes (serializer/deserializer). At 224 Gbit/s per lane it is the link that gates 1.6T ports; 448G is next. The headline names (switch chips, retimers, cables) all embed it. Going one layer below them lands on the SerDes / connectivity-IP capability itself — owned by ~4–5 organizations, and (the twist) the cleanest listed pure-play was just bought out, while the one IP gate still listed is the only networking name that hasn't run.

EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

Imagine the AI datacenter as a postal network. The GPUs and switches are sorting offices; the parcels are data. To send a parcel between offices you need a very specialized loading dock that can fling boxes onto a conveyor at insane speed and catch them perfectly at the other end — that dock is the SerDes. Almost every chip in the network has one inside. Designing a leading-edge dock (224 → 448 billion bits a second) is so hard that only a handful of teams on Earth can do it, and — unlike normal chips — it does not get cheaper or smaller as technology advances (it's analog). That makes the SerDes the chokepoint. The investing twist: the best docks are kept in-house by Broadcom and Marvell (not for sale), the cleanest "sell-the-dock" company (Alphawave) was just bought by Qualcomm, and the one remaining listed "dock toolmaker" (Synopsys) is the only networking name that is still cheap — because the market is busy worrying about its China and Ansys problems, not its dock business.

The framework — two orthogonal axes

MECE 框架

Every connectivity chip lives at one (network domain × stack layer) coordinate. The domain answers where the link runs and how far; the layer answers what physical part it is. Hold both in view and the chokepoint pops out: the same L3 SerDes IP sits inside the chip that wins every domain — scale-up, scale-out and scale-across all pass through it.

THE LAYER STACK · 分層 (read this first)
L1 the switch / GPU system that needs to move data  ·  L2 the connectivity chip (switch ASIC, retimer, AEC, optical DSP) that drives the bits  ·  L3 the SerDes / PHY IP block inside every one of those chips that actually launches/recovers the 100+ GHz signal  ·  UP upstream: the EDA tools + foundry + analog-design know-how that create the IP.

03

Layer 3 — the connectivity silicon, illustrated

連網元件

The building blocks of networking silicon. Five of the six below are different chips — but every one of them embeds the same scarce thing: a leading-edge SerDes / PHY. That shared dependency is exactly why the bottleneck is structural, not a one-product story.

SerDes / PHY IP CHOKEPOINT

The mixed-signal block that turns slow parallel on-die data into one 100+ GHz serial lane and recovers it at the far end. Analog-dominated, so it does not shrink with Moore's law. Every chip below contains many of them.

Captive: Broadcom · Marvell; Merchant IP: Synopsys · Cadence · Qualcomm/Alphawave

Switch ASIC VOLUME

The 102 Tbit/s Ethernet switch (Tomahawk 6) or router (Jericho 4) at the heart of scale-out. Its bandwidth = lane-rate × lane-count, so a switch is essentially hundreds of SerDes wrapped around a packet engine.

Make: Broadcom, Marvell, NVIDIA (Spectrum-X)

Retimer (PCIe / Ethernet) SCALE-UP

A chip that receives a worn-out 224G signal, fully recovers the data, and re-launches a pristine copy — extending reach on a board. It is, by construction, two back-to-back SerDes. Astera's Aries is the canonical example.

Make: Astera, Broadcom, Montage, Parade

AEC — active electrical cable NVLINK COPPER

Copper cable with a SerDes/DSP in each connector, letting copper reach a few meters at 224G — NVIDIA's preferred scale-up medium "out to 2027–2028." Credo's "purple cables." More SerDes, sold as a cable.

Make: Credo, Astera (Taurus), Amphenol

Optical DSP & the LPO threat DISAGGREGATION

The PAM4 DSP retimes/cleans the optical signal. LPO deletes it to save ~40–50% power — bearish this specific chip, but it pushes the equalization burden back onto the host switch/XPU SerDes (bullish the chokepoint).

DSP: Marvell (Inphi), Broadcom; LPO analog: MACOM, Semtech

Custom XPU THE NEW BUYER

Every hyperscaler building its own AI chip (TPU, Trainium, Maia) needs leading-edge SerDes — and most license it from Synopsys/Cadence/Alphawave or buy a custom-silicon partner (Broadcom, Marvell). This is what grows the merchant-IP TAM.

Buyers: Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta · via AVGO/MRVL + SNPS/CDNS

08

The chokepoint — and the variant view

卡點與變異認知

Leading-edge SerDes / connectivity IP (224G shipping, 448G next)

The analog mixed-signal block that launches and recovers a 100+ GHz serial lane. It gates every switch ASIC, retimer, AEC, optical/coherent DSP and custom XPU — and it does not shrink with the process node, so capacity is bounded by a tiny pool of mixed-signal design talent. Only ~4–5 organizations field it at the leading edge: Broadcom and Marvell keep their best SerDes captive (not for sale); Synopsys and Cadence license it as merchant IP; the cleanest listed pure-play, Alphawave, was absorbed by Qualcomm in December 2025. So there is no clean way to buy the chokepoint directly — you buy the toll-gates around it.

Variant perception vs consensus · 變異認知

Consensus trades "networking silicon" as a chip-volume story and buys the hot chips: MRVL +261%, ALAB +133%, CRDO +79%, MACOM +121% YTD. The variant: the deepest, most durable rent is the analog SerDes/connectivity IP — and (a) its cleanest pure-play (Alphawave) was just bought by Qualcomm, so listed SerDes-IP exposure is vanishing, while (b) the one remaining listed merchant-IP gate, Synopsys (−1% YTD), is the only networking-adjacent name that didn't run — mispriced as a China-EDA / Ansys-troubled roll-up even as the connectivity-IP toll (224G→448G, UCIe, PCIe6) keeps growing. (Honest caveat: the LPO host-SerDes tailwind flows mostly to the captive owners (AVGO/MRVL) and hyperscaler ASIC teams — it supports the chokepoint thesis more than the specific SNPS call.)

If we're wrong, it's most likely because: (1) SNPS's de-rating reflects a durable franchise problem (China revenue permanently impaired; Ansys value-destructive) and IP is too small a slice (~$1.75B of ~$7B) to re-rate it; (2) hyperscalers in-source SerDes (or buy via Qualcomm), eroding the merchant-IP TAM; (3) the best SerDes stays captive at AVGO/MRVL, so the listed "IP gate" never captures the headline rent. SNPS is a relative-value "unloved arms-dealer" idea, not a pure SerDes bet.

09

Are we early? The awareness curve

現在是早期嗎

The whole game is owning a chokepoint before the crowd prices it — the way the 2018 MLCC and 2021 CPU/substrate shortages showed up in lead-times and pricing weeks before the stocks moved. The hard truth here: nearly every networking-silicon chip has already run. The one exception is the deepest, most durable link — the IP gate — which the market has written off for unrelated reasons. That asymmetry is the finding.

How to be early next time 如何提前布局

1 · Buy the unloved arms-dealer

When the deepest link's listed proxy is cheap for unrelated reasons (SNPS's China/Ansys overhang) while its toll keeps growing, that gap is the asymmetry.

2 · Watch where the rent is captive

The best SerDes (AVGO/MRVL) is never sold — so "buy the chokepoint" means buying the owner of the chip, or the merchant gate that arms everyone else.

3 · A buy-out removes a pure-play

Qualcomm taking Alphawave shrinks listed SerDes-IP supply — the remaining proxies get scarcer, not less valuable. Track who the M&A leaves behind.

4 · Sell the recognition, not the news

Once a chip name has tripled and upgrades flood in, the asymmetry is gone. MRVL (+261%) and ALAB (+133%) are past that point — own them for compounding, not a re-rating.
