Executive snapshot

為什麼是現在

"AI needs liquid cooling" is now a fully-discovered trade — Vertiv, Modine, the Taiwanese cold-plate names have all roughly doubled. So this run does what the rotation is for: it goes one layer past the parts everyone owns (cold plate, quick-disconnect) and asks what binds deeper, toward the chip. The tempting answer is "the coolant" — and that is exactly the trap. And the bear case the cold-plate bulls under-price: in-chip microfluidics (etched into the silicon) cools up to 3× better and, if it arrives, dissolves the cold-plate/TIM layer entirely. EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

Think of cooling an AI chip like cooling a car engine. Everyone already bought the radiator and the hoses (the cold plate and the quick-connect couplings) — those stocks have doubled. So we ask: what's the next hard part as the engine gets twice as hot? The obvious guess is "the coolant" — but coolant is cheap and anyone can make it (it's basically antifreeze-and-water to a published recipe). The genuinely hard, scarce part is the pump-and-plumbing system that has to move twice as much coolant without leaking — and, soon, a system that lets the coolant boil right on the chip to carry away even more heat. That whole system has to be tested and approved together, which only a few companies can do. The wildcard, years out: engineers are learning to carve tiny channels into the chip itself so coolant flows inside the silicon (Microsoft showed this works ~3× better) — if that lands, it makes today's radiators obsolete. The investing punchline: almost every cooling stock has already run, except one quiet European name (Munters) that keeps winning huge AI orders while its stock sits flat.

LAYER KEY · follow the heat from the die outward

DIE / TIM heat leaves the chip through a thermal-interface material into the cold plate (Run #4 / die-level frontier)  ·  SECONDARY LOOP a pump + CDU + blind-mate manifold carry it away — and the new two-phase direct-to-chip system lets the fluid boil at the plate the chokepoint  ·  HEAT REJECTION the facility loop dumps the heat to air/water (dry coolers, CRAH, evaporative — where Munters sits)  ·  FLUID the coolant filling all of it — single-phase (commodity) or two-phase dielectric (scarce but PFAS-cursed)  ·  IN-CHIP the 2028+ disruptor: channels etched into the silicon itself

6-TERM GLOSSARY

DLC (direct liquid cooling) coolant piped straight to a cold plate on the chip · CDU (coolant distribution unit) the pump-and-heat-exchanger box that drives the in-rack loop and isolates it from the facility loop · single-phase vs two-phase single-phase = liquid stays liquid; two-phase = the fluid boils on the plate, absorbing far more heat per gram (the next step when single-phase tops out ~2,000W) · dielectric fluid an electrically non-conductive engineered fluid (for two-phase / immersion) — the niche 3M just exited · TIM (thermal interface material) the paste/metal between die and lid/plate; the dominant heat bottleneck at high flux · microfluidics hair-thin channels etched into the chip so coolant flows inside the silicon — the long-run disruptor.

The chokepoint & the variant view

卡點與變異認知

The secondary loop & the two-phase-DTC system — not the molecule

Going one layer below the cold plate, the intuitive answer is "the coolant" — and it is wrong. Single-phase direct-to-chip fluid is a published commodity (PG25 = 25% propylene-glycol + 75% water, an open OCP spec) with six-plus qualified makers; the recurring "razor" is a thin ~$30–80/rack/yr services annuity, not a toll. The link that actually binds is the secondary loop — the CDU, pump and blind-mate manifold whose flow roughly doubles at Rubin — and, as single-phase tops out near ~2,000W, the two-phase direct-to-chip system that lets the fluid boil at the plate (5–10× the heat-transfer). The scarce thing is the co-qualified evaporator + fluid + CDU system and its design-win slot, which only a handful of vendors can ship. The decisive twist this report leads with: almost the entire loop already re-rated (Vertiv +88%, Modine +92%, Flex +143% YTD), so tightness is high but the buyable edge is the disaggregation — the one un-bid link (heat-rejection, Munters −4%) and the un-priced bear case (in-chip microfluidics deletes the loop, 2028+).

The variant perception — a three-part disaggregation 變異認知

Consensus: "Liquid cooling = the AI-infra winner; buy Vertiv/the cold-plate names; the coolant is a nice recurring razor." The crowd has now bid the whole visible loop (Flex +143%, Modine +92%, Vertiv +88%, nVent +60%). The variant has three layers; the schematic shows the core of it — why "the molecule" is the wrong place to go deeper:

① The coolant razor is a myth at the molecule level

Single-phase DLC is a published spec (PG25, OCP), 6+ qualified makers (Castrol got "OCP Inspired" May-26), TAM ~$0.3→2B. The "recurring fluid" is a thin services annuity (~$30–80/rack/yr) and a monitoring contract (Ecolab 3D TRASAR), not a toll. Bearish the fluid pure-play.

② The binding link is the 2P-DTC system + the loop under ~2× flow — not the fluid, not yet the silicon

Consensus hasn't separated two-phase DTC (live, ~2027, the real next chokepoint) from two-phase immersion (dying — incompatible with serviceable blind-mate Kyber, and PFAS-cursed). The scarce thing is the co-qualified system + design slot. Differentiated.

③ In-chip microfluidics is a 2028+ disintermediation the cold-plate bulls under-price

TSMC IMC-Si / Microsoft-Corintis cool ~3× better by etching channels into the silicon — collapsing the cold-plate/TIM layer and migrating value to the foundry/OSAT. A tail risk to the consensus long. How it's wrong: NVIDIA could ride 45°C warm-water single-phase to Feynman (no two-phase) → ② premature; OR in-chip arrives faster (2027–28) → the external loop de-rates sooner; OR a PFAS-free 2P fluid becomes genuinely scarce → the molecule re-acquires a moat after all.

Rent-capture test (margins settle it): CDU/loop system-integrator (Vertiv/Modine — captures the loop rent) ≈ 2P-DTC system (Accelsius/ZutaCore, private, the next rent) > heat-rejection (Munters, un-bid) > the fluid molecule (commodity, thin) — and the die-level frontier (Flex/JetCool, microfluidics) where rent migrates to the foundry long-run. The tell: the most-cited "deeper" answer (the coolant) keeps the least rent — which is exactly why the crowd's intuition is the trap.
