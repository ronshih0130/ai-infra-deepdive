01

Executive snapshot

為什麼是現在

Every AI campus needs to take utility power at medium voltage and chop, switch and protect it before it ever reaches a GPU. That job belongs to switchgear — and it is now the single most-cited reason data centers slip their opening date. Lead times run 40–80 weeks and much of the market is sold out through 2028. Eaton alone points to a 228 GW data-center pipeline (its capacity-opportunity metric, ≈12 years at 2025 build rates on our math) behind a firm $14.5B order backlog. The scarce part isn't the metal cabinet: it's the sealed vacuum interrupter that breaks the current inside every breaker — and a staged European ban on SF6 gas is pushing new-build switchgear onto vacuum, hitting the commodity tier first (2026) and the hard high-voltage version later (2028+).

EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

Picture the electricity arriving at a data center like a firehose at very high pressure. Before it can safely feed the servers you need big industrial switches and circuit-breakers — the "switchgear" — that can turn that firehose on and off and slam it shut in a fault. The hard part of a breaker is the tiny sealed vacuum bottle (a "vacuum interrupter") where the electric arc gets snuffed out in a vacuum. Very few factories in the world can make good ones, especially at high voltage, and a European law is now banning the old gas (SF6) that used to do this job — so demand for these vacuum bottles is jumping. That scarce bottle is the chokepoint. The catch this report uncovers: the bottle-makers are scarce, but the real profit is grabbed by the big companies that bundle bottles into finished switchgear — and their shares (and the one pure bottle-maker's shares) have already jumped. So we ask: is there any of this shortage left un-priced, and where does an amateur still find it early?

The framework — two orthogonal axes

MECE 框架

Power delivery is a ladder of voltages. The domain answers where on the ladder you are (grid → substation → MV distribution → LV → rack → die); the layer view answers what you're buying at that rung. The chokepoint is the cell where a scarce component (the arc-break element) meets the rung that is both regulation-stressed and demand-stressed: MV distribution & protection. Everything above it (transformers, turbines) has had its own run in this rotation; everything below it (rack VRM, on-package delivery) too. This report is the missing middle rung.

03

Component deep-dive — what's actually inside the box

關鍵零件

A medium-voltage circuit breaker looks like a fridge-sized cabinet, but its whole job comes down to three poles, each with a vacuum interrupter. Zoom in and the scarcity stacks up: a sealed ceramic body, a metal bellows that lets the contact move while keeping vacuum, and a pair of copper-chromium contacts that survive being vaporised by an arc thousands of times. Each schematic below is drawn, not just named, so a non-expert can see where the hard part is.

Vacuum interrupter CHOKEPOINT

The sealed bottle where the arc is drawn and snuffed in vacuum. At medium voltage it is the only mainstream SF6-free break medium. Doing it reliably at ≥72.5kV is a hard physics problem.
ABB · Eaton · Siemens · Meidensha · Baoguang (CN) — top-5 ~60–70% of a ~$3.2B market (est.)

CuCr contact SUB-CHOKE

Copper carries the current; chromium grains resist arc erosion and stop the contacts welding shut. The recipe + powder-met process is a specialist craft.
Plansee (private, AT) Western leader · SIRUI + others (CN). No clean listed Western vehicle.

Ceramic envelope + bellows SUB-CHOKE

A high-purity alumina tube brazed to metal end-caps holds the vacuum for 30+ years; a stainless bellows lets the contact move without breaking the seal.
Kyocera · CoorsTek · NGK · Morgan Advanced (MGAM.L) — ceramics oligopoly

Why vacuum wins at MV DRIVER

SF6 is a 25,200×-CO₂ greenhouse gas the EU is banning on a voltage-staged clock. At medium voltage, vacuum is the proven replacement — so the ban converts directly into VI demand.
EU F-gas Reg 2024/573 — ≤24kV from Jan-2026
04

How the scarce part is built up into a sold-out product

組裝路徑 L3→L2→L1

Follow one vacuum interrupter up the value chain. It starts as copper + chromium powder + an alumina tube (L3), becomes a sealed vacuum interrupter (L3 module), is dropped into a three-pole circuit breaker or contactor (L2), and is finally wired into a switchgear lineup (L1) that a hyperscaler waits 18 months for. The scarcity is created at the bottle; the price is set at the lineup.

Thin red arrows mark the constricted flow into and out of the vacuum interrupter (scarcity is created here). Thick grey arrows downstream carry the higher margin — the rent is captured at the breaker + switchgear lineup, not at the bottle. That gap is the whole investment problem.

05

Where the same bottle gets deployed — three service maps

應用對應

One vacuum interrupter platform serves several distribution jobs. In a data center the same core shows up in the primary MV switchboard, in ring-main units (RMUs) feeding transformer rooms, and in vacuum contactors switching large motors and chillers. The SF6 ban touches all three at MV — which is why relief capacity (Siemens Energy's Berlin VI plant, ABB's $200M MV expansion) is being poured specifically here.

The same VI core is deployed across three MV jobs, so the SF6 ban hits demand three times over. The relief box on the right is the tell to track: when this capacity lands (2027–28) the tightness eases — a bearish signal for the scarcity trade.

07

The multi-year clock — a regulation-forced demand ramp

多年時間軸

Unlike most chokepoints, this one has a legislated calendar. The EU F-gas Regulation phases out SF6 by voltage class, so each year a bigger slice of the installed base is forced onto vacuum + dry-air. The hinge is Jan-2026 (≤24kV live now); the second shoe is Jan-2028 (HV 52–145kV). The wildcard row is the SiC solid-state breaker — the technology that could leapfrog the mechanical bottle entirely.

08

The chokepoint, scored — and the variant nobody's pricing

卡點評分 + 變異觀點

The SF6-free / high-voltage vacuum interrupter

Every medium-voltage breaker on an AI campus needs a vacuum interrupter. A European law bans SF6 in new-build switchgear on a staged clock (it doesn't force rip-and-replace of the existing base). The live 2026 date is ≤24kV — where vacuum is already mature and China-flooded — so the near-term catalyst lands in the commodity tier. The durable scarce link is the high-voltage / SF6-free interrupter (≥72.5kV): a hard physics + powder-metallurgy craft only four or five firms do well, whose regulatory driver is 2028+ and which still competes with clean-air GIS. So the edge is a later, less-priced link — not "vacuum interrupters" in general, and not the metal cabinet the market keeps buying. Honest tension: the 2026 demand step-up is in the half we're bearish on; the scarce half's catalyst is still two years out.

Variant perception · the disaggregation

Consensus: "Switchgear is sold out — buy the electrical majors." True, but fully in the tape. Our variant: the durable, hard-to-replicate scarcity is a specific sub-product — the SF6-free / HV vacuum interrupter — whose demand is legislated up, while the commodity LV interrupter is a China-flooded, deflating business. The popular trend is bullish the aggregate switchgear box but splits the interrupter market into a scarce half and a commodity half.

How it's wrong: if (a) the binding link is really OEM assembly capacity (labour, engineering), not the bottle — then the rent stays at the OEM regardless; or (b) SiC solid-state breakers leapfrog mechanical vacuum at hyperscalers by ~2028, capping the VI's terminal value; or (c) clean-air GIS beats vacuum at HV, moving the scarce link sideways. The falsifier below (§11) is dated to these.

Dated falsifier — what would prove the thesis wrong

By the next 1–2 earnings prints (into H1-2027), exit the scarcity trade if: (a) MV switchgear lead times roll under ~30 weeks and OEM book-to-bill drops below 1.0 (tightness breaking); or (b) Siemens/ABB VI capacity lands early and interrupter ASPs soften; or (c) a hyperscaler announces a production SiC solid-state / SST MV distribution design-in (structural leapfrog). Any one of these breaks the "durable scarce bottle" story.

Open questions · self-critique (retail lens)

1. Shares are soft. The ~$3.2B VI market size and top-5 ~60–70% are third-party research estimates, not audited — directional only.

2. Meidensha's merchant split is fuzzy. Public sources disagree on how much VI is merchant vs captive; the "buried pure-play" framing is deliberately softened.

3. Is the VI truly the binding link? Genuinely contestable vs OEM assembly / transformer — scored with a co-binding caveat, not asserted.

4. No clean early vehicle. The un-priced dollar (CuCr, ceramic, SiC SSCB) is mostly private or a different cyclical bet — the "be early" mandate is only partly satisfiable here.

5. Catalyst-timing mismatch. The live 2026 SF6 date is ≤24kV (the commodity tier we're bearish on); the scarce HV/SF6-free VI's regulatory driver is 2028+ and contested by clean-air GIS. And we never size the SF6-free/HV sub-segment (undisclosed) — so the "durable scarce link" rests on a link whose demand step-up is still two years out and un-quantified. That is the biggest hole.

Retail steelman of the bear: "All three have run, the pure play is at ~30× after +88%, the rent-capturers are at 22–35×, a legislated tailwind is fully public, and a solid-state technology could obsolete the whole mechanical bottle. Why pay up now?" — a fair challenge the thin EVs concede.
