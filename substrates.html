01

Executive snapshot

為什麼是現在

The AI story everyone trades is packaging — CoWoS, HBM, hybrid bonding. But every one of those stacks is built by depositing a layer, then polishing it flat to the nanometre, over and over. That polishing step (CMP) is done by two consumables that wear out and must be re-bought every wafer: the pad and the slurry. As chips add layers and go 3D, the number of polish steps compounds — and one company quietly owns most of the pads.

EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

A chip is built like a skyscraper — floor by floor. Before you can lay the next floor, the current one has to be sanded perfectly flat. That "sanding" is CMP (chemical-mechanical planarization): a spinning pad presses the wafer while a liquid slurry (tiny abrasive grains + chemistry) grinds it smooth. Modern chips get sanded 30-plus times, and the new "hybrid bonding" that glues chiplets together needs the surface flat to under a nanometre — impossible without perfect CMP. Pads and slurry wear out, so fabs buy them again and again (a "razor-and-blades" business). The catch: one company — Qnity (spun out of DuPont) — makes most of the pads. That's the chokepoint. The question this report answers: who owns it, is the pad safer than the slurry, and — since all the obvious stocks already jumped 60–70% this year — is there any early money left?

03

The four pieces of a CMP step, illustrated

CMP 元件

A polish step needs four things. Only two of them wear out and get re-bought every wafer (pad, slurry) — and of those, the pad is the hardest to second-source. The tool is a one-time capex; the fab is the buyer.

Polishing PAD CHOKEPOINT

A grooved polyurethane disc pressed on the wafer. The groove pattern + hardness are IP, co-qualified per fab/node for 12–24 months, then sticky for ~a decade. Hardest link to switch.

Makers: Qnity (>50%) · Fujibo · JSR

SLURRY CO-BINDING

Nanoparticle abrasive (ceria/colloidal silica) + reactive chemistry, tuned per material (oxide, W, Cu, Co/Ru). More contested than pads — several qualified makers + a rising Chinese one.

Makers: Entegris (~23%) · Fujimi · Merck · Anji

CMP TOOL MACHINE

The polisher itself — spins the pad, feeds slurry, re-textures the pad ("conditioning"). Concentrated (AMAT ~70%) but it's capex, not a per-wafer razor, so it captures a secondary, not the peak, rent.

Makers: Applied Materials · Ebara

Hybrid-bond surface WHY IT MATTERS

The reason CMP is now strategic, not commodity: direct Cu-Cu bonding needs the two faces flat to <0.3 nm Ra with Cu dished 1–2 nm. Miss it and the stack fails. Reported shipping in TSMC SoIC-X (Apple M5 Pro teardown, TechInsights).

Enabled by: co-qual pad+slurry pairs

04

From raw material to finished chip — where CMP sits

從原料到晶片

Trace the flow. Cheap raw inputs (urethane, silica) become a co-qualified consumable pair, which runs on a tool inside the fab, once per layer — repeated 30-40× — to build the chip and, at the end, the hybrid-bond stack. The recurring per-wafer spend concentrates at the consumable.

The tool is bought once; the fab captures its rent by selling wafers. The consumable is the only link that gets re-bought on every wafer and every layer — which is why the razor, not the razor-handle, is the durable toll.

05

Which slurry/pad serves which use-case

應用對應

The same "CMP" word hides very different chemistries. The point of this map: the slurry franchise fragments by material (that's why no one owns >~23% of slurry) — while the pad is largely material-agnostic, so one maker spans all of it. Concentration is structurally higher for pads.

Slurry is a set of five material-specific franchises (so the leader holds only ~23%); the pad is one franchise that spans all five (so the leader holds >50%). Same "CMP" theme — very different concentration, and therefore very different rent durability.

Unlike a raw-material chokepoint (one scarce mine), this is a qualification chokepoint: the barrier is the 12–24-month recipe certification that locks a specific pad+slurry into a fab, then holds for ~a decade. That's why the rent is recurring, and why the pad — hardest to re-qualify — captures the most durable slice.

08

The variant view — pad vs slurry, and the China cut

變異認知 / 拆解

Consensus post-spin bought "Qnity = an AI-materials winner" as one blob (+70% YTD). The disaggregation that actually matters: the pad and the slurry are different businesses with different defensibility — and the single biggest risk to CMP rent, Chinese substitution, hits the slurry hard and the pad barely at all.

VARIANT PERCEPTION · what I think the Street is under-weighting

Consensus: "CMP is a nice AI-materials growth basket; buy Qnity/Entegris/Fujimi, they all ride hybrid bonding." Variant: the pad and slurry are not the same trade. The pad is a >50%, one-franchise, China-resistant near-monopoly whose rent compounds with CMP-step intensity; the slurry is a five-way material-specific fight that China is actively entering from the bottom. So within the same theme, Qnity's pad rent is structurally safer than Entegris/Fujimi's slurry rent — yet the market prices Qnity as "a spin-off" and hasn't fully separated the monopoly pad P&L from the more ordinary interconnect/slurry parts. How it's wrong: if a Chinese or Korean pad house (or a fab-funded 2nd-source) qualifies at leading edge, or if hybrid-bonding volume slips, the pad's premium compresses toward the slurry's — and both are already priced for good news (+60–70% YTD).
