EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

A computer chip can't be soldered straight onto a circuit board — it sits on a little intermediate board called a package substrate, which is built up from thin layers of a special insulating film called ABF. Here's the wild part: ABF stands for Ajinomoto Build-up Film — invented by the Japanese company famous for MSG seasoning — and Ajinomoto makes ~95% of all of it. So a seasoning company quietly gates every advanced AI chip, and it's raising prices because demand is exploding. Even deeper, the glass cloth inside ABF (T-glass) is ~90% controlled by one firm, Nittobo. The investor question: own the film monopoly (Ajinomoto), the broad materials giant (Shin-Etsu), or the substrate maker (Ibiden, already up 3x) — and notice which is hiding in plain sight.

03

The chokepoint & severity

卡點與嚴重度

ABF build-up film (a ~95% monopoly, on a ~90% T-glass monopoly)

Every advanced GPU/CPU/ASIC package is built from ABF film, and Ajinomoto controls ~95% of it — one of the tightest single-supplier chokepoints in all of technology, raising prices +30% into a >20% supply gap. The film itself depends on T-glass, where Nittobo holds ~90% and admits its capacity expansion "may still fall short." This is a genuine near-monopoly with real pricing power, not just a tight market — the strongest single-supplier position in the whole rotation. The only reason it isn't more crowded: it's hidden inside a Japanese food company and an obscure glass maker.

VARIANT PERCEPTION vs CONSENSUS · 變異認知

Consensus, to the extent it has noticed: "ABF is tight — buy the substrate fabs (Ibiden)." But Ibiden has already run ~7x off its lows (+192% YTD) and faces several competitors (Shinko, AT&S, Unimicron). The disaggregation: the real monopoly isn't the substrate fab — it's the film (Ajinomoto ~95%) and the T-glass (Nittobo ~90%) beneath it, both hiding inside diversified/obscure Japanese companies that the AI crowd hasn't priced as AI plays. The film monopoly is raising prices 30% with no competitor able to respond. If we're wrong, it's that (a) glass-core substrates leapfrog ABF this decade, eroding the franchise, or (b) Ajinomoto's ABF profit stays too small a slice of a huge food company to move the stock.

Who captures the rent?

Margins settle it. Ajinomoto has the textbook monopoly margin on ABF — but it's a small slice of a giant food company, so the stock is diluted (and only +57%). Ibiden captures the substrate-fab value and has re-rated hardest (+192%), but competes with several fabs. Shin-Etsu is the broad, lower-risk materials gate (silicon wafers #1 + resist). Conclusion: the tightest monopoly (Ajinomoto/Nittobo) has the least pure stock exposure; the purest substrate exposure (Ibiden) is the most priced. The hidden edge is owning the monopoly and accepting the dilution.
