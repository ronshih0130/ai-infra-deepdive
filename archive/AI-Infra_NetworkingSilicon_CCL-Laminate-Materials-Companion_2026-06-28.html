01

Executive snapshot

copper interconnect · CCL + connector

Explain it simply · 白話版

A GPU "supercomputer" like NVIDIA's NVL72 is really 72 chips that must talk to each other as if they were one. Inside one rack they talk over copper wire, not fibre — because copper needs no laser, burns ~20 kW less power per rack, and costs about 1/6 of optics. NVL72 alone packs 5,184 copper cables, >2 miles of wire.

But at today's speed (224 Gb/s per lane) copper signal dies after under 2 metres. To squeeze the signal through, the industry needs two ultra-hard, ultra-pure things: a special low-loss circuit-board material (CCL) and precision connectors/cables. Both are made by a handful of mostly-Asian firms. That handful is the chokepoint — the "picks-and-shovels beneath the picks-and-shovels."

The catch (the honest part): the obvious names already exploded. The purest material play, Elite Material (2383.TW), is +219% YTD / +496% in a year; Amphenol sits near its all-time high. The one un-bid relative-value name is TE Connectivity (−12% YTD), and the genuinely-early dollar is one layer deeper still (the glass cloth) — or in the optics that replaces copper after 2027.

Glossary — 6 terms a non-expert stalls on
SerDes — the chip circuit that serialises/recovers a 224 Gb/s signal. The link above this report (Runs #5/#11); here we study the wire it drives.
PAM4 — a signalling scheme cramming 2 bits per symbol so a lane hits 224 Gb/s. Doubles speed but halves noise margin → punishes any lossy material.
CCL (copper-clad laminate) — the raw sheet (resin + woven glass + copper foil) a PCB is etched from. "Ultra-low-loss" grades (M8/M9) lose the least signal.
Df (dissipation factor) — how much signal a material bleeds as heat. Lower = better. Megtron-8 ≈ 0.0015; ordinary FR-4 is ~10× worse.
NVLink spine — the copper "backbone" cartridge wiring every GPU to every NVSwitch in a rack. Amphenol builds the GB200 version.
Scale-up vs scale-out — scale-up = GPUs inside one rack acting as one chip (copper). Scale-out = rack-to-rack (optics). Copper owns scale-up… for now.

Component deep-dive — the four links beneath the chip

draw it, don't list it
Chokepoint link Materials gate Assembly gate Deepest / upstream

Glass cloth DEEPEST

Woven low-Dk glass fibre, impregnated with resin, is the skeleton of the laminate. The flatter and lower-loss the weave, the cleaner the 224G signal.

Who: Nittobo ~90% T-glass, AGC, Nan Ya; resin from Shin-Etsu/DIC. Sold out to mid-2027.

CCL laminate CHOKEPOINT

Copper-clad laminate: the sheet the board is etched from. Ultra-low-loss M8/M9 grades barely bleed the 224G signal — a hard materials-science moat.

Who: Panasonic (Megtron-8), Elite Material (EM-892K2), TUC, Iteq, Doosan.

Connector + cable CHOKEPOINT

The blind-mate, high-density connector and cable cartridge that physically join GPU↔NVSwitch. Each junction must hold 224G with near-zero reflection.

Who: Amphenol (NVLink spine), TE, Molex, FIT. Top-5 ≈ 52–62% (vendor est.).

NVLink copper spine SYSTEM

The assembled backbone: thousands of cables wiring every GPU to every switch as one coherent memory domain — the thing all the materials & connectors add up to.

Who: integrated by Amphenol for GB200; shifts to PCB-backplane at Rubin.

How it builds up — glass cloth → laminate → board → spine → rack

At every step value is added but the scarcity is set upstream: a board fab can buy more presses, but it cannot conjure M8 laminate that is on allocation, nor glass cloth whose only big maker is sold out to 2027. The rent therefore pools in the two red boxes — and, deepest of all, in the glass cloth that feeds them (the same Nittobo monopoly found beneath Run #6's package substrate).

The chokepoint & severity

4-factor /20

The passive copper channel — CCL laminate (materials gate) + connector/cable system (assembly gate)

Every 224G scale-up link inside an AI rack must pass through two hard-to-replicate, concentrated links: an ultra-low-loss laminate made by ~5 firms (and a glass cloth made essentially by one), and a precision connector/cable made by a top-5 that holds ~half the market. Neither can be "ordered into existence" — laminate is on 20+ week allocation and glass cloth is sold out to 2027. Composite severity 16/20, capped only because copper itself is on an optical clock.

Variant perception & disaggregation — four ways this isn't the obvious trade

1 · The rent is beneath the chip, not in it. Consensus buys the SerDes silicon and the optics (Runs #5/#11/#13). The un-shrinkable rent is the passive copper channel — but the obvious expressions already ran (Elite +219% YTD, Amphenol near its high), so the insight is right while the easy entry is gone.

2 · The rent migrates within copper: cable → laminate → optics. NVL72's copper-cable spine (Amphenol's win) does not cleanly repeat — Rubin/Kyber shift to a PCB-board backplane (more laminate, fewer discrete cables), and Feynman goes optical. So "buy the spine-cartridge winner" mis-times the content shift; the materials gate (CCL→glass cloth) is the more durable copper bet.

3 · Copper is on a clock — bullish now, bearish-terminal. 224G passive copper dies under 2 m; 448G is worse. Past ~2 m and past 2027 the rent migrates to optics/CPO. That clock is exactly why severity is 16, not 19.

4 · The deepest monopoly is the least investable — and it's the same one as Run #6. Nittobo (~90% T-glass) sits beneath both the package substrate (Run #6) and the board laminate (this run). It is the tightest link of the whole AI-hardware stack (severity 19) yet there is no clean listed pure-play — the recurring "scarcest link, no investable handle" finding (cf. nanocrystalline cores, SerDes IP, QD couplings).

Rent-capture test who keeps the scarcity rent?

Glass cloth (Nittobo) > CCL laminate (Elite/Panasonic — +20-40% price hikes = genuine pricing power) > connector/cable system (Amphenol — 2-3 sourced, so volume rent not a toll) > #2 connector (TE — smaller AI mix). The scarcest link keeps the most margin but is least investable; the best-priced investable rent (CCL) has already been bid to ~110× earnings.

How it could be wrong

Copper extends further than feared (better AEC retimers, MaxLinear/Credo) → the optical clock slips right and the copper-media names compound longer. Or the opposite: Rubin's PCB-backplane + early CPO compresses discrete-cable content faster than expected, hurting Amphenol's spine franchise specifically. And the "shortage" is partly cyclical pricing: the deepest monopoly is itself adding capacity — Nittobo is reportedly ~tripling T-glass capacity (¥50B+, 2026–27) and Panasonic is expanding Megtron lines — which softens the "structural scarcity to 2027+" durability the whole materials thesis leans on, and could de-rate the +40% price story.
