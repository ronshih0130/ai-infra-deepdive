Executive call

the fresh cut, one layer below the headline

Explain it simply · 白話版

An HBM chip is a tower of ~12-16 razor-thin DRAM dies stacked on one "brain" die at the bottom, glued and molded together, and bolted next to the GPU. Everyone is buying the tower-makers (SK Hynix, Micron, Samsung) — and they are minting money. But look one floor down: (1) whoever wins the memory war, every tower must be ground thin, molded in resin, and underfilled with glue — and a handful of Japanese firms own those tools/chemicals near-outright (Towa ~100% of HBM molding; Namics is SK Hynix's only underfill supplier). (2) The bottom "brain" die now has to be built in a logic chip factory (TSMC), not a memory fab — a new toll. (3) Because HBM eats ~3-4× the wafer space per gigabyte of normal memory, it is starving ordinary DRAM, and that shortage (DDR5 prices doubling) may be the more un-capped money. The un-crowded money is in the floor tools/materials and the commodity-DRAM squeeze — not another bid on the tower-makers.

Variant perception — what we believe that the Street mostly doesn't

Consensus: "HBM = bullish memory, own SK Hynix/Micron." True but fully priced (SK Hynix +237% YTD, Micron +262%). Our variant is a disaggregation: HBM is bullish the aggregate, but the least-priced scarcity sits at three co-binding floors — (a) the sole-source stacking materials + near-monopoly molding/grinding tools that every stack passes through regardless of who wins the DRAM war (Namics MR-MUF sole-source for SK Hynix; Towa ~100% HBM molding, +56% YTD lagging; Disco grind/dice; Resonac NCF); and (b) the commodity-DRAM cannibalization — HBM's ~3-4× wafer-per-bit penalty is draining DDR5 supply, and that shortage is less price-capped than HBM itself (which NVIDIA has locked via prepaid LTAs). The base-die-at-TSMC story (much-discussed) is a real dependency but is already the SemiAnalysis/TrendForce consensus framing — more a "who captures rent" question than hidden scarcity.

If we're wrong, it's most likely because…

Samsung catches up (vertically integrated SF4 base die, first-shipped HBM4 Feb-26, reported OpenAI/AMD wins) and the "who wins" question stops being neutral to the tool/material makers; or CXMT floods commodity DRAM by 2028, reversing the cannibalization trade; or the buyers keep funding new supply (NVIDIA prepays, supplier capex) faster than the choke can extract rent; or the tool/material links are simply too small / too illiquid / already re-rated (Resonac +166%) to matter for a public-market amateur.

Layer key & 6-term glossary
Core DRAM die — one thin memory layer; 24/32Gb; ~12-16 stacked per HBM4 tower.
Base / logic die — the "brain" at the bottom; HBM4 moves it from a memory process to a TSMC/Samsung logic node.
TSV (through-silicon via) — vertical copper wires drilled through each die to connect the stack (2,048 in HBM4).
Wafer thinning/grinding — grinding each die to ~30-50µm so 12-16 fit in a stack; Disco's tools.
TCB vs MR-MUF — two ways to bond+fill the stack: thermal-compression bonding + NCF film (Samsung/Micron) vs mass-reflow molded underfill (SK Hynix, Namics resin).
Compression molding / EMC — encasing the stack in epoxy; Towa's tool, Sumitomo Bakelite's compound.

Illustrative aggregation. The tool/material pools are a small fraction of the ~$58B HBM TAM — which is precisely why they are under-bid relative to the DRAM makers, yet each is a near-monopoly toll.

Owner vs buyer — where does the rent settle?

Margins settle it. The scarcity rent settles at the DRAM oligopoly — SK Hynix posted a ~72% operating margin in Q1-26 (op ₩37.6T on ₩52.6T rev); Micron ~75% GM FQ2-26 (guiding ~81%). That is 2× the best tool vendor. But note the fresh nuance: HBM pricing is partly locked by prepaid long-term agreements — but the two makers now diverge: Micron keeps price caps in its LTAs (capping HBM upside), while SK Hynix is reportedly removing the caps (TrendForce, 2 Jul 2026), so HBM upside is less capped at Hynix than at Micron. Independently, all three say commodity DRAM is currently the higher-margin product — SK Hynix is even throttling HBM4 to chase the un-capped DDR windfall (the op-margin gap favors commodity DRAM by >15pp). So rent ranks: DRAM makers (72-77% op) > TSMC base die (58% op) > equipment (40-45% op) > materials (11-34%). The tool/material links earn a durable toll, not the scarcity rent — but they are un-bid and method-agnostic, which is the asymmetry. Crucial rebuttal to my own bear: Disco's ~41% FY25 op margin shows the toll can compound — the risk is that weaker links (Hanmi op −1.6% YoY, ASMPT GM ~36%) may not.

Deployment map — HBM4 vs HBM3E across the node

what Rubin changes, and where the pressure lands

Bandwidth HBM4

2,048-bit interface doubles the bus; Rubin R200 hits ~22 TB/s total (vs GB300 ~8 TB/s) at the same 288GB.

Stack height yield stress

12-Hi (36GB) → 16-Hi (48GB). Thinner dies + more TSVs → stack yield drops (HBM3E <~70%) → the materials/tools gate binds harder.

Base-die migration new toll

The bottom die moves from a memory fab to a TSMC/Samsung logic node — a foundry toll, and the surface for "custom HBM."

Cannibalization 2nd-order

HBM eats ~3-4× the wafer area per GB (Micron: "3-to-1"). It is starving commodity DRAM — the less-price-capped shortage.

derived, not asserted

The binding link: the HBM stack-assembly materials + molding/grinding gate

Whoever wins the DRAM war, every HBM4 stack must be ground thin, TSV-etched, bonded, underfilled, molded, and tested. That physical gate is owned by a handful of Japanese near-monopolies: Namics (sole-source MR-MUF underfill for SK Hynix's ~57% share), Resonac (top of a 3-firm ≈99% NCF oligopoly), Towa (~100% of HBM compression molding, est.), Disco (~65-80% grind/dice, est.), and Hanmi (~71% TCB, ~90% at the leading edge). The severity discount vs a perfect 20: the rent owner is the DRAM maker (the gate earns a durable toll, not the scarcity rent), the links are individually small/illiquid, and hybrid bonding sunsets the TCB/mold franchise from ~2027.

The central counter-argument — three floors, ranked by how un-priced

the disaggregation is the edge

"HBM is bullish memory" is right about the aggregate and wrong about where the un-priced scarcity sits. Three candidate floors, ranked most→least un-priced: (c) tools/materials > (b) commodity-DRAM cannibalization > (a) base-die-at-TSMC. The schematic contrasts the consensus view with the disaggregated one.

Why the tools/materials floor is genuinely un-priced

Towa is up only +56% YTD (vs SK Hynix +237%, Micron +262%) despite ~100% HBM molding share and just ~4 analysts. Sumitomo Bakelite +47%. These sit inside "boring" Japanese chemical/tool companies the memory crowd doesn't screen. It is the Ajinomoto/Nittobo pattern this rotation keeps finding: the tightest monopoly hides in the least-bid stock. The rent is a toll, not scarcity rent — but a near-monopoly toll on a market growing HBM TAM ~$38B→$100B is a durable compounder few are pricing.

How this variant is WRONG (the falsifiers)

(1) Samsung catches up — vertically integrated SF4 base die, first-shipped HBM4 Feb-26, reported OpenAI/AMD wins → "who wins" stops being neutral. (2) CXMT floods commodity DRAM by 2028 (bit share 9→12%, HBM3 by end-26) → reverses floor (b). (3) Buyers fund supply — NVIDIA prepays + supplier capex break the choke and cap rent. (4) Hybrid bonding (2027+) deletes TCB/mold, sunsetting Towa/Hanmi's franchise. (5) Too small/illiquid — Resonac already +166%; the un-bid names may be un-bid for good reason (size, access).

Risk register & open-questions self-critique

the unglamorous ones
Mislocated chokepoint

A co-bottleneck may be tighter: the TSMC leading-edge-logic squeeze (base die + logic + CoWoS all fight for N3/N5), or the KGD / stack-yield wall itself (HBM3E stack yield <~70%, worse at 16-Hi) — which is really a test + yield constraint. We deliberately don't re-center on that: the ATE/test tollgate was Run #14 (Advantest), and CoWoS was Run #2 — this run takes the fresh cut at the assembly materials/tools. But if the true binding link is stack yield, value migrates to test/inspection, not molding. The assembly gate is concentrated but the rent sits at the DRAM maker.

Buyers fund new supply

NVIDIA prepays (~$540-770M each, SS) + supplier capex (SK Hynix >$30B, Micron ~$20B) break the choke over time and cap rent. The whole thesis is a window, not a perpetuity.

Hybrid bonding deletes the tool/material

At HBM4E (~2027) Cu-Cu hybrid bonding starts replacing TCB + microbumps + underfill + some molding — directly sunsetting Towa/Hanmi/Namics' franchises. Timing (2027-28) is the only cushion.

Samsung / China break the neutrality

If Samsung's vertical SF4 model wins share, or CXMT floods commodity DRAM by 2028, the "method-agnostic, who-wins-doesn't-matter" premise weakens and the cannibalization trade reverses.

Access / liquidity / FX

The best-priced links are small-cap Japanese (Towa, Sumitomo Bakelite) or private (Namics) — hard for a US amateur to size, and yen/won moves shave USD returns. Some may be un-bid for good reason.

Open questions (unresolved holes — not a victory lap)

1. Rent capture at the gate is unproven. I assert the tool/material makers earn a durable toll, but Hanmi's op profit actually fell −1.6% YoY and ASMPT's GM slipped to ~36% — equipment is cyclical, and I can't prove the toll compounds through a memory down-cycle.

2. Towa's ~100% HBM-molding share is (est.), triangulated from 3 secondary sources, not a primary disclosure; the standard-IC-molding splits are single-source. If the real HBM figure is 70-80%, the monopoly framing weakens.

3. Namics "sole-source, no 2nd source" is well-reported but not company-confirmed, and the exclusivity-sunset "~early 2027" is single-source. The strongest-severity link is also the least primary-sourced.
