01

Executive snapshot

為什麼是現在

Air cooling tops out near 50 kW per rack. An NVIDIA GB300 rack draws ~130 kW; the Rubin-era rack heads for 1 MW — so liquid is no longer optional, it is the architecture. Everyone now agrees on that, which is why the cold-plate and coolant-distribution-unit (CDU) pure-plays have already doubled. The unappreciated detail: a direct-to-chip loop is only as reliable as its weakest connection, and a modern rack has hundreds of them — the quick-disconnect (QD) couplings that let a technician pull a hot GPU tray without draining the loop. They must mate blind, seal without a drip, and survive thousands of cycles. That is a brutal, qualification-gated, concentrated link — and its content per rack is doubling from GB200 to GB300. The twist this report documents: the firm that arguably owns the tightest QD position (Dover, via CPC) is up just +13% YTD, while the cooling crowd bid the cold-plate/CDU names +57–93%.

EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

Think of cooling a rack of GPUs like plumbing a building where every radiator runs scalding hot and the pipes are packed shoulder-to-shoulder. You pump liquid right onto each chip through a metal cold plate, gather the warm liquid in manifolds, send it to a CDU (a pump-and-radiator box) and dump the heat outside. Because servers must be pulled out and serviced while the rest of the rack keeps running, every pipe junction uses a quick-disconnect — a self-sealing coupling that snaps together when the tray slides in and shuts off without spilling a drop. Here is the catch most people miss: a leak of even a few millilitres onto live electronics can destroy a $3–4 million rack — so these little couplings carry enormous responsibility, are slow and expensive to qualify, and only a handful of firms make the hyperscale-grade ones. And a GB300 rack needs roughly twice as many of them as a GB200. The investing question: the obvious cooling stocks have already doubled — is there a less-crowded way to own the tightest part, and is anything still early?

03

The cooling loop, illustrated

冷卻迴路元件

The four building blocks that move a megawatt of heat off the chips. The first (the cold plate) is commoditising; the second (the QD coupling) is where the physical scarcity and reliability burden concentrate; the third (the CDU) is where the dollar content and system rent pool; the fourth (in-chip / coolant fluid) is the next-latent frontier.

Cold plate COMMODITISING

A copper micro-channel block pumps liquid millimetres from the die. Real engineering — flatness ≤0.05 mm — but many qualified makers and Asian copper-aluminium hybrids are driving ASP down. Volume grows; pricing power thins.

Makers: AVC, Auras, Boyd, Cooler Master, Delta

QD coupling CHOKEPOINT

Two self-sealing halves that mate blind and shut off without a drip. ~200→450 per rack, each a potential leak point onto live silicon. Long qualification, concentrated supply, content doubling GB200→GB300 — the tightest link.

Makers: CPC (Dover), Stäubli, Parker, Danfoss

CDU / loop RENT POOL

The pump-and-radiator box that moves heat from the rack loop to the facility. Where the dollar content and system integration concentrate — and where a full-stack vendor can attach power, controls and service.

Makers: Vertiv, Boyd, CoolIT, Motivair/Schneider, Delta

Fluid / in-chip NEXT-LATENT

Two-phase & immersion need special dielectric fluid — 3M's Novec exit left a gap. Further out, coolant runs inside the chip (TSMC "water-in-wafer") for ~3,600 W parts. Barely a market today; the next links to wake.

Players: Chemours, Solvay, Corintis (priv.), TSMC

04

From chip to chiller — the build-up

晶片到冷卻塔的建構

Every watt of heat passes through every stage left-to-right. The value and the scarcity concentrate at the connector — because that is the step that is hardest to qualify, impossible to skip, and multiplied hundreds of times per rack. The cold plate collects the heat; the QD couplings are the gates every drop of coolant must pass through, twice (supply and return), at every tray.

Build-up view — the heat (left) exists at every chip; usable cooling only works if every connector seals. The GB300 roughly doubles the QD count vs GB200, so the connector layer's responsibility (and content) compounds faster than the loop around it.

Where each part is leveraged

元件如何對應應用

The same loop hardware serves three live architectures. Note that QD couplings appear in every serviceable liquid loop — only the (still-research) in-chip path, where coolant never leaves the package, could one day escape the external connector.

The connector burden is highest in single-phase D2C — exactly the architecture that is ~55% of the market and ramping fastest with GB300. Rear-door/immersion relieve the QD count; in-chip microfluidics, if it scales, is the long-run disintermediation risk to the entire external-loop thesis.

08

The chokepoint — and how tight

卡點與嚴重度

The blind-mate, dripless quick-disconnect (QD) coupling

You cannot service a 130-kW (soon 1-MW) liquid rack without disconnecting hot trays from a live loop — so every junction is a quick-disconnect that must mate blind (the tray slides in, the technician can't see the connector), seal without a drip (millilitres on live silicon can destroy the rack if leak-detection doesn't catch it first), and survive thousands of cycles. A GB300 NVL72 carries ~24 of these per compute tray — roughly double the GB200 (~10/tray) — pushing per-rack counts toward ~450. That is a brutal, qualification-gated, concentrated link: only a handful of firms (CPC/Dover, Stäubli, Parker, Danfoss) make hyperscale-grade UQDs. The catch for investors: the cooling pure-plays around it have already run +57–93% YTD, and — crucially — UQD is an OPEN, multi-sourced standard (Intel/OCP), so no one owner captures monopoly pricing. The link is tight; the rent is volume, not a toll.

Variant perception · the disaggregation that is the edge 變異認知

CONSENSUS BELIEVES

"Liquid cooling" is one trade, and you own it through the cold-plate & CDU pure-plays — AVC (+57%), nVent (+66%), Vertiv (+93%), even the coolant name Chemours (up sharply YTD). The shared belief: the cold plate / CDU is the scarce content, and being in any cooling pure-play captures the build-out.

THIS REPORT'S VARIANT

Within the loop, the tightest, most reliability-critical, fastest-content-growing sub-link is the QD coupling (2× content GB200→GB300; one drip = dead rack) — yet the cold plate is commoditising and the pure-plays are priced. The best-priced exposure to the connector is not a run-up pure-play but the diversified industrial the cooling crowd isn't bidding — Dover, whose CPC/Everis is a UQD leader, up only +13% YTD because cooling is a minority of a ~$30B company.

How it could be wrong: UQD's open multi-vendor standard means buyers (NVIDIA/hyperscalers) deliberately prevented a QD monopoly, so the connector earns volume, not pricing power — and a diversified Dover may be too small in cooling to move the stock (cooling could be <5% of revenue). Cold-plate ASPs may hold and the pure-plays keep compounding. And if in-chip microfluidics scales by 2027–28, the entire external-QD thesis erodes. The variant is "own the tight link cheaply," not "the QD is an un-substitutable monopoly."

RENT-CAPTURE TEST · who keeps the scarcity rent? (margins settle it)

Rank by where the dollar sticks: system integrator (Vertiv) ≈ QD owner (Dover/CPC) > cold-plate pure-play (Auras) — but with a twist on the QD. The CDU/system integrator (Vertiv) captures rising rack-level content + service at a structural backlog (>$15B). The QD owner (CPC/Dover) sells a high-margin, reliability-critical precision part — real rent, but capped by the open standard (volume, not monopoly price) and diluted inside a diversified parent. The cold-plate pure-play (Auras) has the highest operating leverage to volume but the thinnest moat (commoditising, China competition). So the scarcest link is owned cheapest through a hidden parent, the richest system rent sits at the priced integrator, and the cold plate — most-bought — is the loosest link.

How to be early next time 如何提前布局

1 · Count the part that multiplies

A rack has one CDU but hundreds of connectors. When content per rack doubles (GB200→GB300 QDs), the multiplied part compounds faster than the headline box — and that is where scarcity sticks.

2 · Look for the link hidden in a big parent

The cooling pure-plays are obvious and bid. The QD leader sits inside a diversified industrial (Dover/CPC) the cooling crowd ignores — the same "hidden monopoly" shape as Ajinomoto's ABF film.

3 · Tight ≠ a toll if the spec is open

UQD is multi-source by design, so the connector earns on volume, not monopoly price. Check whether a standard is open before assuming pricing power — the buyers often engineer the choke away.

4 · Respect that "tight" ≠ "cheap"

The QD is tight and the cooling names are up 57–93% YTD. Tightness tells you the business is good; only price tells you whether you're early — here, mostly through the un-bid owner.

If you can only do one thing

The market priced the cold-plate/CDU pure-plays in Run #4's window and has now bid everything obvious in cooling +57–93% YTD. The unappreciated fact is that the binding constraint is the blind-mate connector — the part that multiplies to ~450 per rack and where a single drip can kill the rack — not the cold plate everyone is buying. That link has no clean listed pure-play, so the best-priced way to own it is the hidden owner: Dover (CPC/Everis), up only +13% YTD at ~21× while the pure-plays doubled. Auras is the higher-leverage pure-play at a reasonable multiple; Vertiv is the superb-but-priced system integrator. And keep scanning the genuinely-early latent layer — coolant fluid (post-3M) and in-chip microfluidics — because that is where the next chokepoint is forming. The honest caveat: the QD's open standard means it earns volume, not a monopoly toll — this is "own the tight link cheaply," not "buy an un-substitutable choke."
