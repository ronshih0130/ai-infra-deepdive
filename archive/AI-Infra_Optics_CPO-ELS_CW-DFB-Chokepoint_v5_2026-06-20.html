01

Executive snapshot

為什麼是現在

Runs #1 and #7 chased the optics chain down to the EML laser and the MOCVD tool that grows it. This refresh follows the 2026 architecture shift those runs foreshadowed: co-packaged optics (CPO). CPO moves the optical engine onto the switch/GPU package — but a laser can't survive next to a hot ASIC, so it's pulled out into an External Light Source (ELS). The new bottleneck is the high-power continuous-wave (CW) DFB laser feeding that ELS — and specifically the merchant slice of it, because NVIDIA and Broadcom already locked up or internalised their own supply.

EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

A data-center GPU runs so hot that you can't sit a delicate laser right next to it — it would cook. The newest design, co-packaged optics (CPO), solves the speed problem by moving the optics onto the chip's package, but that forces the laser to move outside, into a little plug-in box called an External Light Source (ELS). That box needs a special, powerful, ultra-steady laser (a "CW-DFB"). Very few companies can make it, and the two biggest buyers — NVIDIA and Broadcom — already grabbed or built their own supply. So the real prize is the small merchant pool that everyone else has to buy from. The catch this report is honest about: the purest company in that pool already went up about 17× in a year — the secret is out. The job now is spotting where the next one hides.

03

The light sources, by generation

光源元件 · 分代圖解

Five ways to put light into an AI link. The industry is migrating left→right; the chokepoint is the Gen-3 ELS — and four of the five still depend on a III-V (InP/GaAs) laser, which is exactly why the bottleneck is structural.

G1 · EML INCUMBENT

Electro-absorption modulated laser — the volume source for 800G/1.6T pluggables. ~8 lasers per 800G module (~$80–100 laser BOM). NVIDIA-locked.

Volume: Lumentum, Coherent, AAOI

G2 · CW + SiPh SCALING

A steady CW laser feeds a silicon chip that modulates. Only 2–4 CW lasers per module (~$20–40 BOM, <½ of EML). Cuts laser dollars even as it scales.

PIC: GlobalFoundries, TSMC COUPE, Tower

G3 · CPO + ELS CHOKEPOINT

For CPO the laser exits into an external module (ELSFP) and pipes 400mW-class CW light in by fiber. Spec is locked into the PIC — you can't swap vendors. Merchant supply is the pinch.

Merchant: Sivers, Coherent; captive: NVIDIA/Broadcom

G2.5 · VCSEL-NPO RIVAL PATH

Broadcom's near-packaged optics uses GaAs VCSELs over multimode fiber — simpler, no InP CW. Great for short scale-up; limited reach. A rival that bypasses the ELS chokepoint entirely.

Broadcom 3.2T NPO (OFC 2026)

G4 · Monolithic 2030+ ESCAPE

Grow the III-V laser directly on the silicon photonics wafer — no separate laser, no ELS. The long-run escape that could dissolve the whole chokepoint. Pre-revenue today.

Pioneer: Aeluma (III-V on 300mm Si)

08

The chokepoint & the variant view

卡點與變異認知

The merchant 400mW CW-DFB laser for ELS

Silicon can't make light, and a laser can't sit on a hot ASIC — so CPO forces the laser out into an external module fed by a high-power CW-DFB laser. That laser is spec-locked into the photonic chip, takes 18–24 months and an InP fab to qualify, and the merchant fab pool runs ~83% utilised. But the decisive twist: NVIDIA ($4B into Coherent + Lumentum) and Broadcom (vertical + VCSEL NPO) took their supply off the merchant market, so the scarcity rent only accrues to the small merchant pool — Sivers plus the GlobalFoundries / Ayar / Marvell-Celestial ecosystem. The physics is acute; the buyable rent is narrow and mostly already discovered.

The variant perception — a two-part disaggregation 變異認知

Consensus: "CPO is the next AI-optics wave — buy the optics names." The variant has two layers, and the schematic below shows the first:

① Architecture disaggregation

CPO is bullish the CW-DFB ELS but mix-bearish the EML the incumbent multiples are built on. Laser BOM falls ~$80–100 → ~$20–40; the modulation value migrates onto the silicon PIC (GFS/TSMC) and the merchant CW supplier. The "buy optics" trade can be right on the aggregate and wrong on the EML-levered name.

② Captive-vs-merchant (the real edge)

The scarcity rent accrues only to the merchant pool, because NVIDIA + Broadcom took their supply in-house. That pool is tiny (Sivers + GFS/Ayar/Celestial) and mostly priced. How it's wrong: if monolithic (Aeluma) or VCSEL-NPO (Broadcom) leapfrogs the external laser, the chokepoint dissolves before the merchant pool earns the toll — the early play (ALMU) is literally the bear case.

Rent-capture test (margins settle it): captive (NVDA/AVGO, no merchant toll) → scaled merchant laser+fab (Coherent, GM 39.6% and rising) → merchant pure-play (Sivers, still EBITDA-negative — rent unproven) → SiPh foundry (GFS, durable volume toll). Sivers' negative EBITDA is the tell that the merchant premium isn't captured yet. Confirming tell: Broadcom leads CW-DFB capacity yet is only +14% YTD — its optics are captive and immaterial to a chip giant, so the merchant scarcity never surfaces in its stock. Captive rent ≠ an investable optics play.

How to be early next time 如何提前布局

1 · Watch the architecture shift, not the headline

The ELS chokepoint existed in spec sheets and supply talks 18–24 months before the stocks moved. The next one (monolithic? isolators?) is visible the same way today.

2 · Go one layer below the fabless name

When Sivers ran, the fab that builds its lasers (WIN) and the parts every ELS needs (isolators, PM fiber) stayed cheap. That's the un-bid layer.

3 · Distrust the pure-play after a 17× move

SIVE at ~90× sales with negative EBITDA is a momentum/binary trade, not an early entry. Own the scaled toll-gate (COHR) for compounding, not the re-rating.

4 · The escape route is also the early bet

Monolithic (ALMU) is both the genuinely-early call and the bear case for the whole chokepoint — size it as optionality, not conviction.
