01

Executive snapshot

為什麼是現在

"AI needs cooling" is a consensus trade now — everyone owns Vertiv. But a GB200 NVL72 rack dumps ~2.4 MW of heat, air can't touch it, and that heat has exactly one path out: through a Coolant Distribution Unit (CDU). The CDU is the appliance that circulates clean coolant to the cold plates and hands the heat to the facility's chilled water across a wall of thin metal plates — without ever mixing the two. Look inside it and the scarce, hard-to-qualify parts are two: the brazed-plate heat exchanger (BPHE) that is that wall, and the redundant pump that must never stop (a stopped pump can throttle the processors in seconds — a cited worst-case is ~23s, workload-dependent). This report goes one layer beneath "buy a CDU maker" and asks: who owns the scarce core, and is any of it still early? The uncomfortable answer: the market already crowned the purest core play — Taiwan BPHE maker Kaori is up ~450% in a year — so the residual edge is the cheaper way in: the same component buried inside a diversified industrial (Alfa Laval, +18% YTD at ~17× EV/EBITDA) re-rated far less — the cheaper way to own the identical core (its BPHE sits inside Boyd's 2.3 MW CDU). Proven thesis, relative-value expression.

What this run delivers · 先講結論: the CDU is a genuine severity-15 systemic chokepoint, but it is not a monopoly (40+ NVIDIA cooling partners; several qualified BPHE and pump vendors) and the market has already priced the cleanest core play (Kaori +450%). So the honest output is a relative-value one: of the three listed links, Alfa Laval is the cheapest, highest-quality, least-run way to own the same BPHE core (+18% at ~17× EV/EBITDA; its BPHE is the core of Boyd's 2.3 MW CDU); Moog owns the sealless-pump link (already +69%); Vertiv owns the durable box rent (fully priced at ~51×). The genuinely-early dollar is the private pump layer + the laggard proxy (Nidec) + the leak-detection/filtration razor. This is a "the bottleneck already paid — own the core cheaper, or watch for the next one" run, not a fresh-discovery buy.

EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY · 白話版

An AI rack is basically a stack of chips so hot that a fan can't save them — you must run liquid right up against the silicon. But you can't pump the building's ordinary cooling water through million-dollar GPUs (it's dirty, the wrong pressure, and a leak would be catastrophic). So each rack gets its own sealed loop of clean coolant, and a Coolant Distribution Unit (CDU) — think of it as the rack's dedicated "heart and kidney" — pushes that clean coolant around and dumps the heat into the building's water through a metal wall, so the two liquids never touch. Two parts inside the CDU are genuinely hard to make: the heat-exchanger (that metal wall, actually a stack of wafer-thin brazed plates) and the pump (which, if it ever stops, can cook the chips in seconds — a commonly-cited worst-case is about 23s — so it has to be built to aircraft-grade reliability with a backup). Here's the catch for an investor: the one small company that only makes that heat-exchanger core, Taiwan's Kaori, has already gone up about five-fold in a year — the crowd found it. The same part made by a big diversified company (Sweden's Alfa Laval) is up far less (about +18%), because it's a tiny slice of a giant — which makes it the cheaper way to own the same core. And the two other pure-play CDU makers (CoolIT, Motivair) were just bought by bigger companies (Ecolab and Schneider). Real bottleneck; the cleanest small pure-play already ran five-fold or disappeared into a conglomerate — so the sensible move is to own the core through the cheaper, quality names.

6-TERM GLOSSARY

CDU coolant distribution unit — pumps + isolates a rack/row's clean secondary loop from the facility's primary loop · BPHE brazed-plate heat exchanger — a copper/stainless stack of thin plates that transfers heat between the two loops without mixing them (controls ΔT to ~2–3°C) · L2L / L2A liquid-to-liquid (hands heat to building water) vs liquid-to-air (rejects to room air) CDU · secondary loop the clean, sealed coolant circuit between the CDU and the cold plates · approach temperature how close the secondary-loop temp gets to the facility water — smaller = a better/bigger heat exchanger · N+1 / 2N redundancy schemes so a pump can fail without throttling the rack.

03

Inside the box — the CDU component gallery

卡點的物理

Draw the CDU open and there are five functional blocks. Two are commoditizing (controls, filtration), one is a Run-#10 topic (the quick-disconnect), and two are the scarce, hard-to-qualify core: the brazed-plate heat exchanger and the redundant pump. Those two are where the rent — and the reliability risk — concentrate.

Brazed-plate heat exchanger CHOKE

The metal wall between the two loops — a vacuum-brazed stack of pressed stainless/copper plates. More plates, tighter approach temp, higher heat density per litre. Qualification (leak-tight under pressure cycling, fouling, 5-yr life) is the moat.
Who makes it: Kaori (8996) · Alfa Laval · Danfoss · SWEP · Kelvion · (China) Envicool

Redundant pump CHOKE

Moves the coolant against the loop's pressure drop. The single-point-of-failure link: a stopped pump can throttle the GPUs in seconds (cited worst-case ~23s, workload-dependent), so CDUs run duty/standby (N+1) or 2N with ≤100 ms failover and ≤5% flow wobble. Reliability > raw capacity.
Who makes it: Grundfos · Wilo · Xylem · Nidec — mostly private/diluted

Manifold + quick-disconnect SECONDARY

Distributes coolant from the CDU to each cold plate; the dripless blind-mate couplings let a tray be pulled hot. Tight, but multi-sourced and covered in Run #10 — a real link, not the binding one.
Who makes it: nVent · Staubli · CPC · Danfoss · Parker

Controls + leak detection COMMODITIZING

The brains: variable-speed pump control, redundancy logic, and leak detection (the safety veto that can shut a rack). Rising in importance for reliability, but software/sensors are broadly sourced — value accrues to the integrator, not a scarce part.
Who makes it: CDU integrators in-house · Schneider · sensor OEMs

Filtration + fluid chemistry SERVICES ANNUITY

Keeps the loop clean so the BPHE and cold-plate microchannels don't foul; the coolant (Run #16) plus filters is a recurring service annuity — Ecolab's water-treatment logic for buying CoolIT. Sticky, but not the scarce hardware.
Who makes it: Ecolab (CoolIT) · Chemours · fluid/filter OEMs

Integration + NVIDIA co-qual RENT POOL

Assembling the parts into a reference-qualified, warrantied 1–2.5 MW box is where the near-term rent sits — but it is fragmenting (40+ NVIDIA partners; OCP open designs), so the box premium erodes toward the scarce core over time.
Who wins: Vertiv · Delta · Boyd · CoolIT/Ecolab · Motivair/Schneider
04

From parts to CDU to rack loop (L3 → L2 → L1)

組裝視圖

How the scarce components build up into the finished cooling system, and what changes at each step. The value doesn't compound evenly: it concentrates at the co-qualified CDU box (near-term rent) and at the scarce BPHE + pump core (durable scarcity) — and thins at the ends.

The BPHE + pump "core" stays scarce all the way up the stack (it's the physics), whereas the CDU box premium depends on qualification and warranty — which NVIDIA's reference designs and OCP open-sourcing are steadily commoditizing. That is the whole disaggregation of this report: durable rent = the core; eroding rent = the box.

05

Which CDU serves which density — deployment map

部署對應

Not all CDUs are equal. As rack density climbs from ~30 kW to 130 kW to Rubin's 250–600 kW, the CDU moves from a small in-rack unit to a row-level sidecar to a facility-scale machine — and the BPHE/pump core scales with it. This is where the "L2L is the tight, valuable slice" claim comes from.

Capacity points verified: CoolIT CHx2000 = 2 MW L2L at 5°C approach (GB300 NVL72); Boyd's in-row CDU with a custom Alfa Laval BPHE at its core = 2.3 MW; Motivair (Schneider) MCDU-70 = 2.5 MW, portfolio to 10 MW+; DCX facility CDU = 8.15 MW at 45°C warm water. The through-line: whatever the form factor, demand routes back to the BPHE + reliable-pump core.

08

The chokepoint, scored — and how it could be wrong

卡點評分

The CDU heat-exchange + pump core — the appliance gate on every liquid-cooled rack

Every liquid-cooled AI rack needs a CDU; the CDU cannot skip its BPHE (the two loops must not mix) or its pump (a stop can throttle the rack in seconds); both are qualified over 12–18 months and sourced from a short list of vendors. That is a genuine severity-15 systemic chokepoint. But it is honestly softer than a monopoly: NVIDIA lists 40+ cooling partners and open-sources reference CDU designs, and the BPHE/pump each have several qualified suppliers (Kaori, Alfa Laval, Danfoss, SWEP, Kelvion; Grundfos, Wilo, Xylem). So the tightness is real but multi-sourced — which is why severity is 15, not the 18–19 of the glass-cloth/GOES monopolies — and, critically, the market already priced the cleanest expression (Kaori +450%).

VARIANT / DISAGGREGATION — the edge, and the trap inside it

Consensus says "AI cooling = buy Vertiv" or "buy a CDU maker." The edge is a three-part disaggregation: (1) the CDU box is fragmenting — 40+ NVIDIA partners plus OCP open reference designs — so the integrator's premium erodes toward the scarce BPHE + pump core one layer down; (2) the market has already found the purest core play (Kaori +450%/12m), so the component thesis is proven but priced — the residual edge is the cheaper, quality proxies (Alfa Laval +18% at ~17× EV/EBITDA vs Kaori's re-rate; the sealless-pump link Moog; the laggard Nidec) and the private reliable-pump layer; (3) the "absorbed pure-play" tell — CoolIT→Ecolab ($4.75B, 29× fwd EBITDA) and Motivair→Schneider ($850M, 75% stake, closed Feb-2025) — validates the chokepoint and removes the clean vehicles, which is itself the signal.

RENT-CAPTURE TEST — who keeps the scarcity rent?

Margins settle the argument. Kaori (BPHE, tiny base) ran +450% with margin expansion — the component captured huge, durable rent, and the market found it. Vertiv (the system) captured strong rent too (backlog +109%, margins expanding) but faces reference-design commoditization, so its box rent is durable-for-now but eroding. The pump makers (Grundfos, Wilo — private) capture reliability rent invisibly. Verdict: rent is shared between the co-qualified integrator (near-term, eroding) and the scarce core (durable) — but the pure-component rent has already been paid out in Kaori's tape. That is the defining difference from GOES/glass-cloth, where the material stayed cheap: here the material pure-play won, which is bullish confirmation of the framework and bearish for a late entry into the obvious name.
